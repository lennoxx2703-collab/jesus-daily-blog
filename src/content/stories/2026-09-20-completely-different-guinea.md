On a beach in Conakry, Guinea, two men who did not plan to meet found Scripture between them.

Rees Morgan was weary. He had come to Conakry as a Project 3000 explorer with the International Mission Board, learning French and seeking unreached people. One day he walked the shore looking for a break. He noticed a young man holding a Bible. He tried his new language. The young man — Vennico — smiled and said he did not speak French. They laughed. Vennico said he was a Christian. Morgan said that was great, then went to hang his hammock and open his own Bible.

A few minutes later he looked up. Vennico was watching with quiet curiosity.

“Do you want to read the Bible with me?” Morgan asked.

“Yes, I would love that!” Vennico answered.

That invitation became friendship. Over the next months Morgan welcomed Vennico into a modest apartment for deep dives into Scripture. Vennico had attended a Catholic school and begun reading the Bible there, but the message had stayed abstract. With Morgan, the gospel became clear. It transformed his life. He professed faith in Christ and was baptized.

Vennico later told another IMB missionary, Tristan Liam, how he had once lived a reckless, “hedonistic” life — and how faith in Christ changed everything. “I am completely different!” he exclaimed. “Everything about me has changed!” When Liam asked him about prayer after hearing of mockery toward believers, Vennico answered with a clarity that still lands: he looks to his right and to his left and knows he is not perfect; his prayers are not perfect — “But, Christ! He is perfect.”

When Morgan’s term ended and he returned to the United States, he did not leave Vennico alone. He introduced him to Liam. Now, every Thursday, Liam navigates Conakry’s busy streets, picks Vennico up, and brings him home for rice, beans, fish, and hours in the Word — informal talk about family and culture, and the hard honesty of living as a new believer in a predominantly Muslim environment. They study Scripture, church history, the Reformation, and mission. Vennico writes sermons verse by verse, leads a neighborhood Bible study on Monday nights, and keeps inviting young men the way he and Morgan once did with a spike-ball outreach that grew into weekly Bible study.

His family story is still unfolding. About thirty percent of his relatives talk with him about his faith; the rest still oppose him. He names it slow progress — and keeps going. He dreams of planting a church on the unreached border between Guinea and Sierra Leone, a vision he shares with both Morgan and Liam.

What began as a walk on the beach — a missionary needing rest, a young man holding a Bible — became conversion, baptism, and a long discipleship road. Jesus did not wait for a perfect setting. He used sand, a hammock, and an open book. He still remakes people so thoroughly they can say, without pretending, *I am completely different*.

If your own life feels reckless, unfinished, or half-understood — or if you hold a Bible you do not yet fully grasp — Vennico’s story is not a demand that you copy Guinea’s shore. It is a witness that Christ is perfect when we are not, and that He still turns hearts toward God through ordinary friendship and the Word made clear. Ask Him. Open a Gospel. Stay with people who will read with you. And if He has already remade you, you are welcome to [share your story](../share/).
