It was about the sixth hour — noon — when the sun stood high and the road lay quiet. Jesus, weary from the journey, sat down by Jacob’s well near a town of Samaria called Sychar. His disciples had gone into the city to buy food. He waited alone beside the ancient stone that had given water for generations.

A woman of Samaria came to draw water.

We are not told her name. We are told enough. She came at an hour when many women preferred the cooler morning or evening. She came carrying a jar and whatever story she carried inside. Scripture simply lets us watch what happened when Jesus met her there.

Jesus said to her, “Give me a drink.” (John 4:7, WEB)

She was startled — not only by the request, but by the Speaker. “How is it that you, being a Jew, ask for a drink from me, a Samaritan woman?” (John 4:9, WEB). Jews and Samaritans had long avoided one another. Mercy often begins by crossing lines we thought were fixed.

Jesus answered her, “If you knew the gift of God, and who it is who says to you, ‘Give me a drink,’ you would have asked him, and he would have given you living water.” (John 4:10, WEB)

She looked at the well — deep, familiar, practical — and at Him, a traveler without a bucket. “Sir, you have nothing to draw with, and the well is deep. So where do you get that living water? Are you greater than our father Jacob, who gave us the well…?” (John 4:11–12, WEB)

He did not scold her questions. He answered thirst with truth.

“Everyone who drinks of this water will thirst again, but whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never thirst again; but the water that I will give him will become in him a well of water springing up to eternal life.” (John 4:13–14, WEB)

Something in her reached for it. “Sir, give me this water, so that I don’t get thirsty, neither come all the way here to draw.” (John 4:15, WEB) He would lead her deeper — not by force, but by light.

“Go, call your husband, and come here.” (John 4:16, WEB)

“I have no husband,” she answered. (John 4:17, WEB)

Jesus said to her, “You said well, ‘I have no husband,’ for you have had five husbands; and he whom you now have is not your husband. This you have said truly.” (John 4:17–18, WEB)

He knew. He did not pretend not to know. And still He stayed at the well. He named what was true so that grace could meet her as she was — not as she wished to appear.

She shifted toward safer ground: religion, mountains, the right place to worship. “Our fathers worshiped in this mountain, and you Jews say that in Jerusalem is the place where people ought to worship.” (John 4:20, WEB)

Jesus answered, “Woman, believe me, the hour is coming when neither in this mountain nor in Jerusalem will you worship the Father… But the hour comes, and now is, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth, for the Father seeks such to be his worshipers. God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.” (John 4:21–24, WEB)

The Father seeks. Not only is sought. He seeks worshipers who come in spirit and truth — not performing on a mountain of image, but opening the heart where they stand.

She said, “I know that Messiah is coming, he who is called Christ. When he has come, he will declare to us all things.” (John 4:25, WEB)

Jesus said to her, “I am he, the one who speaks to you.” (John 4:26, WEB)

Here, at a well in Samaria, to a woman with a complicated past and an honest thirst, Jesus unveiled Himself with quiet clarity. “I am he.”

Then His disciples returned, and marveled that He was speaking with a woman — yet no one asked Him why. The woman left her water jar, went away into the city, and said to the people, “Come, see a man who told me everything that I have done. Can this be the Christ?” (John 4:28–29, WEB)

She left the jar. The errand that brought her could wait. Encounter had rearranged her priorities. And the same mouth that had come for water now invited a town: *Come, see.*

Many of the Samaritans believed because of the word of the woman. They came to Him and asked Him to stay. He stayed two days. And many more believed because of His word. They said to the woman, “Now we believe, not because of your speaking; for we have heard for ourselves, and know that this is indeed the Christ, the Savior of the world.” (John 4:39–42, WEB)

That is the shape of a Gospel encounter: Jesus meets someone where they are, speaks living water into ordinary thirst, tells the truth without cruelty, reveals Himself, and sends the one who has been found to say, *Come and see* — until others hear for themselves.

If you come carrying your own jar — questions, shame, a long road, a faith that feels half-formed — you are not outside the story. The same Lord who sat weary by Jacob’s well still draws near. He still knows. He still offers water that does not run dry.

You do not need a perfect past to meet Him. You need only to come as you are, and ask for the gift.

Come to the well. Ask Him for living water. Stay long enough to hear Him say who He is — and then, when you are ready, go and tell someone: *Come, see.*
