Evening is when a lot of storms seem to arrive. The house gets quiet, the distractions run out, and whatever you held together all day starts pressing in. A worry about money. A conversation that went wrong. A test result you're still waiting on. It can feel like the water is rising and nobody notices.

Mark tells us that the disciples' worst night on the lake began at exactly this hour.

> "On that day, when evening had come, he said to them, 'Let's go over to the other side.'" (Mark 4:35)

It was Jesus' idea to cross. They weren't out there because they had wandered off or done something wrong. They were in the boat because He had asked them to get in. That matters, because sometimes we assume a storm means we missed God somewhere. Here, the storm came while they were doing exactly what He said.

## Don't you care?

> "A big wind storm arose, and the waves beat into the boat, so much that the boat was already filled. He himself was in the stern, asleep on the cushion, and they woke him up, and told him, 'Teacher, don't you care that we are dying?'" (Mark 4:37–38)

Some of these men were fishermen. They knew this lake. If they were scared, it was bad. And there was Jesus, asleep on a cushion.

Their question is so honest it almost hurts to read: *don't you care?* Maybe you've prayed something like it, even if you didn't use those words. *Where are You? Why does it feel like You're sleeping through this?*

Notice that Jesus doesn't scold them for waking Him. He doesn't send them away for asking the wrong way. They brought their fear straight to Him, raw and unpolished, and He got up.

## A word to the wind

> "He awoke, and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, 'Peace! Be still!' The wind ceased, and there was a great calm." (Mark 4:39)

No ritual and no struggle. Just a word. The same voice that spoke light into being at the beginning speaks to the wind, and the wind listens. The sea went from chaos to *a great calm*, not a slow fade but a sudden hush.

Then He asks them a question: "Why are you so afraid? How is it that you have no faith?" (Mark 4:40). It's a gentle challenge, not a rejection. He's inviting them to see something they had missed. The safest place on that lake was never the shore. It was the boat Jesus was in.

The disciples end the night more amazed than relieved: "Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?" (Mark 4:41). That's the real gift of the storm. They came out of it knowing Him better.

## Tonight, in your boat

Jesus doesn't promise that every storm will stop the moment we ask. Sometimes He calms the sea, and sometimes He calms us in the middle of it. But this story tells us something we can lean on in either case: He is in the boat. He isn't on the shore watching to see if you make it. He is close enough to wake.

So before you sleep, go ahead and wake Him. Tell Him what's flooding in. You don't need careful words. "Lord, don't You care?" is a prayer He has already answered, by getting up, by speaking peace, and in the end by going to the cross for you.

He told them, "Let's go over to the other side." He meant to get them there, and He did. He means to get you there too.

*Lord Jesus, You are here in my boat tonight. I bring You the wind and the waves I can't control. Speak Your peace over my heart and over this night. Help me trust that You care, and that You will bring me to the other side. Amen.*
