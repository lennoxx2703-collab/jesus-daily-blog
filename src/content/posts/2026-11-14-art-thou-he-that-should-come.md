Saturday evening can be a strange kind of quiet. The week is behind you, the busy part of the weekend is mostly done, and in the stillness the questions you kept at arm's length all week tend to walk back in. *Is any of this real? Is He really there? Why hasn't anything changed?*

If that's where you are tonight, you're in better company than you might think.

> "Now when John had heard in the prison the works of Christ, he sent two of his disciples, and said unto him, Art thou he that should come, or do we look for another?"
> — Matthew 11:2–3 (KJV)

This is John the Baptist. The man who leapt in his mother's womb. The one who stood in the Jordan, saw Jesus coming, and cried out, "Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world" (John 1:29). If anyone was sure, it was John.

And now he's in Herod's prison, in the dark, and the certainty has worn thin. He had preached about an axe at the root of the trees and a winnowing fan. What he heard from his cell sounded gentler and slower than he expected. So he sends the most honest question a believer can ask: *Are You really the one?*

## Jesus doesn't scold him

Notice what Jesus does not say. He doesn't send back, "How could you doubt after everything you've seen?" He doesn't tell John's disciples their teacher has failed.

> "Jesus answered and said unto them, Go and shew John again those things which ye do hear and see: The blind receive their sight, and the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, and the deaf hear, and the dead are raised up, and the poor have the gospel preached to them."
> — Matthew 11:4–5 (KJV)

He answers with evidence, and He answers in John's own language. Those words echo Isaiah, the prophet John knew by heart: "Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped" (Isaiah 35:5). Jesus is saying, in effect, *Look again at what God promised, and look at what is happening. You weren't wrong about Me.*

And then, the moment John's messengers leave, Jesus turns to the crowd and honors him: "Among them that are born of women there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist" (Matthew 11:11). The doubt didn't shrink John in Jesus' eyes. Not for a second.

## The blessing for when it doesn't make sense

There's one more line, and it's the tender one:

> "And blessed is he, whosoever shall not be offended in me."
> — Matthew 11:6 (KJV)

*Offended* here means tripped up, made to stumble. Jesus knew His way of working would trip people up. He heals and feeds and forgives, but He doesn't always open the prison door. John was not released. That's hard, and the Bible doesn't hide it.

So Jesus offers a blessing for exactly that place: for the one who keeps trusting Him while the story isn't turning out the way they pictured it. That isn't a demand to feel certain. It's an invitation to stay near Him with your questions still in hand.

## Bringing your question to Him

The most important thing John did in that cell wasn't having the doubt. It was where he sent it. He didn't send his question away from Jesus; he sent it *to* Jesus.

You can do the same tonight. You don't need to tidy it up first. A father once said to Him, "Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief" (Mark 9:24), and Jesus healed his son anyway. Honest is enough.

Then "go and shew again" — look again at what He has done. In the Gospels: the blind seeing, the lepers touched, the dead raised, the poor hearing good news. And in your own life: the times you were carried when you didn't know it, the people He sent, the mercy you didn't earn.

Faith that has asked hard questions isn't weaker faith. Sometimes it's the kind that has finally become your own.

## A prayer for this evening

*Lord Jesus, some nights I'm not sure. Thank You that John could ask You plainly and You answered him kindly. I bring You my questions instead of hiding them. Show me again what You have done, and help me not to stumble when Your ways are not what I expected. Keep me near You through this night. Amen.*
