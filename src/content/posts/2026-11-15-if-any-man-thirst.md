Sunday evening has a particular feeling to it. The weekend is winding down. Whatever you hoped would fill you up — the rest, the time with people, the church service, the long walk, the quiet — has come and gone. And sometimes, if you're honest, you're still thirsty. Monday is waiting on the other side of the night, and something in you feels dry.

If that's you tonight, there's a moment in John's Gospel that was made for exactly this hour.

## The last day of the feast

> "In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink. He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water." — John 7:37–38 (KJV)

This was the Feast of Tabernacles — a week-long celebration in Jerusalem, crowded and joyful, full of songs, lights, and ceremonies remembering how God had carried His people through the wilderness and given them water from the rock. And now it was the last day. The biggest day. The moment when the celebration peaks — and then everyone packs up and goes home.

That is when Jesus stood up. Not at the beginning, when everyone was fresh and expectant, but at the end, when the festival had done everything a festival can do. And into that moment He *cried out*. John doesn't say He mentioned it or suggested it. He raised His voice so the thirsty could hear Him over the crowd.

## He calls the thirsty, not the qualified

Notice who He invites: "If any man thirst." Not if any man is ready. Not if any man has his life in order, or prayed enough this week, or feels spiritual tonight. The only qualification is thirst — and thirst is simply the honest awareness that you need something you don't have.

That's good news for a dry Sunday evening. The emptiness you feel is not a sign that you're disqualified. It's the very thing He's calling out to. Centuries before, Isaiah heard the same heartbeat in God's voice: "Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath no money" (Isaiah 55:1). No money, no merit, no performance. Just come.

And notice what He tells them to do: "come unto me, and drink." Not come to the feast, not come to the ceremony, not even come to a better routine — come to *Me*. The weekend can't satisfy that thirst. Neither can a better week. The water is a Person.

## Rivers, not just a sip

Then Jesus promises something more than relief. Whoever believes in Him won't just stop being thirsty — "out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water." John explains right after that He was speaking of the Spirit, whom those who believe on Him would receive (John 7:39).

That means the life Jesus gives doesn't sit in you like water in a jar that slowly runs low by Sunday night. It flows. It keeps coming from a source that isn't you. You don't have to manufacture your own spiritual energy for Monday morning. You can come back to Him, again and again, and drink — and somehow, quietly, what He gives you begins to spill over onto the people around you.

## Before you sleep

Maybe tonight you don't need a big plan for the week. Maybe you just need to do what He said: come, and drink.

The psalmist prayed, "As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth my soul after thee, O God" (Psalm 42:1). You can pray that too, even if all you can bring is the panting. Tell Him where you feel dry. Tell Him what didn't fill you this weekend. Then let Him be enough for tonight. The last words of the Bible still hold the same invitation: "whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely" (Revelation 22:17).

## A prayer for this evening

*Lord Jesus, the weekend is ending and I'm still thirsty. Thank You that You call out to people like me — not the ones who have it together, but the ones who are dry. I come to You tonight, not to a feeling or a routine, but to You. Fill me with Your Spirit. Let Your living water flow in me through this coming week, and let it reach the people I'll meet tomorrow. Give me rest tonight in You. Amen.*
