Some evenings the hardest thing isn't sadness. It's hope. You've heard good news — a prayer answered, a door opened, a word that God hasn't given up on you — and something in you refuses to lean on it. *It can't be that simple. It's too good. I'll get my heart up and it'll fall again.*

If that's where you are tonight, the disciples were there first.

## A locked room and a sudden voice

It was the evening of the first Easter. Rumors had been flying all day: the tomb was empty, women had seen angels, two friends had come running back from Emmaus saying they'd eaten with Him. The disciples were huddled together, talking it all over, when this happened:

> "And as they thus spake, Jesus himself stood in the midst of them, and saith unto them, Peace be unto you. But they were terrified and affrighted, and supposed that they had seen a spirit."
> — Luke 24:36–37 (KJV)

Notice His first words. Not *Where were you?* Not *Why did you run?* Just: *Peace be unto you.* The last time most of them had seen Him, they were scattering into the dark of Gethsemane. He comes back, and the first thing He offers is peace.

And still they're terrified. Peace was standing right in front of them, and fear got there first. That's honest. That's us.

## "Handle me, and see"

Jesus doesn't scold their fear. He answers it:

> "Why are ye troubled? and why do thoughts arise in your hearts? Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have."
> — Luke 24:38–39 (KJV)

*Why do thoughts arise in your hearts?* He knows about the spiral, the second-guessing, the late-night arguments in your head. And His answer isn't a lecture. It's His hands. The scars are still there. The risen Lord didn't come back polished and untouched; He came back marked by what He went through for them.

That matters at the end of a long day. The One you're praying to tonight is not a distant idea. He is *"Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever"* (Hebrews 13:8, KJV) — the same Jesus, with the same wounded hands, inviting you to come close enough to see that it's really Him.

## Too good to be true

Then comes one of my favorite lines in the Gospels:

> "And while they yet believed not for joy, and wondered, he said unto them, Have ye here any meat?"
> — Luke 24:41 (KJV)

*They believed not for joy.* It wasn't stubbornness. It was that the news was so good they couldn't let themselves have it yet. Joy and disbelief were mixed together in the same hearts, in the same moment.

If your faith tonight is a tangle like that — part wonder, part *I don't dare* — you are not disqualified. Jesus stood right there in the middle of that tangle. He didn't wait for them to get their belief sorted out before staying.

## He asked for supper

And what did He do next? Something wonderfully ordinary:

> "And they gave him a piece of a broiled fish, and of an honeycomb. And he took it, and did eat before them."
> — Luke 24:42–43 (KJV)

The resurrection, the victory over death, the turning point of all history — and He sits down and eats leftover fish. Not to prove a theological point in some cold way, but because that's how friends show they're really there. He made Himself at home in their evening.

That's still how He tends to come. Not always in thunder, but at a kitchen table, in a quiet room, in the middle of a conversation you were having about whether any of it is true.

## For tonight

You don't have to force your heart to feel certain before you go to sleep. Bring Him the mix: the fear, the hope you're scared to trust, the joy you can't quite hold yet. Let His first words to you be the same as theirs: *Peace be unto you.*

Maybe pray something simple:

*Lord Jesus, You know the thoughts that arise in my heart. Some of me believes, and some of me is afraid it's too good. Stand in the middle of my evening anyway. Show me Your hands. Stay for supper. Amen.*

He's not a ghost, and He's not a rumor. He's risen, He's near, and He's not in a hurry to leave.
