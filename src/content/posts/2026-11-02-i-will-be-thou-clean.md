It's lunchtime on a Monday, and for some of us the week already has a stain on it. A sharp word at breakfast. A thing you promised yourself you wouldn't do again, done again. An old habit, an old fear, an old shame that followed you right into the new month like it had a key to the door.

You probably don't doubt that God *can* help. You've heard that your whole life. The quieter question is whether He *wants* to. Whether He's tired of you. Whether you've used up your turn.

Mark tells about a man who carried exactly that question.

> "And there came a leper to him, beseeching him, and kneeling down to him, and saying unto him, If thou wilt, thou canst make me clean. And Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand, and touched him, and saith unto him, I will; be thou clean. And as soon as he had spoken, immediately the leprosy departed from him, and he was cleansed."
> — Mark 1:40–42 (KJV)

## "Thou canst"

Notice what the man is sure of. He doesn't say, "If you're able." He's heard enough about Jesus to know power isn't the problem. *Thou canst.* That part is settled.

Leprosy in his world meant more than a sick body. It meant living outside the town, calling out "unclean" so people would keep their distance, going years without anyone's hand on his shoulder. A man like that learns to expect people to step back. So when he finally kneels in front of Jesus, his doubt isn't about Jesus' strength. It's about Jesus' heart.

*If thou wilt.* If You want to. If I'm worth the trouble.

That might be the most honest prayer in the Gospels, and it might be yours today.

## He touched him

Jesus could have healed him from across the road. He did that for others with a word. But here Mark slows down: He *put forth his hand, and touched him.*

Before the man was clean, while he was still the person everyone avoided, Jesus touched him. The cleansing didn't come first and the touch afterwards, as a reward. The touch came while he was still unclean.

That's how Jesus comes toward you on a Monday you've already stained. He doesn't wait for you to tidy up before He reaches out. He isn't holding His hand back until you've earned it. His hand is already moving.

## "I will"

And then the two words the man had been afraid to hope for: *I will.*

Not "maybe." Not "we'll see how you do this week." Not "again?" Jesus answers the exact question the man asked, and He answers it with His whole heart. Mark even tells us what was going on inside Him first: *moved with compassion.* Pity wasn't something He worked up to. It was what moved His hand.

If you ever wonder what Jesus feels toward you when you come back with the same mess one more time, here's your answer. The Bible elsewhere says it plainly too: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9, KJV). Faithful means He keeps doing it. *I will* wasn't a one-time mood. It's who He is.

## For the rest of today

So here's a small thing to do before the afternoon swallows you up. Take whatever has followed you into this week, the stain, the habit, the shame, and kneel with it, even if only inside your own head at your desk. Pray the leper's prayer honestly: *Lord, if You will, You can make me clean.*

Then listen for His answer, because it hasn't changed in two thousand years.

*I will; be thou clean.*

You don't have to carry the morning into the afternoon. He's already reached out. Let Him touch it, and walk on into the rest of Monday clean.
