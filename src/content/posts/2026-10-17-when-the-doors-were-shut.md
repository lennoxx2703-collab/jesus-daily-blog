Saturday evenings can be strangely quiet. The rush of the week is over, but the questions it stirred up are not. Sometimes this is the hour when the worries we kept busy enough to ignore come and sit down beside us.

The disciples knew an evening like that.

> "Then the same day at evening, being the first day of the week, when the doors were shut where the disciples were assembled for fear of the Jews, came Jesus and stood in the midst, and saith unto them, Peace be unto you." (John 20:19, KJV)

## Behind the locked door

It was the evening of the very first Easter. That morning the tomb had been found empty. Mary Magdalene had come running with the words, "I have seen the Lord" (John 20:18, KJV). And yet, by nightfall, the disciples were huddled in a room with the doors shut.

That is so honest. They had heard the good news, and they were still afraid. They had every reason to hope, and they still bolted the door.

Maybe you know that feeling. You believe Jesus is risen. You have sung it and prayed it. And still there are rooms in you that you keep locked: a fear about money, a grief you don't talk about, a failure you'd rather nobody saw. You lock the door because it feels safer than letting anything in.

## He came anyway

Here is the wonder of this evening: the locked door did not keep Jesus out.

He didn't wait outside until they were brave enough to open it. He didn't send a message telling them to pull themselves together first. He "came … and stood in the midst." Right into the middle of their fear. Right into the room they had sealed.

And the first word He spoke was not a rebuke. These were the same men who had run away on Thursday night. Peter had denied Him three times. Jesus had every right to begin with, "Where were you?" Instead He said, "Peace be unto you."

That is how He comes to you tonight too. Not to scold you for being afraid, but to stand in the middle of the fear and speak peace.

## He showed them His wounds

> "And when he had so said, he shewed unto them his hands and his side. Then were the disciples glad, when they saw the Lord." (John 20:20, KJV)

Jesus didn't hide His scars. He showed them. The wounds were the proof that it was really Him, the same Jesus who had gone to the cross, now alive.

There is comfort in that for every wounded heart. The risen Lord still carries the marks of His love for you. He knows pain from the inside. When you bring Him your hurt, you are not bringing it to someone who has never bled. You are bringing it to the One whose hands were pierced for you.

And notice what happened: "Then were the disciples glad." The fear didn't vanish because their situation changed. The authorities were still outside. The doors were still shut. They were glad because they saw the Lord.

## Peace, and then a sending

> "Then said Jesus to them again, Peace be unto you: as my Father hath sent me, even so send I you." (John 20:21, KJV)

He said it twice. Peace, and then peace again. Some fears need to hear it more than once, and Jesus is patient enough to repeat Himself.

But His peace doesn't keep us locked in forever. The same voice that calms the room eventually opens the door. "Even so send I you." Tomorrow is Sunday, the first day of a new week, and He will walk out ahead of you into it.

A week later the disciples were in that room again, "the doors being shut," and Jesus came again and said the same words (John 20:26, KJV). He is not tired of coming back.

## Tonight

Before you sleep, name the locked room in your heart. You don't have to force it open. Just tell Him it's there.

Then picture Him standing in the middle of it, hands open, scars visible, saying the words He has always said to frightened friends: *Peace be unto you.*

Let that be the last word of your Saturday.
