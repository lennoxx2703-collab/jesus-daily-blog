Tuesday doesn't get much attention. It isn't the fresh start of Monday or the relief of Friday. It's just a regular day with regular people in it: the colleague who's having a rough week, the neighbor you nod at, the person in the queue behind you, the family around your kitchen table. If you're wondering whether a day like this matters to Jesus, He answered that a long time ago.

> "Ye are the salt of the earth: but if the salt have lost his savour, wherewith shall it be salted? it is thenceforth good for nothing, but to be cast out, and to be trodden under foot of men." (Matthew 5:13, KJV)

## He says "ye are"

Look closely at who Jesus was talking to. He had just finished the Beatitudes: blessed are the poor in spirit, the mourners, the meek, the hungry, the persecuted. The crowd on that hillside was made up of fishermen, farmers, tired mothers, and people nobody important had ever noticed.

He didn't tell them to *try* to become salt. He didn't hand them a program for improving their saltiness. He said, *Ye are.* It was already true because they were with Him.

That's still how it works. If you belong to Jesus this morning, you don't walk into Tuesday hoping to earn a place in what God is doing. You walk in already carrying something of Him.

## What salt actually does

In Jesus' world salt wasn't decoration. It kept fish from spoiling on the way from Galilee to market. It drew out the flavor of plain bread. It was rubbed into wounds and mixed into offerings. Ordinary people couldn't do without it.

And it works in small amounts. Nobody eats a bowl of salt. A pinch does its quiet work and then disappears into the meal. You notice salt mostly when it's missing.

That's a freeing picture for an ordinary day. You probably won't change the world before lunch. But a pinch of patience in a tense meeting, an honest word where a shortcut would be easier, or a kind question to someone who looks worn out can keep a room from going sour. It can bring out the good in people that was already there. Most of the time nobody will trace it back to you, and that's fine. Salt doesn't sign its work.

Paul put it this way: "Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt, that ye may know how ye ought to answer every man" (Colossians 4:6, KJV). *Seasoned* is the right word. It doesn't mean preachy or sharp. It means gracious words that make the conversation better.

## When the savour fades

Jesus is honest that salt can lose its savour. In His day, salt mixed with too much sand and dust could go flat, so it looked like salt and did nothing.

Most of us know that feeling. We can get so tired, distracted, or hurried that our faith turns into just the outward look, with nothing left of the taste. The answer isn't to try harder at being salty. In Mark's Gospel Jesus says, "Have salt in yourselves, and have peace one with another" (Mark 9:50, KJV). The saltiness comes from staying close to Him. You go back to the source, and He gives back what the week has worn away.

## For this Tuesday

Before you head out, think of two or three places where you'll be today: a desk, a classroom, a hospital corridor, a group chat, a dinner table. Then ask Jesus, simply, where He wants a little salt to fall.

You might pray: *Lord Jesus, You called ordinary people salt, and You call me that too. Keep me close enough to You that I don't lose my savour. Season my words with grace today. Let me make some small place a little better, and let it point back to You, not to me.*

## A blessing for the day

May your words today be seasoned with grace. May some small kindness of yours keep a hard moment from turning bitter. And may the people you meet go home a little more hopeful, without ever quite knowing why, because Jesus was there in you.
