By the middle of the day, most of us have already met the people we find hardest to love. The coworker who talks over everyone. The family member who sent that message. The stranger in line who was short with you for no reason. The person in the mirror, too, some days.

It is easy to love people in theory. It is much harder at 1:30 on a Tuesday, when they are actually in front of you.

Jesus knew that. And on the last night before the cross, sitting at the table with the men who would soon run away, He gave them one thing to carry out of that room.

## A new commandment

> "A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you, that ye also love one another. By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another." — John 13:34–35 (KJV)

Look at when He said it. Judas had just gone out into the night. Peter was about to promise loyalty he could not keep. The others would scatter within hours. Jesus called them "little children" a moment earlier, and He was not being sentimental. He knew exactly who they were.

And still He said: love one another. Not "love the ones who are easy." Not "love them once they deserve it." Love one another, these same flawed, frightened, arguing friends.

## What makes it new

The command to love your neighbor was already old. It was written in the law long before Jesus came. What was new was the measure: *as I have loved you.*

That changes the question. It is no longer "How much does this person deserve?" It becomes "How has Jesus loved me?"

He loved them patiently, through three years of slow learning and wrong answers. He loved them honestly, telling them hard truths without walking away. He loved them practically, feeding them, washing their feet, cooking breakfast on the shore after they failed Him. And He loved them all the way, to the cross and beyond it.

That is the love He asks us to pass on. Not a feeling we have to manufacture, but a love we have first received.

> "We love him, because he first loved us." — 1 John 4:19 (KJV)

You cannot give what you have never been given. So the first step in loving the difficult person in front of you is not gritting your teeth. It is remembering how patiently Jesus has loved you, on your worst days, in your slowest seasons, when you said you'd follow and then didn't.

## How the world will know

Notice what Jesus says will mark His followers. Not our arguments. Not our knowledge. Not how busy we are for Him. "By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another."

People around you may never open a Bible. But they will notice whether you are patient when you are interrupted, whether you speak kindly about someone who isn't in the room, whether you forgive the small thing instead of keeping score. Love is the sign Jesus chose. It is quiet, and it is very hard to fake.

Paul described what that love looks like in ordinary life:

> "Charity suffereth long, and is kind; charity envieth not; charity vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up." — 1 Corinthians 13:4 (KJV)

Long-suffering and kind. That is a good description of an afternoon lived with Jesus.

## For the rest of today

Think of one person you will see or talk to before the day ends, the one who is hardest to love right now. You don't have to fix everything between you. Just ask Jesus for one small act of love toward them: a patient answer, a kind word, a prayer for them in secret, a choice not to repeat the complaint.

You might pray something like this:

*Lord Jesus, You loved Your friends at the table knowing everything they would do. You have loved me the same way. Fill me with that love this afternoon, so I can give some of it away. Help me love the people in front of me as You have loved me. Amen.*

The commandment is new every day, because every day He loves you first.
