Tuesday morning often arrives already tired. Monday took more than it gave. The inbox is still full. The conversation you dreaded either happened or is still waiting. Your body woke up, but your heart may still be catching its breath.

If that is you, Jesus has a word that does not ask you to pretend otherwise.

> "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." (Matthew 11:28, KJV)

## An invitation, not a checklist

Notice who He calls. Not the already-sorted. Not the people who slept well and woke with a perfect plan. He calls the ones who *labour* — who have been working hard and still feel behind — and the ones who are *heavy laden* — carrying a weight they did not choose and cannot put down alone.

That might be a deadline. It might be a diagnosis. It might be a quiet shame, a lonely marriage, a child you worry about, or the long fatigue of simply trying to be faithful when nobody is watching. Jesus does not sort the piles first. He simply says, *Come unto me.*

He does not say, *Come when you have fixed yourself.* He does not say, *Come when you feel spiritual again.* He says come *now*, with the load still on your shoulders.

## A different kind of rest

The rest Jesus offers is not a nap and a clean slate on the calendar — though He cares about your body too. It is something deeper: the relief of no longer carrying life alone. The relief of knowing the One who holds the universe is willing to hold you.

He continues:

> "Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light." (Matthew 11:29–30, KJV)

A yoke joins two together for the work. Jesus is not inviting you to drop every responsibility and float. He is inviting you to walk the day *with Him* — side by side, learning His pace, His gentleness, His lowly heart. The difference is not that the path disappears. The difference is who shares it.

His yoke is easy not because the world suddenly becomes soft, but because He is kind. His burden is light not because nothing hard will happen on a Tuesday, but because He carries what you cannot.

## How to come this morning

Coming to Jesus does not require a polished prayer. It can be as simple as sitting still for one quiet minute and telling Him the truth: *Lord, I am tired. I am carrying this. I want to walk with You today instead of alone.*

Open the Gospel. Read His words slowly. Ask Him to teach you His heart — meek, lowly, near. Then step into the first ordinary thing on your list with Him: the commute, the sink full of dishes, the meeting, the school run. Rest is not only found in silence. Rest is found in His presence while you move.

If you feel too weary even to pray well, remember: the invitation still stands. He spoke it first. Your job is not to manufacture rest. Your job is to come.

## A quiet Tuesday blessing

May this Tuesday morning find you answering His call. May the weight you woke with meet the Savior who already knew its shape. And may you discover, somewhere between the first cup of coffee and the last task of the morning, that His yoke really is easier than the one you have been dragging alone.

Come unto Him. He will give you rest.
