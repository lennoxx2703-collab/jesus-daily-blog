By Thursday evening most of us are running low. The week has taken its share, and the natural thing is to pull inward: get home, close the door, let someone else carry the world for a few hours. When we're tired or hurting, our circle shrinks to the size of our own needs.

John tells us about the hardest evening in history, and about a moment when Jesus did the opposite.

> "Now there stood by the cross of Jesus his mother, and his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Cleophas, and Mary Magdalene. When Jesus therefore saw his mother, and the disciple standing by, whom he loved, he saith unto his mother, Woman, behold thy son! Then saith he to the disciple, Behold thy mother! And from that hour that disciple took her unto his own home." (John 19:25–27, KJV)

## He saw them

Think about where Jesus is. Nailed to wood, every breath a fight, carrying the weight of the world's sin. If anyone ever had the right to think only of His own pain, it was Him in that hour.

And yet John says, simply, *when Jesus therefore saw his mother.* He looked down. In the middle of His own agony, He noticed the people standing close by: a mother watching her son die, a friend who had stayed when the others ran. He saw what they were about to lose, and He did something about it.

That tells us something tender about the heart of Jesus. Your sorrow is never too small for Him to notice, even when He is carrying far more than you are. If He saw Mary from the cross, He sees you tonight on your couch, in your kitchen, in the car before you go inside.

## He gave them to each other

Jesus didn't only comfort Mary with a word. He gave her a home. "Behold thy son." And He gave John a calling: "Behold thy mother." Two grieving people, handed to one another, so neither would walk the coming days alone.

This is still one of the ways Jesus loves us. He rarely sends comfort by itself; He usually sends it with a face. A friend who calls at the right time. A neighbor who brings soup. An older believer who prays for you without being asked. The Psalm says, *"God setteth the solitary in families"* (Psalm 68:6, KJV), and the cross is where that family was born.

It also means that sometimes *you* are the John in someone's story. Someone near you may be standing at their own cross this week: a diagnosis, a loss, a quiet loneliness nobody has named. Jesus may be saying to you, gently, *behold.* Look. Notice. Make a little room.

## From that hour

The last line is my favorite: *"And from that hour that disciple took her unto his own home."* Not someday. Not once things calmed down. From that hour.

Love that follows Jesus usually looks ordinary. An extra chair at the table. A text that says, "I'm thinking of you." A visit that costs an evening. Paul put it plainly: *"Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ"* (Galatians 6:2, KJV).

## Tonight

Before you sleep, try two small things.

First, let Jesus see you. Tell Him where you're standing tonight, whatever it is you're watching or losing. He noticed Mary from the cross; He won't overlook you.

Then ask Him one quiet question: *Lord, who have You given me to look after?* A name might come to mind. Don't make a big plan. Just let it be that name you pray for tonight, and maybe the person you reach out to tomorrow.

You are not meant to carry this week alone, and neither is anyone near you. That was settled at the foot of the cross.

*Lord Jesus, You saw Your mother and Your friend even in Your pain. See me tonight. Thank You for the people You've given me, and show me who You're asking me to take into my home and heart. Amen.*
