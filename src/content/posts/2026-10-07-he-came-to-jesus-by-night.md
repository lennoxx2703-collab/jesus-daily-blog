Some questions only come out at night. During the day we're busy, competent, a little guarded. Then the dishes are done, the house goes quiet, and the things we didn't have time to think about start to surface. *Is any of this real? Can someone like me actually change? What does God really think of me?*

John tells us about a man who brought his questions to Jesus at exactly that hour.

> "Now there was a man of the Pharisees named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews. The same came to him by night." (John 3:1-2)

## A careful man after dark

Nicodemus was no beginner. He was a respected teacher, a member of the ruling council, someone people came to for answers. Maybe that's why he came at night. It's hard to be the expert in daylight and admit in front of everyone that you're not sure.

Whatever his reasons, Jesus didn't send him away for coming late or coming quietly. He didn't say, "Come back when you're ready to be seen with Me." He made room for a nervous, curious man in the dark.

That's good news if your faith feels more like a set of questions than a set of answers. Jesus has always had time for honest seekers who show up after hours.

## Starting over is His idea

Nicodemus opened politely: "Rabbi, we know that you are a teacher come from God" (John 3:2). Jesus went straight to the heart of things:

> "Most certainly I tell you, unless one is born anew, he can't see God's Kingdom." (John 3:3)

Nicodemus was confused, and honestly, who wouldn't be? "How can a man be born when he is old?" (John 3:4). It's a question a lot of us carry. *I'm set in my ways. I've been like this for years. How could I possibly start again?*

Jesus' answer is that new life isn't something we manufacture by trying harder. It's something God gives. "The wind blows where it wants to, and you hear its sound, but don't know where it comes from and where it is going. So is everyone who is born of the Spirit" (John 3:8). You can't control the wind, but you can feel it. You can't make yourself new, but God can, and He loves to.

## The sentence he heard that night

In that late conversation, Jesus said the words that have become the most famous in the whole Bible:

> "For God so loved the world, that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life. For God didn't send his Son into the world to judge the world, but that the world should be saved through him." (John 3:16-17)

It's easy to forget that these words were first spoken to one uncertain man in the dark. Not shouted at a crowd, but offered in a quiet room to someone still working it out. God's love wasn't a lecture for Nicodemus. It was an invitation.

## Slow faith is still faith

We don't know what Nicodemus said when he left. John doesn't tell us he had a dramatic breakthrough that night. But he keeps showing up in the story. Later, when the council wanted to condemn Jesus without a hearing, Nicodemus spoke up for fairness (John 7:50-51). And after the crucifixion, when most of Jesus' friends had scattered, he came again:

> "Nicodemus, who at first came to Jesus by night, also came bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about a hundred Roman pounds." (John 19:39)

The man who first came in secret ended up standing at the cross in broad daylight, honoring Jesus when it cost him something. His faith grew slowly, but it grew.

Yours can too. If you're still in the asking stage, you're not behind. You're exactly where Nicodemus started.

## Bring Him your questions tonight

So as this evening winds down, you don't need to tidy up your doubts before you pray. Bring Him the real questions, the ones you'd never say out loud at work or at church. *How can these things be?* (John 3:9) is a prayer Jesus has heard before, and He answered it with love.

He's still not sending anyone away for coming at night.

## An evening prayer

*Lord Jesus, I come to You tonight with more questions than answers. Thank You for making room for seekers in the dark. Do in me what I can't do for myself, and give me new life by Your Spirit. Help me believe that You came not to condemn me but to save me. Grow my faith, even slowly, until I can follow You in the daylight. Amen.*
