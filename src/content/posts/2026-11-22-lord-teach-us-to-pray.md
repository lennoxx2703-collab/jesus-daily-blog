Sunday morning has its own quiet. The week hasn't started yet. Maybe you're headed to church in a little while, maybe you're still in bed with a coffee, maybe you haven't prayed in a long time and something in you wonders if you still know how.

If that last one is you, you're in good company.

> "And it came to pass, that, as he was praying in a certain place, when he ceased, one of his disciples said unto him, Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples." — Luke 11:1 (KJV)

Think about who's asking. These are men who grew up in synagogues. They knew the psalms. They had heard prayers their whole lives. And still, after watching Jesus pray, one of them says, more or less: *We don't know how to do what You just did.*

Notice what made them ask. Not a sermon about prayer. Not a guilt trip. They watched Him. Luke says Jesus was praying "in a certain place," and they waited until He finished. Something about the way He talked to the Father made them hungry. It wasn't performance. It was closeness. They saw a Son talking to His Dad, and they wanted that.

## Prayer is learned, not inborn

There's a quiet lie a lot of us believe: that some people are just "good at praying," and the rest of us fumble. The disciples' request breaks that open. Prayer is something you can be taught. You're allowed to be a beginner. You're allowed to say, "Lord, I don't know how. Teach me."

That sentence is already a prayer. Maybe the most honest one there is.

And Jesus doesn't sigh at the question. He doesn't say, "You should know this by now." He simply answers:

> "When ye pray, say, Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done, as in heaven, so in earth. Give us day by day our daily bread. And forgive us our sins; for we also forgive every one that is indebted to us. And lead us not into temptation; but deliver us from evil." — Luke 11:2–4 (KJV)

## The first word

Before anything else, Jesus hands them one word: *Father.*

Not "Almighty Distant One." Not a title you have to earn your way up to. *Father.* The very word Jesus Himself used. He is inviting His friends into His own relationship — letting them speak to God the way He does.

Everything else in the prayer grows out of that word. Because He is Father, His name is worth honoring. Because He is Father, we can trust His kingdom and His will over ours. Because He is Father, it's not childish to ask for bread — for rent, for strength, for the things you need *today*. Because He is Father, we can bring our sins without hiding and ask for help forgiving the people who hurt us. And because He is Father, we can admit we are weak and ask Him to keep us from what would pull us under.

It's short. You could pray it in thirty seconds. It's not a formula to impress God; it's a shape for a heart to grow into.

## For this Sunday

You don't need perfect words this morning. You can start exactly where the disciples started: *Lord, teach me to pray.*

Then try the first word slowly. *Father.* Sit with it for a moment. Let it be true. You are not talking into the ceiling; you are talking to Someone who calls you His child.

If more words come, let them come. Bread, forgiveness, protection, His will — the prayer gives you room for all of it. If no more words come, that's alright too. A child who just climbs into a father's lap and says "Dad" has still said something real.

The disciples watched Jesus pray and wanted what He had. The beautiful thing is that He didn't keep it to Himself. He shared His Father with them. He shares Him with you.

So go ahead, wherever you are this morning. Say it like you mean it, even if your voice is small: *Our Father.*

He's listening.
