Halfway through the day is a strange place to stand. The morning's plans have met reality. Some of them survived. Some didn't. And if you're honest, there may be something bigger you've been holding onto for months — a hope, a role, a version of your life — that is quietly slipping out of your hands.

Jesus has a word for that feeling. It isn't the word we usually want. But it's a good one.

## Sir, we would see Jesus

Near the end of His ministry, in the crowded days before Passover, some Greeks come to Philip with a simple request: *"Sir, we would see Jesus"* (John 12:21, KJV). Philip tells Andrew, and the two of them bring it to the Lord.

You'd expect Jesus to say, "Bring them in." Instead He says something that sounds almost unrelated:

> "The hour is come, that the Son of man should be glorified. Verily, verily, I say unto you, Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit."
> — John 12:23–24 (KJV)

That's His answer to people who want to see Him. *If you want to see who I really am, watch the seed.*

## A seed in a drawer

Think about a single grain of wheat. You can keep it safe in a drawer for years. It will stay exactly what it is — whole, intact, and alone. Nothing bad happens to it. Nothing happens to it at all.

The only way that grain becomes a field is by going where no seed would choose to go: down into the dark, under the dirt, where its old shape breaks open. From above, it looks like loss. From below, it's the beginning of everything.

Jesus was talking first about Himself. Within days He would be buried in a borrowed tomb. To His friends it would look like the end of the story. But that one grain, laid in the ground, has been bearing fruit ever since — in every person who has ever been forgiven, healed, or brought home through His death and rising. You are part of that harvest.

## Then He turns to us

> "He that loveth his life shall lose it; and he that hateth his life in this world shall keep it unto life eternal. If any man serve me, let him follow me; and where I am, there shall also my servant be: if any man serve me, him will my Father honour."
> — John 12:25–26 (KJV)

"Hateth" is strong language, and Jesus isn't asking you to despise yourself or the good gifts of your life. He's talking about grip. The life we clutch — controlled, protected, kept in the drawer — slowly shrinks. The life we place in the Father's hands, even when that feels like burying it, is the one that grows.

Maybe you know exactly what that means today. A plan that fell apart. A relationship that changed. A dream you carried for years that you're finally having to set down. It can feel like a funeral.

But notice what Jesus does *not* say. He doesn't say, "Lose it, and that's that." He says *fruit*. He says *where I am, there shall also my servant be*. You're not going into the ground alone. He went first, and He is already there.

## He felt it too

The very next thing Jesus says is honest: *"Now is my soul troubled; and what shall I say? Father, save me from this hour: but for this cause came I unto this hour"* (John 12:27, KJV).

He didn't pretend burial was easy. His soul was troubled. If yours is too, you are in good company. Surrender isn't the absence of feeling; it's trusting the Father with what you feel.

## For this midday

Take a minute before the afternoon rushes in. Ask yourself, quietly: *What am I holding so tightly that nothing can grow from it?*

Then open your hand, even a little, and pray something like this:

*Lord Jesus, You fell into the ground for me. I'm afraid of losing what I'm holding. But I'd rather be a seed in Your hands than a grain kept safe in a drawer. Take what I give You. Grow what You want from it. And wherever You are, let me be there too. Amen.*

The ground looks dark from up here. But it was the place where the harvest began.
