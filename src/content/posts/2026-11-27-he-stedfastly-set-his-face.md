Midday on a Friday has its own feeling. The week is almost over, but not quite. The energy you started Monday with is mostly spent, and what's left is a kind of tired determination: just get through the afternoon. Just finish what you started.

Luke gives us one short sentence that turns the whole story of Jesus in a new direction. Up to this point, He has been teaching and healing all around Galilee. Then:

> "And it came to pass, when the time was come that he should be received up, he stedfastly set his face to go to Jerusalem, and sent messengers before his face: and they went, and entered into a village of the Samaritans, to make ready for him. And they did not receive him, because his face was as though he would go to Jerusalem." — Luke 9:51–53 (KJV)

### A face that knew where it was going

Jesus was not drifting toward the cross. He was not caught off guard by it. He knew what Jerusalem meant: betrayal, a crown of thorns, a Roman cross. He had already told His disciples plainly that He would suffer and be killed (Luke 9:22). And still, Luke says, He *set His face* to go.

That phrase echoes an old prophecy about the Servant of the Lord:

> "For the Lord GOD will help me; therefore shall I not be confounded: therefore have I set my face like a flint, and I know that I shall not be ashamed." — Isaiah 50:7 (KJV)

Like a flint. Not hard toward people, but immovable in His purpose. Nothing along the road was going to talk Him out of loving you all the way to the end.

It even showed on Him. The Samaritan village could tell just by looking: *his face was as though he would go to Jerusalem.* His resolve was visible.

### Why He kept walking

It's worth asking what held Him to that road. It wasn't stubbornness, and it wasn't duty in some cold sense. Hebrews gives the answer:

> "Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God." — Hebrews 12:2 (KJV)

*For the joy that was set before him.* The joy was redemption. A people brought home. It was you, forgiven and found. Jesus looked down the road past the suffering, saw what it would win, and kept walking.

Paul put it in the most personal words he could find: "the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me" (Galatians 2:20). Not loved humanity in general and accepted me as a side effect. *Loved me. Gave himself for me.*

### On a Friday, of all days

It's fitting to sit with this on a Friday. Christians have long remembered Friday as the day Jesus finished that road, when the face set toward Jerusalem was struck, spit on, and finally lifted up on a cross. Every step from Luke 9:51 on was heading there.

So if you're running low this afternoon, here's something to lean on: **your salvation doesn't rest on how steady your face is. It rests on how steady His was.** He didn't turn back when the village closed its doors. He didn't turn back in Gethsemane. He didn't come down from the cross when they dared Him to. He finished.

That means on the days your resolve wobbles, and everyone's does, you are still held by a Savior whose resolve never did.

### Setting our faces toward Him

His faithfulness also gently invites ours. Not a grim, white-knuckled kind, but the kind that keeps turning back toward Him. A follower's face can be set too: toward the next honest step, the next kind word, the next quiet prayer in the middle of a busy day.

You don't have to see the whole road. He already walked the hardest part of it. You just have to keep looking at Him.

### A midday prayer

*Lord Jesus, thank You for setting Your face toward Jerusalem when You knew what waited there. Thank You for not turning back. When I'm tired and my resolve runs thin, remind me that You finished what You started, for me. Turn my face toward You again this afternoon, and carry me through the rest of this day. Amen.*
