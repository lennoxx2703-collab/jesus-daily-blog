Saturday morning has a different pace. No alarm in the same tone, no meeting at nine, maybe a slower coffee. And in that quieter space, the real questions sometimes surface — the ones the busy week kept pushing down. *Am I actually all right with God? Is something still missing?*

A young man once carried that question all the way to Jesus. Mark tells us he came running, and he knelt in the road.

> "And when he was gone forth into the way, there came one running, and kneeled to him, and asked him, Good Master, what shall I do that I may inherit eternal life?" (Mark 10:17, KJV)

He had done a lot right. He could say he had kept the commandments since his youth. He was earnest, respectable, wealthy. And still he ran, because something in him knew that being good on paper was not the same as being at rest.

## Before the word, a look

What Jesus does next is easy to rush past. Before He says anything hard, Mark slows down:

> "Then Jesus beholding him loved him, and said unto him, One thing thou lackest: go thy way, sell whatsoever thou hast, and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven: and come, take up the cross, and follow me." (Mark 10:21, KJV)

*Jesus beholding him loved him.* He looked at the man — really looked, the way only He can — and loved him. Not after the man passed a test. Not because the answer would go well. The love came first.

That order matters this morning. Whatever Jesus might be asking of you, He does not ask it with folded arms. He asks it as Someone who has already looked at you fully and loved what He saw. His call is never the opposite of His love. It is His love, speaking honestly.

## The one thing

"One thing thou lackest." Jesus put His finger on the place the man's heart was actually resting — his possessions. Not because money is the only danger, but because for this man it had become the quiet throne. Jesus did not hand him another rule to keep. He invited him to let go of what held him and to come, follow Him.

The man went away sad, "for he had great possessions." It is one of the saddest sentences in the Gospels. And notice: Jesus did not chase him with a lighter offer. He let the love and the invitation stand.

For each of us the "one thing" may look different. A grudge. A reputation. A need to be in control. A comfort that has slowly become a hiding place. Saturday is a good day to ask Him gently, *Lord, what am I holding that is holding me?* — and to trust that He asks out of love.

## Possible with God

The disciples were stunned. If this man could not make it, who could? Jesus answered:

> "With men it is impossible, but not with God: for with God all things are possible." (Mark 10:27, KJV)

That is the good news under the hard word. You are not asked to pry your own fingers loose by willpower. The same Jesus who looks at you with love is the One who makes surrender possible. What you cannot release on your own, God can loosen in you.

## Beginning Saturday with Him

So before the errands, the family plans, or the long-overdue rest, sit for a minute under His gaze. Let it be true that Jesus looks at you and loves you — right now, before you fix anything.

Then pray simply: *Jesus, You see me and You love me. Show me the one thing I'm clinging to. Give me grace to open my hands, and let me follow You today.*

## A Saturday blessing

May you know the look of Jesus resting on you with love. May whatever holds you too tightly begin to loosen in His hands. And may this unhurried day become a day of following Him — not out of fear of what you lack, but out of joy in the One who already loves you.
