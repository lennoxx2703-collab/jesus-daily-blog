Saturday has a different sound to it. Maybe there are kids in your house today, or in the park down the street, loud and sticky and completely uninterested in being efficient. Maybe your home is quiet and you miss that noise. Either way, let's sit for a few minutes with a scene Mark tells us about, because it says something about how Jesus sees the people the grown-ups would rather move along.

> "They were bringing to him little children, that he should touch them, but the disciples rebuked those who were bringing them." (Mark 10:13)

You can almost picture it. Jesus has been teaching hard things. The crowds are big, the questions are serious, and the disciples have decided that their job is to protect his time. So when parents start bringing their small children for a blessing, the disciples step in. *Not now. He's busy. This isn't important enough.*

I don't think they were being cruel. I think they were being sensible. And that's what makes Jesus' reaction so striking.

> "But when Jesus saw it, he was moved with indignation, and said to them, 'Allow the little children to come to me! Don't forbid them, for God's Kingdom belongs to such as these.'" (Mark 10:14)

Indignation. That's a strong word, and Mark doesn't use it lightly. The thing that made Jesus angry here wasn't a child interrupting him. It was someone standing between a child and him.

## The ones who get moved along

Most of us have been on both sides of that door.

Some of us have been the child. Not literally, maybe, but in the sense that we've felt too small to matter. Too young in the faith, too messy, too far behind, too ordinary to take up Jesus' time. Somewhere along the way someone told us, out loud or without words, that we needed to get it together before we came close. And we believed it.

If that's you, hear his voice today: *Allow them to come to me.* He isn't annoyed that you showed up. He is glad. The people who thought they were helping by keeping you at a distance were wrong, and Jesus said so with some heat.

And some of us have been the disciples. We've guarded the door. We've decided, quietly, who seemed serious enough, put-together enough, worth the effort. We've rushed past people because we were busy doing important things for God. This passage is a gentle correction for us too. The interruptions we wave away may be the very people Jesus wants to hold.

## Receiving like a child

Then Jesus turns it around and says something even more surprising:

> "Most certainly I tell you, whoever will not receive God's Kingdom like a little child, he will in no way enter into it." (Mark 10:15)

He doesn't say children need to grow up and become like the disciples. He says the disciples need to become like the children.

What does a little child do when someone they trust holds out their arms? They don't negotiate. They don't present their résumé. They don't wonder if they've earned it. They just come. They receive.

That's the posture Jesus is talking about. Not childish, but childlike: open-handed, unpretending, willing to be carried. The Kingdom isn't a prize for the most impressive. It's a gift for the ones who will let themselves be given to.

That can be hard for capable adults. We're used to earning things. We'd rather show up competent. But at some point, every one of us has to come to Jesus the way those children did: small, dependent, and welcome.

## Taken up in his arms

Mark ends the scene with one of the tenderest lines in the Gospels:

> "He took them in his arms, and blessed them, laying his hands on them." (Mark 10:16)

He didn't just allow it. He didn't give a quick wave from across the crowd. He picked them up. He held them. He put his hands on them and blessed them, one by one.

That's who Jesus is. Not a busy teacher tolerating interruptions, but a Savior who stops, kneels, and gathers up the small.

So wherever you are in the middle of this Saturday, whether it's loud or quiet, full or lonely, here's an invitation. Come to him the way a child would. Bring the thing you think is too little to mention. Bring the version of yourself you think he'd rather wait for. Nobody gets to stand in that doorway and send you away. He's already holding out his arms.

*Lord Jesus, thank you that you were indignant on behalf of the small, and that you still are. Forgive me for the times I've kept others from you, and for the times I've kept myself away. Teach me to receive your Kingdom like a child: simply, gladly, without pretending. Take me up in your arms today and bless me. Amen.*
