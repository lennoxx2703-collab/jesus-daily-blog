Friday mornings arrive already crowded. The week wants finishing. Messages stack up overnight. Someone needs an answer before lunch. Even before the kettle boils, the day can feel like it has claimed you.

Mark shows us Jesus on a morning like that — or harder. The night before, the whole city had gathered at the door. He healed until sundown faded into dark. Fame was spreading. Need was loud. And still:

> "And in the morning, rising up a great while before day, he went out, and departed into a solitary place, and there prayed. And Simon and they that were with him followed after him. And when they had found him, they said unto him, All men seek for thee." (Mark 1:35–37, KJV)

## A great while before day

Jesus does not wait for a quiet gap in the schedule. He rises *a great while before day* — while the house is still sleeping, while Capernaum has not yet begun to knock. Solitude with the Father is not leftover time. It is first time.

That is a word for Friday. You may not have hours. You may not have a hillside. But you can have a few minutes that belong to Him before the inbox, the commute, the school run, the shift. Not because you are more spiritual than yesterday, but because you are human, and humans run dry when they only run.

He went out. He departed. He found a solitary place. The verbs are deliberate. Communion with the Father did not happen by accident on the busiest morning of His week so far. He made room for it.

## And there prayed

Mark does not record the words. He only tells us that Jesus prayed. The Son who could heal a city still needed the Father. The One people sought still sought God first.

If prayer felt optional to Jesus, it would be optional for us. It wasn't. So it isn't. Not as a performance. Not as a way to earn the day. As breathing. As returning to the One who sends you into the day you are about to face.

You do not have to sound impressive in that solitary place. You can bring the unfinished list, the tired body, the person you're worried about, the decision you don't want to rush. He already knows. Prayer is how we stop pretending we can carry Friday alone.

## All men seek for thee

Then the disciples find Him. Simon speaks for the urgent world: *"All men seek for thee."* It sounds almost like a rebuke. People are waiting. The door is crowded again. Why are You out here?

Jesus does not apologize for praying. Later in the chapter He will keep moving — to other towns, other needs — because the Father's calling is larger than one city's demand. But first notice this: the crowd's urgency does not cancel communion. Need is real. Prayer is still first.

That matters on a Friday when everyone seems to want something from you. Your family. Your job. Your phone. Your own anxious mind. "All men seek for thee" can feel like the motto of the morning. Jesus shows another way: be found by the Father before you are found by the crowd.

You will step into the noise soon enough. Emails will wait the length of a honest prayer. Most of them will. And even when they don't, your soul still needs what only the Father gives.

## Then into the day

Jesus leaves the solitary place rested in the Father — not emptied by the people, but filled for them. That is the shape of a morning with Him. Quiet first. Then the calling. Not escape from Friday, but strength for it.

So before the day's noise thickens, rise a little early if you can. Or simply stop for a minute where you are. Depart, even inwardly, into a solitary place. Pray. Let the urgent voices wait outside the door for a while. Then walk into Friday with Him — the One who sought the Father before the city sought Him.

"My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up." (Psalm 5:3, KJV)
