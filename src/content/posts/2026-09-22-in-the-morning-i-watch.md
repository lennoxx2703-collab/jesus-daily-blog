Some mornings begin with words already lined up: the todo list, the worry, the apology we still owe, the message we hope will come. We talk to God quickly, if we talk at all, and then hurry into the day as if prayer were a box to check before the real work starts.

David describes something slower and more expectant:

> “O Lord, in the morning you hear my voice; in the morning I prepare a sacrifice for you and watch.” (Psalm 5:3)

He does not only speak. He prepares. And then he watches. The morning becomes a place of attention — not frantic striving, but a heart turned toward God and ready to notice Him.

## More than a morning checklist

It is easy to treat prayer like another task. We say the right phrases, name the needs, and move on. Psalm 5 invites a different posture. David trusts that God hears his voice *in the morning*. That alone is mercy. We do not have to shout our way into heaven or earn an audience. The day opens with a God who already listens.

But David does not stop at being heard. He prepares a sacrifice — in his setting, an act of worship offered with care. For us, that preparation may look quieter: a few honest sentences, a psalm read slowly, silence before the phone, gratitude before urgency. Preparation is not performance. It is making room. It is saying, before the day claims every inch of us, “This time is Yours.”

Then comes the word that can reshape a whole morning: *watch*.

## Watching for what God will do

Watching is not passive boredom. It is hope with open eyes. David has prayed, and now he looks for God’s movement — not only in dramatic answers, but in the small mercies that usually slip past an unattentive heart. A needed word of peace. A restraint from a sharp reply. Strength for a hard conversation. Kindness we did not manufacture. Light on a decision that felt locked.

Jesus later teaches His friends to watch and pray (Mark 14:38). Watching keeps prayer from becoming a monologue. We speak, and then we stay present. We ask, and then we notice. Faith is not only petition; it is attention.

Some of us watch for the wrong things. We watch the news first. We watch other people’s reactions. We watch our own anxiety like a screen we cannot turn off. Psalm 5 redirects the gaze. In the morning, before we give our attention away, we can give it to the Lord who already hears us.

## When the morning feels empty

Of course, not every morning feels holy. Some feel thin, tired, or loud with dread. Watching can feel pointless when nothing seems to change. David knew that world too. The psalm around this verse is not soft wallpaper; it names enemies, injustice, and the need for refuge. Watching does not deny trouble. It refuses to let trouble be the first and last word.

Lamentations reminds us that the Lord’s mercies are new every morning (Lamentations 3:22–23). New does not always mean spectacular. Often it means enough. Enough grace for this hour. Enough steadiness for the next faithful step. Enough light to walk without pretending we can see the whole road.

If your morning feels empty, you are still invited. You do not need a polished prayer. You can come with a tired voice and a half-formed hope. God hears. Prepare what you can — even a single honest sentence. Then watch, not for a perfect day, but for the nearness of Jesus, who meets people at ordinary dawns and broken tables alike.

## A simple way to begin

You might try this as the day opens:

Speak briefly. Tell God what is true — the joy, the fear, the person on your mind.

Prepare simply. Set aside five quiet minutes. Read a short passage. Breathe. Offer the day before you try to manage it.

Watch gently. As you move into work, conversation, and ordinary tasks, keep a soft attention: *Where is mercy showing up? Where is God inviting trust?*

Jesus often rose early to be with the Father (Mark 1:35). He did not do this because mornings are magic. He did it because love seeks communion before the crowds arrive. We are invited into that same nearness — not as spiritual athletes, but as people who need God at the start.

So this morning, let the first movement of your heart be toward Him. He hears your voice. Prepare what you can. And then watch — because the God who listens in the morning is also the God who walks with you through the day.
