Sunday evening has a particular feeling. The weekend is winding down, and the week ahead starts quietly filling your head. The emails you'll open tomorrow. The thing you didn't finish on Friday. The list that never seems to get shorter. Before Monday even arrives, you're already busy inside.

There's a woman in Luke's Gospel who would understand that feeling completely.

## Martha received Him into her house

> "Now it came to pass, as they went, that he entered into a certain village: and a certain woman named Martha received him into her house." (Luke 10:38, KJV)

Let's be fair to Martha first. She's the one who opened the door. She welcomed Jesus and His friends into her home, and that meant food, water, space, and a lot of work. Her love was real, and it showed up in her hands.

> "And she had a sister called Mary, which also sat at Jesus' feet, and heard his word." (Luke 10:39, KJV)

So now we have two sisters in one house. One is moving from room to room. The other is sitting still, listening.

## Lord, dost Thou not care?

> "But Martha was cumbered about much serving, and came to him, and said, Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone? bid her therefore that she help me." (Luke 10:40, KJV)

*Cumbered* is a good, heavy word. It means weighed down, pulled in every direction. And listen to what comes out of her when the weight gets too much: *Lord, don't You care?*

That's often the question underneath our busyness. Not just "Who's going to help me?" but "Does He even notice what I'm carrying?" Martha said it out loud, right to His face. That takes honesty, and Jesus didn't send her away for it.

## Martha, Martha

> "And Jesus answered and said unto her, Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things: But one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her." (Luke 10:41–42, KJV)

He says her name twice. You don't say someone's name twice when you're annoyed. You say it like that when you love them and want them to stop and look at you.

*Careful* in the old English means full of care, anxious. Jesus sees exactly what's happening inside her: many things, all pulling at once. He doesn't tell her that serving is wrong or that the meal doesn't matter. He just gently shows her that she's been carrying it all without Him at the center.

*One thing is needful.* Not a hundred things. One. To be with Him. To hear Him. To let Him be the still point that everything else moves around.

And then the promise: it "shall not be taken away." The dishes get dirty again. The to-do list grows back by Wednesday. But the time you spend at His feet is never wasted and never lost. It stays with you.

## For your Sunday evening

You don't have to choose between being responsible and being close to Jesus. Martha didn't need to stop loving people with her hands. She needed to stop doing it alone, worried, and away from Him.

So before the week starts, try being Mary for ten minutes. Leave the list where it is. Sit somewhere quiet and read Luke 10:38–42 slowly. Then tell Him honestly what's crowding you, the way Martha did. He can handle it.

Then listen for your own name, said gently, maybe twice.

A simple prayer for tonight: *Jesus, I'm careful and troubled about many things. Tomorrow will be full. Tonight, let me sit with You. Be the one thing at the center of my week.*

Monday will come with its many things. Let it find you having already chosen the good part.
