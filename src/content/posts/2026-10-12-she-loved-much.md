By Monday evening, a lot of us are carrying a quiet list. The sharp word you said this morning. The thing you meant to do and didn't. And underneath those, maybe something older, a part of your story you'd rather nobody ever brought up at the table.

Tonight I want to take you to a dinner where exactly that happened, and to show you what Jesus did about it.

## An uninvited guest

A Pharisee named Simon invited Jesus to eat at his house. It was a respectable evening with respectable people. Then someone walked in who didn't belong.

> "And, behold, a woman in the city, which was a sinner, when she knew that Jesus sat at meat in the Pharisee's house, brought an alabaster box of ointment, and stood at his feet behind him weeping, and began to wash his feet with tears, and did wipe them with the hairs of her head, and kissed his feet, and anointed them with the ointment." (Luke 7:37–38, KJV)

Luke doesn't tell us her name. The town only knew her by her reputation. But notice what she did. She didn't make a speech or offer an explanation. She came close to Jesus, and her tears said everything she couldn't.

Simon saw it differently:

> "This man, if he were a prophet, would have known who and what manner of woman this is that toucheth him: for she is a sinner." (Luke 7:39, KJV)

He didn't even say it out loud. He only thought it. But Jesus heard it anyway.

## "Seest thou this woman?"

Jesus told Simon a short story about two people who owed money, one a great deal and one a little. Neither could pay, and the lender simply forgave them both. "Tell me therefore," Jesus asked, "which of them will love him most?" (Luke 7:42, KJV). Simon gave the obvious answer.

Then Jesus did something tender. He turned toward the woman, but He kept speaking to Simon:

> "Seest thou this woman? I entered into thine house, thou gavest me no water for my feet: but she hath washed my feet with tears, and wiped them with the hairs of her head." (Luke 7:44, KJV)

*Do you see her?* That's the real question of the evening. Simon saw a category. Jesus saw a person. Everyone else in the room had already decided who she was. Jesus looked at her and saw love, the kind of love that only comes from someone who knows how much they've been forgiven.

If you've ever felt like people only see your worst chapter, hear this: Jesus isn't reading you by your reputation. He sees you.

## Many sins, much love

> "Wherefore I say unto thee, Her sins, which are many, are forgiven; for she loved much: but to whom little is forgiven, the same loveth little." (Luke 7:47, KJV)

Jesus didn't pretend her sins were small. He said they were *many*. Grace isn't God shrugging at what we've done. It's God looking straight at all of it and forgiving it anyway.

And then He turned and spoke to her directly, maybe for the first time anyone had spoken gently to her in years:

> "Thy sins are forgiven." (Luke 7:48, KJV)

The others at the table muttered about who He thought He was. Jesus didn't argue with them. He had one more thing to say, and it was for her:

> "Thy faith hath saved thee; go in peace." (Luke 7:50, KJV)

She came in carrying shame. She went home carrying peace. Same woman, same street, same town that still whispered. But something had changed that no whisper could undo.

## For tonight

Maybe you're more like Simon tonight, keeping a tidy score of who deserves what. Or maybe you're more like her, sure that if people really knew you, you'd be asked to leave the table. Either way, the invitation is the same: come close to Jesus.

Before you sleep, try this. Bring Him the thing you'd rather nobody mentioned. You don't need the right words. Tears are fine. Silence is fine. Then listen for what He said to her, because He says it to you too:

*Your sins are forgiven. Your faith has saved you. Go in peace.*

You don't have to earn your way back into the room. He already made a place for you at His feet. Rest there tonight.
