By Friday at lunchtime, a lot of us are running on fumes. The week has taken more than it gave. The inbox, the worries, the small arguments, the scrolling that was supposed to relax us but somehow didn't. You might be sitting with your food right now and realizing you can't remember the last time you felt really *alive* — not just busy, not just functional, but alive.

If that's where you are, here's a short word from Jesus for the middle of your day.

> "I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture. The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly." — John 10:9–10 (KJV)

## There is a thief

Jesus doesn't pretend that everything draining us is neutral. He says plainly: there is a thief. Something comes to *steal, kill, and destroy*.

Most of the time it doesn't look dramatic. It looks like a slow leak. Joy gets stolen by comparison. Peace gets killed by a hundred small anxieties. Relationships get destroyed not in one blow but by years of distraction and hurry. Sin promises life and quietly takes it instead. And at the end of a week like that, we can feel like sheep who've been out in the cold too long.

There's something freeing in hearing Jesus name it. That emptiness you feel isn't proof that you're failing at life. It's evidence that something has been taking from you — and that Jesus sees it.

## There is a door

Then He says, "I am the door."

Shepherds in Jesus' day would gather the sheep into a stone fold at night, and sometimes the shepherd himself would lie down across the opening. Nothing got in or out except past him. He *was* the door.

Notice what happens to the sheep who come in through Him: they "go in and out, and find pasture." That's not a picture of being locked up in religion. It's a picture of safety and freedom together. Coming in to rest. Going out to feed. Living an ordinary day — work, meals, conversations — with the Shepherd guarding the way.

## Not just more, but *abundant*

"I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly."

Abundant life doesn't mean a life with no hard days. Jesus said this a short while before He walked to a cross. It means a life that is full *of Him* — overflowing, not running dry. Later He prayed, "this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent" (John 17:3). The fullness He offers is first of all a friendship: knowing God, and being known.

It's the same thing David sang about long before: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want. He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters" (Psalm 23:1–2). The pasture isn't something we earn by working harder this afternoon. It's where the Shepherd leads the sheep who follow Him.

I'll admit I often look for abundance in the wrong places — one more accomplishment, one more purchase, one more hour of something that numbs me. And I come back emptier. Jesus doesn't scold me for being hungry. He just keeps saying: *the pasture is through Me.*

## For the rest of today

You don't have to fix your whole life before the weekend. Just take a minute, right where you're sitting, and walk back through the door:

*Lord Jesus, You see what this week has taken from me. I've been looking for life in places that only drained me. You are the door, and You are the Shepherd. I come in through You again. Guard what's left of this day, lead me to green pasture, and fill me with the life You came to give. Amen.*

Then finish your lunch slowly. Go back out into the afternoon knowing whose sheep you are. The thief takes. Jesus gives — and He gives abundantly.
