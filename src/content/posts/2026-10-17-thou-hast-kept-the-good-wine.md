Saturdays are wedding days in a lot of places. Somewhere right now, someone is straightening a tie, checking the flowers one more time, and hoping nothing goes wrong. Most of us know that feeling even if we aren't at a wedding. We plan, we prepare, and we quietly hope we won't run short of whatever the day needs.

Jesus' first miracle happened at a party like that, in a small village, at the exact moment something ran out.

## They have no wine

> "And the third day there was a marriage in Cana of Galilee; and the mother of Jesus was there: And both Jesus was called, and his disciples, to the marriage." (John 2:1–2, KJV)

A wedding in that world could last for days. Running out of wine wasn't a small slip. It meant embarrassment for a young couple and their families in front of the whole village, the kind of story people remember.

And then it happened.

> "And when they wanted wine, the mother of Jesus saith unto him, They have no wine." (John 2:3, KJV)

Notice what Mary does. She doesn't panic, and she doesn't lay out a plan. She simply brings the need to Jesus and names it plainly: *They have no wine.*

That is a good shape for prayer on an ordinary Saturday. *Lord, we're short on patience in this house. Lord, I'm out of energy. Lord, there isn't enough money for this month.* You don't need perfect words. You can just tell Him what has run out.

## Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it

Jesus' answer sounds puzzling at first: "mine hour is not yet come" (John 2:4, KJV). But Mary trusts Him anyway. She turns to the servants and says the last words John ever records from her:

> "Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it." (John 2:5, KJV)

It may be the best advice anyone has ever given.

What Jesus asks them to do is surprisingly ordinary. There are six large stone waterpots standing nearby.

> "Jesus saith unto them, Fill the waterpots with water. And they filled them up to the brim." (John 2:7, KJV)

Water, not wine. Hard, heavy work, carrying jar after jar from the well. It must have felt pointless. Yet they filled them *to the brim*, holding nothing back from a command they didn't yet understand.

Sometimes following Jesus looks just like that: unglamorous obedience in small things. Being kind when you're tired. Showing up again. Praying one more time. You pour in water, and you leave the wine to Him.

## The best for last

> "When the ruler of the feast had tasted the water that was made wine, and knew not whence it was: (but the servants which drew the water knew;) the governor of the feast called the bridegroom, And saith unto him, Every man at the beginning doth set forth good wine; and when men have well drunk, then that which is worse: but thou hast kept the good wine until now." (John 2:9–10, KJV)

I love that little line in parentheses: *but the servants which drew the water knew.* The important guest had no idea where the wine came from. The people doing the plain work saw the miracle up close.

And look at the quality of what Jesus gives. It isn't barely enough. It isn't second-rate wine to get the couple through the evening. It's the best of the whole feast, poured out when everyone expected the party to wind down.

That's what He's like. The world tends to give its best first and then fade. Jesus keeps the good wine until now. Whatever has run dry for you this week, He isn't stingy, and He isn't finished.

> "This beginning of miracles did Jesus in Cana of Galilee, and manifested forth his glory; and his disciples believed on him." (John 2:11, KJV)

He showed His glory first not in a temple or a palace, but at a family celebration, caring about a young couple's joy. He cares about yours too, and about the ordinary places where you're running low.

## A midday prayer

*Lord Jesus, You know what has run out in my life today. I bring it to You plainly. Help me do whatever You tell me, even when it looks like just carrying water. Fill what is empty with Your goodness, and let me taste again that You keep the best for last. Amen.*
