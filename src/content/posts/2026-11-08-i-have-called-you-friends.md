Sunday midday is often a table. Maybe a long lunch after church, maybe soup alone at the kitchen counter, maybe a plate balanced on your knee while the afternoon decides what it wants to be. Wherever you're eating, it's a good hour to remember a meal Jesus shared, and a word He chose there on purpose.

It was the last night. The supper was over, Judas had gone out into the dark, and Jesus was talking to the ones who stayed. He knew what was coming in a matter of hours. If there was ever a time to speak only the most important things, this was it. And among those things, He said this:

> "Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends. Ye are my friends, if ye do whatsoever I command you. Henceforth I call you not servants; for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth: but I have called you friends; for all things that I have heard of my Father I have made known unto you." — John 15:13–15 (KJV)

## Not servants

Think about who was in that room. Fishermen who had argued about which of them was greatest. Peter, who would deny Him before sunrise. Men who would scatter and hide. Jesus knew all of that, and He still looked at them and said, *friends.*

A servant is useful. A servant shows up, does the work, and goes home without being told why. There's nothing shameful in serving, and Jesus Himself washed feet that same evening. But He wanted them to know that He didn't see them as hired help in His Father's house. He had let them in on the plan. He had told them what the Father told Him. You don't do that with staff. You do that with friends.

Some of us relate to God mostly as servants who are a little afraid of the boss. We try to keep our record clean, do our duties, stay out of trouble, and hope it's enough. Jesus doesn't throw out obedience here. He says, *ye are my friends, if ye do whatsoever I command you.* But notice the order. The friendship isn't a wage paid for good behavior. The obedience grows out of the friendship, the way you naturally want to please someone who has loved you well.

## What friendship cost Him

He didn't just say the word. He told them what it would cost: *greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends.* And then He went and did exactly that. The next day He laid down His life, for them and for us, while we were still the kind of friends who fall asleep in the garden and run when it gets hard.

That's the strange mercy of it. Human friendship usually starts with something in common, someone who's good company and keeps their side of things. Jesus starts with love, gives Himself first, and then invites us into a friendship we could never have earned.

Proverbs says "there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother" (Proverbs 18:24, KJV). On the cross, and in the empty tomb that this morning's reading remembered, we see Who that Friend really is.

## At your table today

So wherever you are this midday, at a crowded table or an empty one, you're not eating alone in the way that matters most. You belong to Someone who calls you by a warmer name than you'd dare to call yourself.

Maybe that means talking to Him this afternoon the way you'd talk to a friend, without the stiff formal voice, just honestly. Telling Him what the week ahead looks like. Asking what's on His heart. Listening for a while. Friends make room for each other.

And maybe it means passing that friendship on. Someone near you could use a call, a text, an invitation to share the next meal. The love that laid down its life tends to spill over into ordinary kindness.

*Lord Jesus, thank You for calling me Your friend when I've so often been a poor one. Thank You for loving me first and loving me all the way to the cross. Teach me to walk with You gladly, to obey You out of love and not fear, and to be a true friend to the people You put at my table. Amen.*
