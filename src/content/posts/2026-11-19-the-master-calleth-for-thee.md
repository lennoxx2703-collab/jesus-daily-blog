Some days you don't run toward anything. You just sit.

The morning slipped by. The to-do list is still there. Maybe there's a grief underneath it all, or a disappointment you haven't named yet, or just a tiredness that makes it hard to get up and meet anybody — even God. If that's where you are at midday, there's a woman in John's Gospel who knows exactly how that feels.

> "Then Martha, as soon as she heard that Jesus was coming, went and met him: but Mary sat still in the house." — John 11:20 (KJV)

## Mary sat still

Lazarus had been dead four days. The house in Bethany was full of mourners, and the two sisters were reacting to the same loss in completely different ways. Martha heard Jesus was near and went straight out to meet Him, questions and all. Mary stayed where she was.

I love that the Bible doesn't scold her for it. There's no line saying Mary should have had more faith or moved faster. Grief does that sometimes. It pins you to the chair. You know Jesus is close, you've even heard He's on the road, and you still can't quite make yourself go.

Maybe she was hurt that He hadn't come sooner. When she finally does see Him, her first words are, "Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died" (John 11:32). That's not a polite sentence. It's an honest one. And honest is what Jesus has always asked for.

## "He calleth for thee"

Then this happens:

> "And when she had so said, she went her way, and called Mary her sister secretly, saying, The Master is come, and calleth for thee. As soon as she heard that, she arose quickly, and came unto him." — John 11:28–29 (KJV)

Notice what changed. Not her circumstances — Lazarus was still in the tomb. Not her feelings, at least not yet. What changed was that she heard Jesus was asking for *her*. Not the crowd. Not the mourners. Her.

And notice where Jesus is waiting. John tells us He "was not yet come into the town" (John 11:30). He stopped outside, away from the noise, and sent word for Mary. He didn't barge into her grief with a crowd watching. He made a quiet place and called her to it.

That's how He still does it. Long before Bethany, God had said to His people, "Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine" (Isaiah 43:1). Jesus isn't waiting for you to pull yourself together before He wants you near. He's the one who asks first.

## You don't have to arrive composed

Mary didn't walk out with her questions sorted. She came "quickly," but she came crying, and she fell at His feet and said the hard thing out loud. And Jesus didn't correct her theology on the spot. John says when He saw her weeping, "he groaned in the spirit, and was troubled" (John 11:33). Her sorrow moved Him.

So if you've been sitting still today, here's the good news for your lunch break: you don't need a better mood to come to Him. You don't need the right words. The invitation doesn't depend on how you feel. It depends on who is calling.

## For the rest of today

Take a minute somewhere quiet — the car, a stairwell, a short walk around the block. Imagine someone leaning over and whispering the words Martha whispered: *The Master is come, and calleth for thee.* Then just go to Him, the way you are.

*Lord Jesus, I've been sitting still in a heavy place. I don't have it all figured out, and some of my questions still sound like complaints. But You asked for me, so I'm coming. Here I am. Meet me in the middle of this day. Amen.*

He's not far off. He stopped on the road, and He's asking for you by name.
