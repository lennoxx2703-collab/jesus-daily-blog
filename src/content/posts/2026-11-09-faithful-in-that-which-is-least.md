It is the middle of a Monday. The morning's good intentions have met the actual day. There are emails to answer, dishes in the sink, a form to fill out, a child asking the same question for the fourth time, a task at work that nobody will ever notice you did well. None of it feels like the stuff of faith. It feels like the stuff you get through so you can get to the things that matter.

Jesus saw it differently. In the middle of teaching about money and stewardship, He said something so simple it is easy to walk right past:

> "He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much: and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much." — Luke 16:10 (KJV)

Notice what He does not say. He does not say the least things are unimportant warm-ups for the real work. He says the least things are where faithfulness is actually formed. The small, unseen, repeated moments are not a distraction from following Him. They are the place where following Him happens.

## The size of the task is not the size of the moment

We tend to measure our days by their big moments: the hard conversation, the important decision, the time we finally prayed for an hour. But most of life is made of the in-between. And Jesus, who spent about thirty years in a carpenter's workshop in a small town before He ever preached a sermon, knows the in-between better than anyone.

Think about that for a moment. The Son of God planed boards and fixed doors and measured twice and cut once, year after year, and the Father was pleased with Him before He had done a single miracle. Ordinary work, done faithfully, was not beneath Him. It was part of His obedience.

So the reply you write with patience instead of sharpness, the honest number on the report, the extra minute you give to someone who is lonely, the job you finish properly when a shortcut would have passed — these are not small to Him. They are the "least" He is talking about, and He calls them faithfulness.

## Working for a different audience

Paul put it this way to people who had very little control over their working lives:

> "And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men." — Colossians 3:23 (KJV)

That changes the afternoon. Your manager may not notice. Your family may not say thank you. The customer may be rude anyway. But you are not finally working for any of them. You are doing this next ordinary thing in front of Jesus, and He notices everything. Nothing done for Him disappears.

This is not a call to try harder or to turn every task into a spiritual performance. It is an invitation to rest. You don't have to make your Monday impressive. You only have to be faithful with what is actually in front of you, one small thing at a time, with Him.

## What He says at the end

Jesus told a story about servants who were given something to care for while their master was away. To the one who had been faithful with a little, the master said:

> "Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things: enter thou into the joy of thy lord." — Matthew 25:21 (KJV)

"A few things." That is what most of us have today: a few things, not very glamorous ones. And yet those few things, held faithfully, are exactly what the Lord calls good. The words at the end are not "well done, impressive servant," but "good and faithful." And the reward is not just more responsibility. It is joy — His joy, shared with you.

## For the rest of today

Before you go back to the list, take a breath and offer Him the next small thing. Not the whole afternoon, just the next task. Do it heartily, as to Him. Then offer Him the one after that.

You might pray something like this:

*Lord Jesus, You worked with Your hands and You see my ordinary hours. Help me to be faithful in the least things today — honest, patient, and kind where no one is watching. Let this afternoon be done for You. Amen.*

The middle of the day is not empty ground. He is here in it, and the little things are His too.
