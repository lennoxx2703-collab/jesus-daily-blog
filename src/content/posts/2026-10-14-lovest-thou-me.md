Evenings have a way of replaying the day. The sharp word we wish we could take back. The moment we stayed quiet when we should have spoken. The promise to God we made this morning and quietly broke by lunch. When the house goes still, failure can get loud.

Peter knew that feeling better than most. Only days before, he had sworn he would never leave Jesus. Then, warming himself by a charcoal fire in the high priest's courtyard, he denied Him three times. When the rooster crowed, Peter went out and wept.

## Another fire

After the resurrection, Peter goes back to the one thing he knows: fishing. All night, nothing. At dawn a figure on the shore tells them where to cast, the net fills, and John says, "It is the Lord." Peter throws himself into the water.

> "As soon then as they were come to land, they saw a fire of coals there, and fish laid thereon, and bread." (John 21:9, KJV)

A fire of coals. The same kind of fire where Peter had failed. Jesus does not avoid the place of the wound. He builds a fire there, cooks breakfast, and invites His friend to sit down.

## The question

> "So when they had dined, Jesus saith to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs." (John 21:15, KJV)

Notice what Jesus does not say. He does not ask, "Why did you do it?" He does not make Peter recount every denial or promise to do better. He asks about love.

Three denials; three questions. Each one gives Peter a chance to say out loud what his failure had seemed to cancel. By the third time it hurts:

> "Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time, Lovest thou me? And he said unto him, Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee. Jesus saith unto him, Feed my sheep." (John 21:17, KJV)

"Thou knowest all things." Peter has stopped trusting his own big promises. He simply rests on what Jesus sees in him. And Jesus answers not with probation, but with a calling. *Feed my sheep.* The man who failed is the man He sends.

## What this means tonight

Maybe you are carrying something from today, or from long ago, that still sits heavy when the lights go out. You may half expect that when you come to Jesus, He will open a file and read you the charges.

But this is what He is like. He meets you at the very spot where you fell. He feeds you before He says a word about anything else. And then He asks the one question that matters: *Do you love me?*

You don't need a polished answer. Peter's best answer was "Lord, you know." That is enough. Jesus already knows the failure. He also knows the love underneath it, weak as it may feel, and He is not finished with you.

Restoration in His hands is never a demotion. He does not merely let Peter back in; He trusts him with the people He loves most. Whatever you bring Him tonight, He can still use you tomorrow.

## A prayer for the evening

*Lord Jesus, You know where I fell today. Thank You that You don't avoid those places, but come and build a fire there. I can't promise You perfection. But You know all things; You know that I love You. Restore me while I sleep, and in the morning, give me something of Yours to care for. Amen.*
