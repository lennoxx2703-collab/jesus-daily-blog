Saturday evening has its own quiet. The week is behind you, and Sunday hasn't started yet. It's the kind of in-between hour when old thoughts drift back in. Things you said this week. Things you didn't do. The slow, nagging question underneath them all: *Would God really still want me, as I am, tonight?*

Jesus answered that question once, in a sentence so simple it's easy to read past.

> "All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out." (John 6:37, KJV)

## Said to a crowd that was about to leave

The setting matters. Jesus had fed five thousand people the day before. Now the crowd had followed Him across the lake, and most of them wanted more bread, not Him. He told them plainly that He Himself was the bread of life. By the end of the chapter, many of them would turn back and walk with Him no more (John 6:66).

And right in the middle of that crowd, full of mixed motives and half-hearted seekers, He makes this promise. Not to the spiritual elite. Not to people who had their reasons sorted out. To ordinary, hungry, confused people. Whoever comes, He will not throw out.

## "In no wise"

The old English is beautiful here. *In no wise* means in no way, under no circumstances, not for any reason. The Greek doubles the negative for emphasis, something like "I will never, never cast out."

Think about the reasons we expect to be turned away. *I came to Him before and fell back. I only come when I'm in trouble. My faith is weak. My prayers are clumsy. I don't even know what I'm asking for.* Jesus doesn't list exceptions. He doesn't say, "Whoever comes the right way," or "Whoever comes with clean hands." He says whoever comes.

That covers the person who comes running, and the person who barely shuffles in. It covers the first-timer and the one coming back for the hundredth time.

## The coming is enough

Notice what He asks of us: only to come. Not to fix ourselves first. Not to earn a seat. Coming is just turning toward Him, the way the prodigal turned toward home with his speech half-rehearsed, or the way a tired child walks into a room where a parent is waiting.

And notice the first half of the verse, too. *All that the Father giveth me shall come to me.* Your coming isn't only your idea. The Father has been drawing you (John 6:44). So when you feel that small pull toward prayer tonight, that's not you bothering God. That's God inviting you.

## For tonight

Before you sleep, try coming to Him in the simplest way. You could say something like this:

*Lord Jesus, I'm coming to You. Not because I've got it all together, but because You said You wouldn't send me away. Here is my week. Here is my heart. I'm Yours.*

Then rest in what He promised. He doesn't take it back in the night. Tomorrow is the Lord's day, and you get to walk into it as someone already welcomed.

You have not worn out your welcome. In no wise will He cast you out.
