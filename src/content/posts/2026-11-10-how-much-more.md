Some of us pray the way we'd ask a busy stranger for directions. Quickly, apologetically, already half expecting to be brushed off. We keep our requests small so the disappointment will be small too. *Lord, if it's not too much trouble... only if You want to... never mind, I'll figure it out.*

Jesus heard that kind of praying all around Him, and on the hillside in Galilee He answered it with a picture every person in the crowd could see:

> *Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you: for every one that asketh receiveth; and he that seeketh findeth; and to him that knocketh it shall be opened. Or what man is there of you, whom if his son ask bread, will he give him a stone? Or if he ask a fish, will he give him a serpent? If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children, how much more shall your Father which is in heaven give good things to them that ask him?*
> — Matthew 7:7–11 (KJV)

## Bread, not a stone

Picture the scene Jesus paints. A child comes in from playing, hungry, and says, "Can I have some bread?" What father hands that child a rock? Or a snake instead of a fish? The question is almost funny. Nobody in the crowd had to think about it.

Even ordinary parents, tired, distracted, flawed, the ones Jesus bluntly calls "evil" compared with God, know how to feed a hungry kid. Most of us would give the bread before the sentence was finished.

That is the whole argument, and it rests on two small words: *how much more.* If a tired human parent can be trusted with bread, how much more the Father who never sleeps, never runs out, and never misreads His children?

## Ask like a child, not a stranger

Jesus is not handing us a technique for getting whatever we want. He is showing us who we are talking to. Prayer changes when you remember that you are not filling out a form for a distant office. You are a son or a daughter walking into the kitchen.

Children don't rehearse speeches before they ask for breakfast. They just ask. They ask again. They knock on the door with both fists when it's shut. Jesus says that is exactly the posture He wants: keep asking, keep seeking, keep knocking. Not because the Father is reluctant, but because asking keeps us close to Him.

## When the answer doesn't look like bread

Maybe you've asked and what came back felt more like a stone. A door stayed closed. A prayer you prayed for years is still waiting. Jesus doesn't pretend that never happens. But He does tell us what our Father is *like*: He gives "good things." Sometimes what we call bread would have hurt us, and sometimes the good thing He is giving is Himself, in the waiting.

James would later put it this way: *"Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning"* (James 1:17, KJV). His goodness doesn't change with the weather of your week.

## For this Tuesday

So before the day picks up speed, try asking plainly. Name the thing. The bill, the diagnosis, the friend who's drifting, the habit you can't shake, the peace you can't find. Don't shrink it to make it sound more reasonable. Hand it to Him the way a child holds out an empty hand.

Then walk into your day as someone who has a Father. Not an employer to impress, not a judge to appease, but a Father who already knows how to give good gifts, and who gave the best one when He gave us Jesus.

*Father, I come to You as Your child this morning. Here is what I need. Thank You that You hear me, that You are good, and that You will not hand me a stone. Teach me to trust You for the bread, and to trust You even more when I can't see it yet. In Jesus' name, amen.*
