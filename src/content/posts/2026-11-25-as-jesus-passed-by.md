Somewhere around the middle of the day, the questions get louder. The morning had its energy; now you're in the thick of it, and whatever is hard in your life is sitting right there with you. A diagnosis. A child who's struggling. A limitation you didn't choose and can't fix. And underneath it, a quiet, nagging question: *Why? Whose fault is this? Did I do something to deserve it?*

Jesus' own friends asked that question once. Watch what He does with it.

## He saw

> "And as Jesus passed by, he saw a man which was blind from his birth."
> — John 9:1 (KJV)

It's such a small sentence that it's easy to skip. But read it slowly. Jesus was *passing by*. He was on His way somewhere, in a busy city, with a crowd that had just tried to stone Him (John 8:59). And still, He *saw* him.

This man had probably been sitting in the same spot for years. People walked past him every day. He had become part of the scenery — the blind beggar by the road. Most people stopped seeing him a long time ago.

Jesus didn't. If you feel like part of the scenery today — overlooked, explained, filed away — hear that first: as Jesus passes by, He sees you.

## The wrong question

> "And his disciples asked him, saying, Master, who did sin, this man, or his parents, that he was born blind?"
> — John 9:2 (KJV)

The disciples look at the same man and see a theology problem. Somebody must be to blame. Either he sinned, or his parents did. Pick one.

We still do this — to others, and especially to ourselves. When something breaks, we go looking for the guilty party, because blame feels like control. If I can find the reason, at least the pain makes sense.

Jesus refuses both options:

> "Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents: but that the works of God should be made manifest in him."
> — John 9:3 (KJV)

He doesn't give a lecture on why suffering exists. He doesn't pretend the blindness is good. He simply turns the whole conversation around — from *who caused this?* to *what will God do here?* The disciples were looking backward for a culprit. Jesus looks forward for the Father's work.

That doesn't mean every hard thing in your life will be explained this side of heaven. It does mean your pain is not a verdict on you. It's not God's way of settling a score. It's a place where He intends to show up.

## While it is day

> "I must work the works of him that sent me, while it is day: the night cometh, when no man can work. As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world."
> — John 9:4–5 (KJV)

There's something fitting about reading this at midday. The sun is high. Jesus says, in effect: *This is the time for mercy. Not for debating — for doing.*

And then He does something wonderfully earthy. He spits on the ground, makes mud, and spreads it on the man's eyes (John 9:6). The Light of the world gets His hands dirty. He doesn't heal from a distance or with a tidy word. He touches the man's face.

## Go, wash

> "And said unto him, Go, wash in the pool of Siloam, (which is by interpretation, Sent.) He went his way therefore, and washed, and came seeing."
> — John 9:7 (KJV)

Notice the man still had to walk — blind, with mud on his face, across the city to the pool. He didn't see anything until he obeyed. Sometimes that's how it goes with us too: Jesus speaks, and we take the next step in the dark, trusting the One who sent us.

Later, when the religious leaders grill him about what happened, the man doesn't have all the answers. He just says, *"One thing I know, that, whereas I was blind, now I see"* (John 9:25, KJV). That's enough of a testimony for anybody.

## For this midday

If you've been asking *whose fault is this?* — about your own story or someone else's — try setting that question down for a minute. Pick up a different one: *Lord, what do You want to do here?*

And look up from your desk or your phone long enough to *see* someone today the way Jesus saw that man. Somebody everyone else has stopped noticing.

*Lord Jesus, You saw a man nobody saw anymore. See me in the middle of this day. Take my questions about blame and turn them toward Your mercy. Put Your hands on what's broken, even if it's messy. And give me the courage to take the next step You tell me to take, even before I can see. Amen.*

He's passing by right now. And He sees you.
