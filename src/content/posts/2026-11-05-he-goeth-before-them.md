Thursday mornings don't usually feel holy. The week is already in motion. Coffee, keys, the route you know by heart, a list that started forming before your feet hit the floor. Nothing dramatic is waiting for most of us — just the next stretch of ordinary work, ordinary people, ordinary faithfulness.

John tells us that is exactly the kind of day Jesus leads His people into.

> "To him the porter openeth; and the sheep hear his voice: and he calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out. And when he putteth forth his own sheep, he goeth before them, and the sheep follow him: for they know his voice." (John 10:3–4, KJV)

## By name

Before anything else, notice how the day begins for the sheep. Not with a whistle for the flock as a blur. Not with a general announcement. *He calleth his own sheep by name.*

Your name. The one your parents gave you. The one your friends use. The one you answer to when someone needs you. Jesus knows it, and He uses it. That means this Thursday is not anonymous to Him. You are not "one of the Christians" filing into another weekday. You are someone He can call without checking a list.

If you woke up feeling interchangeable — another face on the commute, another inbox, another set of hands at a job that would keep going without you — let this land. The Shepherd does not lead a crowd. He leads *His own*, and He knows which one is you.

## Leadeth them out

Then He does something practical: He leads them out. Out of the fold. Out into the open. Out toward the day's pasture, which for a sheep means walking, eating, staying close, and trusting the path.

That is a kind word for a Thursday. Following Jesus is not only for Sunday mornings and quiet corners. It is also for leaving the house. For walking into the office, the classroom, the kitchen, the hospital ward, the inbox that never quite empties. He leads His sheep *out*. The Christian life is not meant to stay locked inside a private room forever. Prayer gathers us; then love sends us.

So when you step into today, you are not improvising alone. You are being led out by Someone who already knows the ground.

## He goeth before them

The line I keep coming back to is this: *"he goeth before them."*

He doesn't send the sheep ahead to scout the day and report back. He doesn't shout instructions from behind the fence. He goes first. Into the weather. Into the unknown stretch of hours. Into the conversation you're nervous about, the task that feels too big, the quiet grief that still sits in the passenger seat.

That changes how a Thursday feels. You are not walking into a day Jesus hasn't entered. Wherever you are headed this morning, He has already stepped onto that road. The sheep's job is simpler than we usually make it: stay close enough to hear His voice, and follow.

You don't have to see the whole week from here. Sheep rarely do. They only need to recognize the Shepherd who is already ahead of them.

## They know his voice

John says the sheep follow "for they know his voice." Recognition comes from relationship. The more we listen — in Scripture, in quiet prayer, in the small obedience of an ordinary hour — the easier it gets to tell His voice from the louder ones: fear, hurry, comparison, the old accusation that you're behind and alone.

This morning, before the noise thickens, give Him a minute. Hear your name spoken in love. Let Him lead you out. Trust that He goes before you into whatever Thursday holds.

"The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want. He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters." (Psalm 23:1–2, KJV)
