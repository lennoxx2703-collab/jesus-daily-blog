Sunday mornings can go two ways. Some feel like an open door: slow coffee, a little space, maybe church, maybe a walk. Others fill up before you're even awake, with errands that didn't fit into Saturday and a quiet list of things you really should get to.

Jesus once told a story about a meal that people were too busy to attend. It started at a dinner party of His own. Someone at the table, trying to sound pious, said, "Blessed is he that shall eat bread in the kingdom of God" (Luke 14:15). Jesus answered with a story.

> "A certain man made a great supper, and bade many: and sent his servant at supper time to say to them that were bidden, Come; for all things are now ready." (Luke 14:16–17, KJV)

Notice what the host says. Not "come and help," not "come and bring something." Just: *Come. Everything is ready.* The work is done. The table is set. The only thing missing is the guests.

## Very reasonable excuses

> "And they all with one consent began to make excuse. The first said unto him, I have bought a piece of ground, and I must needs go and see it: I pray thee have me excused. And another said, I have bought five yoke of oxen, and I go to prove them: I pray thee have me excused. And another said, I have married a wife, and therefore I cannot come." (Luke 14:18–20, KJV)

What strikes me is how *normal* these are. Nobody says, "I'd rather do something wicked." Land, work animals, a new marriage. These are good things. Responsible things. The kind of reasons we'd accept from a friend without a second thought.

That's exactly the point. Most of us don't drift from Jesus through rebellion. We drift through good things that keep arriving first. The house, the job, the family, the inbox. None of them are wrong. They just quietly take the seat that was meant for Him.

And notice how polite it all is: *I pray thee have me excused.* Not a no, exactly. Just a "not now."

## The house fills with the unlikely

The host is grieved, and then he does something wonderful. He sends the servant out "into the streets and lanes of the city," to bring in "the poor, and the maimed, and the halt, and the blind" (Luke 14:21). People who never get invited anywhere. People who couldn't repay a dinner if they tried.

Then comes my favorite line in the whole story:

> "And the servant said, Lord, it is done as thou hast commanded, and yet there is room." (Luke 14:22, KJV)

*And yet there is room.* The servant has already brought in a crowd, and the table still isn't full. So the host sends him farther, out to the highways and hedges, "that my house may be filled" (Luke 14:23).

That's the heart of God in one sentence. He isn't hoping for a small, exclusive gathering. He wants a full house. And He's especially glad to fill it with people who assumed the invitation wasn't for them.

If you've been feeling like the leftover guest, the one who's too tired or too messy or too late, hear this: there is still room. There was room for the blind and the lame, and there was room for strangers from the hedges. There's room for you this morning.

## For your Sunday

You don't have to clear your whole life before you come. The host said everything was already ready. So maybe try this today:

- **Name your "five yoke of oxen."** What good thing usually gets to go first? Just notice it. No guilt, only honesty.
- **Give Him the first seat.** Before the errands start, sit for five minutes and simply say, "Jesus, I'm here. I came."
- **Look for someone in the lanes.** Who around you rarely gets invited? A message, a coffee, a chair at your table can carry His invitation further.

The supper is ready. The door is open. And yet there is room.
