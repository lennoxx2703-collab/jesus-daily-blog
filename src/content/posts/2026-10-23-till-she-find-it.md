Most of us know the feeling of losing something small in the house. Keys, a ring, the one paper you actually needed. You check the obvious places, then the less obvious ones, and somewhere along the way your whole morning narrows down to that one missing thing.

Jesus told a story about exactly that feeling.

> "Either what woman having ten pieces of silver, if she lose one piece, doth not light a candle, and sweep the house, and seek diligently till she find it? And when she hath found it, she calleth her friends and her neighbours together, saying, Rejoice with me; for I have found the piece which I had lost." (Luke 15:8–9, KJV)

It's one of the shortest parables in the Gospels, tucked between the lost sheep and the lost son. It's easy to skim past. But it's worth slowing down for, because of who Jesus told it to.

## Why He told it

Luke sets the scene a few verses earlier: "Then drew near unto him all the publicans and sinners for to hear him. And the Pharisees and scribes murmured, saying, This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them" (Luke 15:1–2).

So the parable is an answer to a complaint. Why does Jesus spend His time with people like that? His answer, in effect, is: because they're Mine, and they were lost, and that's what you do when you lose something that matters. You go looking.

## A lamp and a broom

Picture the house. A small, stone home with one low door and maybe one small window. Dim even in daytime. A packed earth floor, probably with straw or dust on it. A silver coin could roll into a crack and simply disappear.

So she does three ordinary things. She lights a lamp. She sweeps the house. She searches carefully. Nothing dramatic, just patient, determined attention, corner by corner.

And notice the little phrase at the end: *till she find it.* She doesn't sweep for a few minutes and decide nine coins is good enough. Ninety percent isn't a passing grade when it comes to the people God loves. She keeps going until that one coin is back in her hand.

That's the picture Jesus chose to show us God's heart. Not a God who waits with folded arms for the lost to find their own way back, but One who lights the lamp, picks up the broom, and gets down on the floor.

## What a coin can't do

There's something tender about the fact that this time the lost thing is a coin. A sheep can at least bleat. A son can come to his senses and walk home. A coin can't do anything. It doesn't know it's lost. It can't call out, can't climb out of the crack, can't make itself shine brighter so it's easier to spot.

Some mornings that's exactly how it feels to be us. Too tired to pray well. Too tangled up to know how we got here. Not even sure we want to be found.

The parable says that's not the end of the story. The finding depends on the One who is searching. Jesus said it plainly another time: "For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost" (Luke 19:10). You don't have to manufacture the shine. You only have to let yourself be picked up.

## The party at the end

And then, joy. She calls her friends and neighbours together. It's a little over the top, really, a gathering for a single coin. But that's the point. Jesus finishes: "Likewise, I say unto you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner that repenteth" (Luke 15:10).

One. Not a crowd, not a revival, not a perfect week. One person turning toward Him is enough to make heaven celebrate.

## For this Friday

Maybe you're coming to the end of a week that scattered you a bit. Maybe there's a part of your life that's rolled into a dark corner and you've stopped looking for it yourself. Maybe someone you love feels very far from God right now.

Here's a small way to carry this story into the day:

- **Let yourself be found.** Before the day picks up speed, say something simple like, "Lord, here I am. Find me in the corners I've been avoiding."
- **Pray for one person by name.** Someone you'd love to see come home. Ask Jesus to keep sweeping until He finds them, and trust that He's more diligent than you are.
- **Look for the joy.** If you see even a small sign of someone turning toward God today, yourself included, take a moment to rejoice. Heaven is.

The lamp is already lit. The broom is already moving. And the One searching for you doesn't stop at nine.
