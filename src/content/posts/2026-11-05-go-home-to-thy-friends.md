Midday on a Thursday has a way of pulling you back into the same rooms. The same desk. The same people who knew you before anything changed. After a quiet morning with God, the afternoon can feel like a return to ordinary ground — and sometimes that feels like a step down.

Mark tells a story for that exact feeling.

> "And when he was come into the ship, he that had been possessed with the devil prayed him that he might be with him. Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him, Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee. And he departed, and began to publish in Decapolis how great things Jesus had done for him: and all men did marvel." (Mark 5:18–20, KJV)

### He wanted the boat

This man had been living among the tombs — cut off, feared, uncontrollable. Then Jesus stepped onto his shore, spoke a word, and the storm inside him stopped. Clothes. Sanity. A future. Of course he wanted to get in the boat. Of course he prayed that he might be *with Him*. After a rescue like that, who wouldn't want to leave the old place behind and travel with the Rescuer?

You may know that prayer. *Lord, take me somewhere else. Let me stay close in the easy sense — away from the people who remember the old me, away from the job that still looks the same, away from the house where nothing dramatic seems to be happening.* Following Jesus can sound, at first, like an exit.

### Jesus suffered him not

Then comes the surprise. "Howbeit Jesus suffered him not."

Jesus said no to the request that sounded most spiritual. Not because He didn't love the man. Because He had another assignment that love required. Get in the boat with Me, the man begged. Go home to thy friends, Jesus answered.

That is not a smaller calling. It is a harder one. The boat would have been adventure. Home meant facing people who had chained him, avoided him, talked about him. Home meant walking back into a town that still smelled like the old story — and speaking a new one.

If this Thursday afternoon feels less like a mountain and more like a kitchen table, a school hallway, a screen full of familiar names, hear Him. He is not always calling you out of your life. Often He is sending you deeper into it, changed.

### Tell them

Notice what Jesus gives him to say. Not a speech. Not a polished testimony with three points. "Tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee."

Two notes in that sentence: what God *did*, and what God *felt*. The facts of mercy, and the heart behind it. You don't have to explain everything. You don't have to sound impressive. You only have to tell the truth about the kindness that found you.

And look where he is sent: *to thy friends*. Not first to strangers on a stage. To the people who already know your name. The ones who saw the worst chapters. The ones who will notice if something is different at lunch, in the meeting, in the way you speak when you're tired.

That is midday discipleship. Not escaping the ordinary circle — carrying Jesus into it.

### He departed

Mark says the man did what Jesus said. He went. He began to publish in Decapolis — ten cities' worth of ordinary roads — how great things Jesus had done for him. And people marveled.

He never got the boat ride he asked for. He got something better: a life that pointed to Jesus right where he had once been lost.

So this afternoon, if you feel the pull to disappear into something more "spiritual" than your actual Thursday, pause. The One who set you free may be saying the same quiet word He said by the sea: Go home to thy friends. Tell them what He has done. Tell them He had compassion on you. The boat can wait. The people in front of you cannot.

> "Come and hear, all ye that fear God, and I will declare what he hath done for my soul." (Psalm 66:16, KJV)
