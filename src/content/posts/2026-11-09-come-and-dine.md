The first workday of the week has a particular kind of tiredness. Monday asks for everything at once: the inbox that filled up over the weekend, the plans that suddenly feel heavier than they did on Sunday, the people who need something from you before you have had a chance to find your feet. By evening you may feel like you worked hard and have surprisingly little to show for it.

There is a morning in the Gospel of John that knows exactly that feeling.

## A night of nothing

After the resurrection, Peter and a handful of the other disciples go back to the lake. Peter says simply, "I go a fishing," and the others go with him. It is what they know. It is what their hands remember. But John tells us plainly: "that night they caught nothing" (John 21:3, KJV).

All night on the water. Nets thrown and hauled in, thrown and hauled in. Wet, cold, sore, and empty.

As morning comes, a figure stands on the shore and calls out, "Children, have ye any meat?" And they have to admit it: "No" (John 21:5). Then the stranger tells them where to cast the net, and suddenly it is so full they cannot draw it in. That is when John turns to Peter and says, "It is the Lord."

## The fire was already burning

Here is the part that I want to sit with tonight.

"As soon then as they were come to land, they saw a fire of coals there, and fish laid thereon, and bread" (John 21:9, KJV).

Before they had caught anything, Jesus had already made breakfast. The fire was lit. The fish was cooking. The bread was ready. He was not waiting on shore for them to earn a meal. He was waiting to feed them.

Then He says something wonderfully gentle: "Bring of the fish which ye have now caught" (John 21:10). He lets them add what they have to what He has already prepared. Their work is welcome at the fire. But it was never the thing that made the meal possible.

And then the invitation: "Come and dine" (John 21:12, KJV).

John adds that "Jesus then cometh, and taketh bread, and giveth them, and fish likewise" (John 21:13). The risen Lord of all creation, the One who had just conquered death, stands by a little charcoal fire and hands tired fishermen their breakfast with His own scarred hands.

## What He does with an empty night

Peter in particular had reasons to stay away from that fire. Only days earlier he had stood by another fire of coals in a courtyard and denied Jesus three times. He knew what failure smelled like. And now here was a fire again, with Jesus beside it.

But Jesus did not start with a lecture. He started with food. Restoration would come in the conversation that followed, but first came warmth, bread, and a place at the table.

That is still how He meets us. When your day felt like a long night of casting nets into empty water, Jesus is not standing on the shore with a list of what you should have done better. He is standing beside a fire He lit before you even got there. "Thou preparest a table before me," David wrote (Psalm 23:5, KJV), and the Good Shepherd keeps doing exactly that.

## Tonight

So before you close this Monday, try a small practice.

Name the empty nets. Tell Him honestly where the day came up short, the way the disciples answered, "No." He already knows, and He asked anyway, because He wants you to say it to Him.

Bring what you did catch. Some things did go well today, even small ones: a kind word, a finished task, a moment of patience. Lay those at the fire too. He welcomes them.

Then hear the invitation. "Come and dine." Receive His peace as a gift instead of a wage. Let Him feed you with His presence before you sleep, and trust that tomorrow's nets are in His hands as well.

The fire is already burning. The bread is already warm. Come and sit with Him a while.
