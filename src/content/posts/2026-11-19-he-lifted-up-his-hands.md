Before the day gets loud, there's often a quiet question sitting underneath everything else: *Where do I stand with God this morning?*

Maybe yesterday didn't go well. Maybe you said something you regret, or skipped the prayer you meant to pray, or just feel far away. It's easy to walk into a new day half-expecting a frown from heaven.

So let's begin with the very last picture Luke gives us of Jesus on earth.

> "And he led them out as far as to Bethany, and he lifted up his hands, and blessed them. And it came to pass, while he blessed them, he was parted from them, and carried up into heaven. And they worshipped him, and returned to Jerusalem with great joy: and were continually in the temple, praising and blessing God. Amen."
> — Luke 24:50–53 (KJV)

## The last thing they saw

Think about who was standing on that hillside. These were the men who had fallen asleep in Gethsemane. Who had run when the soldiers came. One of them had sworn he never knew Him. Not one of them had a spotless record from that week.

And Jesus didn't leave them with a lecture. He didn't leave them with a list of everything they'd gotten wrong. He *led them out*, lifted up His hands — hands that still bore the marks of the nails — and blessed them.

That was the final posture they saw. Not arms crossed. Arms raised over them.

## He's still blessing

Notice the little phrase in the middle: "*while* he blessed them, he was parted from them." He didn't finish the blessing and then leave. He was taken up *in the middle* of it. In a real sense, the blessing never stopped — it just moved to heaven.

That's exactly what the rest of the New Testament tells us:

> "Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them."
> — Hebrews 7:25 (KJV)

*Ever liveth.* Right now, as you're waking up, rubbing your eyes, checking your phone — the risen Jesus is not done speaking good over you. His hands are still lifted.

For anyone who knew the Old Testament, those raised hands would have brought to mind the old priestly blessing:

> "The LORD bless thee, and keep thee: the LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: the LORD lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace."
> — Numbers 6:24–26 (KJV)

Jesus is our great High Priest, and this is the face He turns toward you: shining, gracious, peace-giving.

## Why they went home happy

Here's the part that surprises me every time. Jesus *leaves* — and they return "with great joy."

You'd expect tears. Their Teacher, their Friend, was gone from sight. But they had just been blessed by Him, and they knew He was alive and reigning, and He had promised that the Spirit was coming. Being sent off under His blessing changed everything about how they walked back down that hill.

They went straight into ordinary days — the city, the temple, meals, prayers — and they filled those days with praise.

## For your morning

You don't have to earn your way into God's favor before breakfast. In Christ, you begin the day already standing under His raised hands.

So before the to-do list takes over, try this:

- **Receive it.** Read Numbers 6:24–26 slowly, and put your name in it. *The Lord bless you, ___, and keep you.*
- **Walk out under it.** Whatever this Thursday holds — work, kids, a hard conversation, a doctor's visit — you're carrying a blessing into it, not a verdict.
- **Answer with praise.** The disciples responded to His blessing by "praising and blessing God." Even one sentence of thanks on your way out the door is enough.

Lord Jesus, thank You that the last thing You did on earth was bless the people who had failed You. I'm one of them. Let me live today under Your lifted hands, with joy, and give You thanks all day long. Amen.
