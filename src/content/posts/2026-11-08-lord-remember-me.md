Sunday evening can feel like a quiet audit. The weekend is closing, the new week is lining up at the door, and somewhere in the back of your mind a voice starts adding things up: what you meant to do and didn't, the words you wish you could take back, the prayers you skipped. It is easy to end the day feeling like you have nothing to bring to God except a short, tired sentence.

That is exactly where one man found himself on the worst day of his life.

> "And he said unto Jesus, Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom. And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise." (Luke 23:42–43, KJV)

## A man with nothing left

He was hanging on a cross beside Jesus. He could not go back and fix his life. He could not make restitution, join a congregation, be baptized, or prove he had changed. His hands were nailed down. His time was measured in hours.

He did not pretend otherwise. Just before his prayer, he said to the other criminal, "we indeed justly; for we receive the due reward of our deeds: but this man hath done nothing amiss" (Luke 23:41, KJV). He was honest about himself and honest about Jesus. That was all he had — and it turned out to be enough to turn toward Him.

Then came the prayer. Not long. Not polished. Just: *Lord, remember me.*

## The answer was bigger than the request

He asked to be remembered someday, "when thou comest into thy kingdom." Jesus answered with a word that collapsed all the distance: *To day.* Not after a probation period. Not once the man had earned it. Today — with Me — in paradise.

Notice what Jesus did not say. He did not list the man's crimes. He did not say, "Too late." He did not ask for a résumé of good intentions. While He Himself was dying, carrying the weight of the world, He turned His face toward one more sinner and gave him a promise.

That is the heart of Jesus. He is not stingy with mercy. When we bring Him a small, honest prayer, He answers with Himself.

## For your Sunday evening

Maybe this weekend did not go the way you hoped. Maybe church felt distant this morning, or you didn't go at all. Maybe there's a regret you've been carrying since Friday, or since years ago. You may feel like you have very little to offer tonight.

You don't need much. The thief had less than you do. He had an honest confession and a three-word reach toward Jesus. Jesus did the rest.

So tonight, before the week begins, you can pray the same way:

*Lord, remember me.*

Remember me in what I couldn't fix today. Remember me in the places I'm ashamed of. Remember me in the week ahead, when I'll need You again. And then listen for His answer — the same answer He has been giving since that hill outside Jerusalem: *You will be with Me.*

His remembering is not like ours. When we remember someone, we think about them. When Jesus remembers you, He keeps you. He holds you. He brings you home.

## A quiet close

As the lights go down tonight, let the day's audit end. The account has been settled by the One who hung in the middle. You can rest, not because you finished everything, but because He did.

> "I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety." (Psalm 4:8, KJV)

Lord Jesus, remember me tonight. Thank You that You already have. Amen.
