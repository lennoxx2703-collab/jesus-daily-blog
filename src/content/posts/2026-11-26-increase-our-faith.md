There's a particular kind of prayer that comes out around the middle of the day. It's not eloquent. It usually happens somewhere between one task and the next, when you realize the thing in front of you is bigger than what you've got. A conversation you're dreading. A responsibility that won't let up. A quiet ache you keep carrying into every room. And the prayer is short: *God, I need more. More strength. More patience. More faith.*

The apostles prayed that exact prayer once.

> "And the apostles said unto the Lord, Increase our faith. And the Lord said, If ye had faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye might say unto this sycamine tree, Be thou plucked up by the root, and be thou planted in the sea; and it should obey you."
> — Luke 17:5–6 (KJV)

## Why they asked

Jesus had just told them something hard. Not a miracle to perform — a way to live. Watch yourselves. Deal honestly with people. And when someone keeps coming back to you, keep making room for them. It was the kind of instruction that sounds simple on paper and feels impossible by lunchtime.

So the disciples did what most of us would do. They looked at the size of the job, looked at the size of their hearts, and said, *We're going to need a bigger supply.*

It's a good prayer. It's an honest one. And Jesus doesn't scold them for it. But His answer goes somewhere they didn't expect.

## Not more — just real

Jesus doesn't say, "Yes, here's a larger portion." He talks about a mustard seed — a speck you could lose in the lines of your palm. If you had faith *that* small, He says, you could tell a deep-rooted tree to pull itself up and replant in the sea.

The point isn't that the disciples need to grow a giant faith before anything can happen. The point is that the power was never in the size of their faith. It was in the One their faith was pointed at.

A tiny seed of trust in a great God moves more than an enormous pile of confidence in yourself. Faith isn't a muscle you flex until the tree gives up. It's a hand, opened toward Someone who can actually move trees.

That changes the question. Instead of *"Do I have enough faith for this?"* you get to ask, *"Is the One I'm trusting enough for this?"* And that question has a much better answer.

## Faith looks like the next faithful thing

Right after this, Jesus tells a short story about a servant who comes in from the field and simply keeps serving — not because he's a hero, but because that's the work in front of him. He ends it with these words:

> "So likewise ye, when ye shall have done all those things which are commanded you, say, We are unprofitable servants: we have done that which was our duty to do."
> — Luke 17:10 (KJV)

It's an unusual follow-up to a conversation about faith. But it tells you something important: mustard-seed faith mostly doesn't look like dramatic moments. It looks like doing the next faithful thing, in the middle of an ordinary day, without waiting to feel spiritually impressive first.

You make the phone call. You answer kindly when you'd rather not. You open your Bible for five minutes even though your mind is still racing. You let go of the thing you've rehearsed resenting. None of it feels like moving trees. But that's how roots start to loosen.

## For the rest of your day

Maybe you're halfway through today feeling like your faith is about the size of a crumb. Good news: Jesus already said a crumb-sized seed is enough to work with, because He is the one who does the moving.

So pray the disciples' prayer — *Lord, increase our faith* — and mean it. He loves that prayer. But don't wait for the answer before you take the next step. Point the little faith you have toward Him, and do the next faithful thing.

The tree in front of you is not bigger than He is.

*Lord Jesus, my faith feels small today. Thank You that You aren't. Take the little I have, and help me trust You with the next hour — and the next faithful step. Amen.*
