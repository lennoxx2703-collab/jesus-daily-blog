Monday mornings have a way of handing us everything at once. The inbox, the calendar, the bills, the worry that slept beside you all weekend and is still there when the alarm goes off. Maybe it's a child. Maybe it's a diagnosis, a job, a marriage, a friend who's drifting. You prayed about it. You're still praying. And now you have to get up and walk into the week without knowing how it ends.

There's a man in John's Gospel who knows exactly how that feels.

## A father at the end of himself

He's called "a certain nobleman" — someone with standing, probably connected to Herod's court in Capernaum. The kind of man people step aside for. But none of that matters now, because his son is dying.

So he does what desperate parents do. He hears Jesus is in Cana, about a day's walk away, and he goes. He doesn't send a servant. He goes himself, and he begs:

> "Sir, come down ere my child die." (John 4:49, KJV)

He has a plan. Jesus must come with him, stand in that room, lay hands on that boy. That is what healing looks like to him.

## A word instead of a visit

Jesus doesn't go. He says something else:

> "Go thy way; thy son liveth. And the man believed the word that Jesus had spoken unto him, and he went his way." (John 4:50, KJV)

Think about what that asked of him. No miracle he could see. No sign. No Jesus walking beside him on the road. Just a sentence. *Go thy way; thy son liveth.* And he has to turn around and walk all the way home holding nothing but that.

I love that John says it plainly: *the man believed the word.* Not the evidence — there wasn't any yet. The word. He let what Jesus said be more solid than what he feared.

That's a lot of what faith looks like on an ordinary Monday. Not a feeling of certainty. Just turning toward the week with His word in your hand instead of the worry.

## Met on the road

Then comes one of my favorite little moments in the Gospels:

> "And as he was now going down, his servants met him, and told him, saying, Thy son liveth." (John 4:51, KJV)

Same words. *Thy son liveth.* The servants say exactly what Jesus said, without knowing it. The father asks when the boy started getting better, and they tell him: "Yesterday at the seventh hour the fever left him" (John 4:52, KJV).

> "So the father knew that it was at the same hour, in the which Jesus said unto him, Thy son liveth: and himself believed, and his whole house." (John 4:53, KJV)

The healing happened the moment Jesus spoke — miles away, out of sight. The father just didn't know it yet. He spent that whole road home walking on a promise that had *already* come true.

## What this means for your week

I can't promise you that whatever you're carrying will turn out the way you want by Friday. Jesus doesn't always answer the way we plan. The nobleman wanted a house call and got a sentence.

But here's what this story tells me about Him:

- **Distance doesn't limit Him.** He didn't need to be in the room. He isn't held back by where you are, or where the person you love is.
- **His word is enough to walk on.** You don't have to feel strong to obey "go thy way." You just have to go.
- **He is often working before you can see it.** Some of your prayers may already be answered on a road you haven't walked yet.

And notice how it ends: *himself believed, and his whole house.* The man who came for a healing went home with a faith his whole family shared. Jesus gave him more than he asked for — He gave him Himself.

## A prayer for this Monday

*Lord Jesus, You know what I'm carrying into this week. You know the name I keep bringing to You. I wanted You to come and fix it my way; help me trust Your word instead. Give me enough faith to go my way today, holding on to what You've said. And when I see You've been working all along, let my whole house believe with me. Amen.*

Go your way this morning. He's already ahead of you on the road.
