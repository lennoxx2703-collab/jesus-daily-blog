Saturday middays often look like errands. The shop, the market, the hardware store, the pile of things that didn't get done during the week. Somewhere between the shopping list and the bank balance, a quiet question tends to creep in: *will there be enough?* Enough money, enough time, enough energy for whatever next week asks.

Jesus knew that question well. He'd watched His friends carry it around Galilee. And in Luke 12, after talking about ravens that don't plant and lilies that don't spin, He says something tender and direct:

> "And seek not ye what ye shall eat, or what ye shall drink, neither be ye of doubtful mind. For all these things do the nations of the world seek after: and your Father knoweth that ye have need of these things." (Luke 12:29–30, KJV)

*Your Father knoweth.* Not "might eventually notice." He knows. The list in your pocket is not a secret from Him.

Then comes one of the gentlest sentences in all the Gospels:

> "Fear not, little flock; for it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom." (Luke 12:32, KJV)

Look at how Jesus speaks to them. *Little flock.* Not "my mighty army." Not "you who should know better." He names them as they actually are: small, a bit vulnerable, easily scattered, prone to worry. He doesn't scold them for being little. He just tells them not to be afraid while they're little.

And then He tells them why. Not because the circumstances are guaranteed to be easy. Not because they've earned it. Because of what the Father *enjoys.* It is His *good pleasure* to give. The word carries the idea of delight, of something He wants to do, not something He's been talked into.

That turns a lot of our quiet assumptions upside down. Many of us live as if God is reluctant, as if every good thing has to be pried out of His hands with enough prayer or enough good behaviour. Jesus says the opposite. The Father is not a tight-fisted landlord. He is a Father who is glad to give, and what He wants to give is not just bread for today but *the kingdom,* Himself, His reign, His presence, His life.

Notice the order, too. In the verse just before, Jesus says, "But rather seek ye the kingdom of God; and all these things shall be added unto you" (Luke 12:31, KJV). Then He reveals that the kingdom we're told to seek is the very thing the Father already intends to hand over. We're not chasing something that is running away from us. We're reaching for a gift held out.

So what does that do to a Saturday midday?

It doesn't make the errands vanish. You still need groceries. The bill still has a due date. But it can change the posture you carry them in. You can walk down the aisle as a sheep with a Shepherd, not as an orphan scrambling to survive. You can make the list, do what's yours to do, and then set the outcome down in the hands of Someone who already knows and is glad to give.

Jesus goes on: "For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also" (Luke 12:34, KJV). Fear tends to make us clutch. Trust frees our hands, sometimes even enough to be generous ourselves, because we're no longer convinced it all depends on us.

The prophet Isaiah saw this picture long before: "He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather the lambs with his arm, and carry them in his bosom" (Isaiah 40:11, KJV). That is who Jesus was describing. That is who is near you between the shop and the front door.

### A midday pause

Before you head to the next errand, try this:

- **Name the worry.** Say plainly what you're afraid there won't be enough of.
- **Hear His voice.** Read Luke 12:32 slowly, and put your name after "little flock."
- **Open your hands.** Literally, if you can. Tell Him: *You know what I need. Thank You that You're glad to give.*

You're small. That's alright. You're His. And He isn't holding back.
