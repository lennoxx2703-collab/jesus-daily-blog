By the middle of the week, a lot of us feel like we have very little left to give. The energy we had on Monday is gone. Our patience is thin, our prayers are short, and whatever we bring to God today feels small — maybe embarrassingly small.

There is a woman in the Gospel of Mark who knew exactly what that feels like. And Jesus was watching her.

> "And Jesus sat over against the treasury, and beheld how the people cast money into the treasury: and many that were rich cast in much. And there came a certain poor widow, and she threw in two mites, which make a farthing." (Mark 12:41–42, KJV)

## He sat down and watched

It is the last week of Jesus' earthly life. He has been teaching in the temple courts, answering hard questions, warning about religious leaders who "devour widows' houses" while making long prayers for show (Mark 12:40). Then He does something quiet. He sits down across from the place where offerings were collected and simply watches.

The rich came with their large gifts. People likely noticed them; large gifts tend to get noticed. Then a widow came — someone with no husband to protect her, in a world that gave women like her very little security. She dropped in two of the smallest coins in circulation. Together they were worth almost nothing.

No one in the crowd would have looked twice. But Jesus did.

## More than all of them

> "And he called unto him his disciples, and saith unto them, Verily I say unto you, That this poor widow hath cast more in, than all they which have cast into the treasury: For all they did cast in of their abundance; but she of her want did cast in all that she had, even all her living." (Mark 12:43–44, KJV)

Jesus calls His disciples over, as if to say, *Don't miss this.* By every visible measure, her gift was the smallest of the day. By heaven's measure, it was the largest.

He doesn't measure what lands in the box. He measures what it cost the heart that gave it. The rich gave out of what was left over. She gave out of what she needed. She held nothing back, and in doing so she was trusting God for the very next meal.

This is not a story about guilt, or about pressure to empty your bank account. Jesus had just condemned the people who took advantage of widows. It is a story about *being seen.* The Lord notices what no one else notices — the hidden faithfulness, the quiet trust, the offering that looks tiny from the outside and costs everything on the inside.

## Your two small coins

Maybe your two small coins today look like this:

- A short, tired prayer offered when you barely have words.
- Kindness to someone when you have nothing left yourself.
- Showing up to work, or to your family, faithfully, with no one saying thank you.
- Trusting God with a need you cannot meet on your own.

These things rarely make anyone's list of impressive gifts. But Jesus is still sitting across from the treasury. He sees. And He does not call it small.

## The One who gave everything

There is one more thing to notice. Only days after this moment, Jesus Himself would give "all that he had, even all his living" — His very life, poured out on the cross for us. The widow's gift points ahead to His. She gave her last two coins; He gave Himself.

So whatever you bring Him this afternoon, you bring it to Someone who has already given everything for you. He is not disappointed in the size of your offering. He is glad that you came.

## A midday prayer

*Lord Jesus, thank You for seeing the small and hidden things. Some days I have so little to bring You. Take my two small coins — my tired faith, my quiet obedience, my need — and receive them as an offering of love. Teach me to trust You with all I have, because You gave all You had for me. Amen.*
