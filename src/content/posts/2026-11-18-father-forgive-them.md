Somewhere around the middle of the day, an old hurt can come back without warning. A name pops up on your phone. A song plays. Someone says something that sounds like what *they* used to say. And suddenly you're replaying the conversation again, rehearsing what you should have said, feeling the sting like it was yesterday.

If that's you right now, take a short break with the hardest and most beautiful prayer Jesus ever prayed.

> "And when they were come to the place, which is called Calvary, there they crucified him... Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots." — Luke 23:33–34 (KJV)

## The timing of it

Read it slowly and notice *when* He says it. Not days later, after the wounds had healed and He'd had time to process. Not from a safe distance. He says it while the nails are fresh, while soldiers are gambling for His clothes at His feet, while the crowd is still mocking.

Most of us think of forgiveness as something we might get to eventually, once the pain fades. Jesus shows us it can begin right in the middle of the pain, as a prayer handed up to the Father. He doesn't pretend what they're doing is fine. He calls it what it is — something that needs forgiving. But He refuses to let it turn Him into someone who curses back.

## "They know not what they do"

That little phrase is so gracious. Did the soldiers know they were crucifying a man? Of course. Did the religious leaders know they'd plotted against Him? Yes. But none of them grasped what they were really doing — that they were nailing the Son of God to wood. Jesus looks past their cruelty to their blindness, and prays for them.

I'll be honest: I don't find it natural to see the people who hurt me that way. I usually assume they knew exactly what they were doing. Sometimes they did. But this prayer reminds me that every one of us — me included — has wounded others far more than we realize. And here's the part that undoes me: that prayer from the cross covers me too. Before I ever said sorry, before I even understood what my sin cost, He was already asking the Father to forgive me.

## Forgiven people, learning to forgive

Jesus had taught this long before Calvary: "Love your enemies, do good to them which hate you, bless them that curse you, and pray for them which despitefully use you" (Luke 6:27–28). On the cross, He simply did what He'd said. And His followers caught it — Stephen, as he was being stoned, prayed, "Lord, lay not this sin to their charge" (Acts 7:60).

Forgiveness isn't saying it didn't matter. It isn't necessarily trusting that person again or pretending the relationship is healthy. It's handing the debt to God and choosing not to carry the bill around with us anymore. Paul puts it simply: "even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye" (Colossians 3:13). We don't start from strength. We start from having been forgiven.

And it often takes more than one try. You may forgive at noon and feel the anger come back by evening. That's not failure. That's just bringing it to Him again.

## For the rest of today

If a face came to mind as you read this, you don't have to fix everything before dinner. Just try praying the way He did, even if your voice shakes:

*Father, You know what they did, and You know how much it hurt. I'm not able to let go of this on my own. But Jesus prayed for the people who nailed Him to the cross, and He prayed for me. So I'm asking You — forgive them. And help me to forgive too, a little more today than yesterday. Amen.*

Then take a breath, and go back to your afternoon a little lighter. The One who prayed from the cross is walking with you.
