Saturday evening is a strange in-between hour. The week's work is behind us, but often the weekend didn't turn out restful at all. The errands, the cleaning, the family plans, the catching up on everything we pushed aside since Monday. By the time the light goes, a lot of us realize we haven't actually stopped. We've only changed what we were busy with.

The disciples knew that feeling. Jesus had sent them out two by two, and they had been preaching, healing, walking from village to village. When they came back, they were full of stories, and the crowds were still pressing in. Mark tells us what happened next:

> "And the apostles gathered themselves together unto Jesus, and told him all things, both what they had done, and what they had taught. And he said unto them, Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while: for there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat. And they departed into a desert place by ship privately." (Mark 6:30–32, KJV)

## They told Him all things

Before the rest comes, there is the telling. The disciples gathered around Jesus and gave Him the whole report: what they had done and what they had taught. I imagine some of it was exciting and some of it was confusing. Some villages had welcomed them. Some had shut the door.

Jesus listened to all of it. That is a good way to end a week. Not by grading ourselves, not by replaying every mistake alone in the dark, but by bringing the whole of it to Him. *Here is what I did, Lord. Here is what I said. Here is what went well, and here is what I don't understand.* He is not impatient with the report. He wants to hear it.

## Rest was His idea

Notice who suggests the rest. Not the disciples. They might have kept going; the need around them was real, and there were people coming and going all the time. It was Jesus who looked at His tired friends and said, *come ye yourselves apart.*

Many of us carry a quiet guilt about stopping. There is always one more message to answer, one more thing to fix, one more person who might need us. But the One who loves people most of all is the one who said: come away and rest. Rest is not a reward we earn after everything is finished. Everything is never finished. Rest is an invitation from Him, given in the middle of the need.

*They had no leisure so much as to eat.* Jesus noticed that. He notices it in us too: the skipped meals, the short nights, the shoulders that never quite come down.

## Apart, with Him

The invitation isn't only "go rest." It is "come ye yourselves apart" — come with Me. The desert place was quiet, but it wasn't empty. Jesus was in the boat with them.

That is the difference between collapsing at the end of a day and resting with Jesus. Scrolling until we fall asleep is stopping, but it isn't always rest. The rest He gives is the kind David sang about: *"He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters. He restoreth my soul"* (Psalm 23:2–3, KJV). A shepherd who makes the sheep lie down, because the sheep won't always do it on their own.

## Rest a while

The phrase is gentle: *rest a while.* Not forever. Jesus knew the crowds would find them again, and in fact they did, and He fed five thousand of them that same day. Rest wasn't an escape from loving people. It was what made more love possible.

Tomorrow is Sunday. Tonight might be a good time to practice what Jesus offered His friends:

- **Tell Him all things.** Walk back through the week with Him, out loud or in your heart. The good and the hard.
- **Put one thing down.** Choose one task you will not touch tonight, and let that be an act of trust.
- **Come apart.** Find a few quiet minutes away from the noise. Turn off a screen. Sit with Him.
- **Let Him restore.** You don't have to make the rest productive. Just be with the Shepherd.

The week is in His hands now. The crowds can wait a little. Jesus is in the boat, and He is saying to you what He said to them: *come, and rest a while.*
