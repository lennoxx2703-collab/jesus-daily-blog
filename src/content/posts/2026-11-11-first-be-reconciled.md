Somewhere in the middle of the day, a name tends to surface. You are answering an email, or standing in line for lunch, and suddenly you remember the conversation that went wrong. The message you never answered. The thing you said that landed harder than you meant. The friend who has gone quiet since.

Most of us have a quiet shelf where we keep those names. We do not take them down very often. We just keep living, a little heavier than we need to.

Jesus has something to say about that shelf.

## Leave the gift there

> "Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift." — Matthew 5:23–24 (KJV)

Picture what He is describing. A man has travelled to the temple with an offering. Maybe it is a lamb he raised himself. He has waited in the crowd, he is finally at the altar, and this is the holy moment he came for. And right there, Jesus says, he remembers someone who has something against him.

You would expect Jesus to say, "Finish your offering, then deal with it later." Instead He says the opposite. Put the lamb down. Walk away from the altar. Go find your brother. Then come back.

That is a startling order of priorities. Jesus is not saying worship does not matter. He is saying God is not impressed by worship that steps around a broken relationship we could try to mend. The Father would rather wait for your gift than receive it from a heart that is pretending everything is fine.

## Notice who has the problem

Read it carefully. Jesus does not say, "If you have something against your brother." He says, "If thy brother hath ought against thee."

That is harder. It means the first move is not always about who was right. Sometimes the other person is hurt by something we barely noticed. Sometimes they are partly wrong too. Jesus still says, go. Do not wait for them to come to you. Take the first step yourself.

That is exactly what He did for us. "God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us" (Romans 5:8). We were the ones with the debt, and He crossed the distance anyway. When we go first toward someone else, we are simply doing a small version of what was done for us.

## What reconciliation is, and what it is not

Going to someone does not mean you can control how they respond. Paul put it honestly: "If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men" (Romans 12:18). *As much as lieth in you.* Some doors stay closed. Some relationships are not safe to reopen, and wisdom matters there.

But most of the names on our shelf are not like that. Most of them are just waiting for a short, humble sentence. "I've been thinking about what happened. I'm sorry for my part." "I miss you." "Can we talk?" It rarely takes a speech. It usually takes about thirty seconds of courage.

## A midday step

So here is a small invitation for the middle of this Wednesday.

Ask the Lord, quietly, "Is there someone You want me to go to?" Do not make a list of everyone who ever annoyed you. Just wait for the name that comes up with a little ache attached to it.

Then do one thing. Send the message. Make the call. Or, if the time is not right yet, pray for that person by name and ask God to open the way. That counts as leaving the gift at the altar and turning toward your brother.

"Be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you" (Ephesians 4:32).

The altar will still be there when you come back. So will He. And the gift you bring afterward will be lighter, because you will have set something heavy down on the way.

*Lord Jesus, You came all the way to us while we were still far from You. Show me who I need to go toward today, and give me the humility to take the first step. Heal what I cannot fix, and make me a peacemaker in the small places of my life. Amen.*
