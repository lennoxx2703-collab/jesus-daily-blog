Sunday morning has a different texture than other mornings. Even when the calendar still holds work, laundry, and unfinished conversations, the day carries an old memory: this is the first day of the week, the day the women walked toward a sealed place and found it open.

Mark tells it without flourish:

> “And very early on the first day of the week, when the sun had risen, they went to the tomb.” (Mark 16:2)

They did not go because they felt triumphant. They went because love does what it can. They carried spices for a body they expected to find. Grief had a plan. God had a different one.

## Before you arrive, He has already acted

That is one of the gentlest truths of Easter morning, and it still belongs to ordinary Sundays. The women made their way with heavy hands and heavy hearts. Behind them lay a Friday they could not unsee. Ahead of them, as far as they knew, lay only farewell. And yet the decisive work of God had already happened in the dark. The stone was moved. The grave was empty. Jesus was not waiting for their courage to finish His victory.

Some mornings we wake the same way those women walked — faithful, tired, braced for what we think we will find. We rehearse yesterday’s losses. We measure today’s capacity. We assume the story will continue in the only direction we can imagine. The Gospel meets us there and says: *God has already been at work where you have not yet arrived.*

## The first day is not a demand

It is easy to treat Sunday as a performance — the day we should feel more spiritual, pray more neatly, start the week with a cleaner heart. But the first day of the week in the Gospels is not a stage. It is a surprise. The women came to serve a dead friend and met a living Lord. Their first task was not to impress heaven. It was to receive news they could not invent: *He has risen.*

If your morning feels thin, that does not disqualify you. Thin mornings are exactly the kind of mornings God has used before. The resurrection did not wait for perfect disciples. It met people who were early, imperfect, and still showing up.

Peter later wrote of this same mercy as a new beginning that holds:

> “Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ! According to his great mercy, he has caused us to be born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead.” (1 Peter 1:3)

Living hope is not optimism with better branding. It is hope that breathes because Jesus lives. It does not depend on your night’s sleep or your week’s record. It rests on a Person who walked out of death and still walks with His people.

## He goes ahead of you into the week

Matthew’s account adds a detail that belongs at the start of any week: the angel tells the women that Jesus is going ahead of them to Galilee (Matthew 28:7). The risen Christ does not only clear the past. He walks into the future first.

That changes how you face Monday’s emails, Tuesday’s hard conversation, and the quiet loneliness that sometimes sits beside afternoon light. You are not scouting unknown country alone. The One who met Mary in the garden, who spoke peace to frightened friends, goes ahead of you into rooms you have not entered yet.

So this morning, before the list grows loud, receive the first day’s gift. You do not have to raise yourself. You do not have to pretend the stone is lighter than it is. You are invited to walk toward Jesus with whatever you are carrying — spices, questions, half-formed prayers — and to discover again that He is not where death left Him.

Very early on the first day, love went looking for a body and found a Savior. May your morning find Him, too — already risen, already near, already going ahead.
