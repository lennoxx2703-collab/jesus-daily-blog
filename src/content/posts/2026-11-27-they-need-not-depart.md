Some mornings start with a quiet calculation. You look at the day ahead — the people who will need something from you, the bills, the conversations, the energy it will all take — and then you look at what you actually have. And the numbers don't work. So before you've even finished your coffee, a part of you is already trying to figure out what you can send away.

The disciples knew that feeling. One evening on a hillside by the Sea of Galilee, they did the math too.

> "And when it was evening, his disciples came to him, saying, This is a desert place, and the time is now past; send the multitude away, that they may go into the villages, and buy themselves victuals. But Jesus said unto them, They need not depart; give ye them to eat. And they say unto him, We have here but five loaves, and two fishes. He said, Bring them hither to me." — Matthew 14:15–18 (KJV)

### "Send them away"

It's worth saying: the disciples' suggestion wasn't cruel. It was sensible. It was late, the place was remote, and there were thousands of hungry people. Sending them to the villages was the responsible thing to do. Anyone running the numbers would have said the same.

But notice the shape of it. When the need got bigger than their resources, their instinct was to move the need somewhere else. *Not here. Not us. Somebody else will have to handle this.*

Most of us do that more than we admit. We send away the friend who's struggling, not with words, but with busyness. We send away our own hard questions because we don't have the answers. We send away prayers before we pray them, because surely God has bigger things to deal with than this.

### "They need not depart"

Jesus answers with four words that turn the whole evening around: *They need not depart.*

He doesn't scold the disciples for being practical. He just refuses the premise. These people came to Him, and He is not in the business of sending hungry people away. Not then, and not now. "Him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out" (John 6:37).

And then, strangely, He hands the problem right back: "Give ye them to eat." Not because He expects them to pull it off, but because He wants them to discover what happens when their little meets His hands.

### "Bring them hither to me"

"We have here but five loaves, and two fishes." You can hear the word *but* doing a lot of work. It's the honest inventory of people who know they're short.

Jesus doesn't argue with the inventory. He just says, *Bring them here. To Me.*

That's the whole morning in one sentence. Not *fix it*. Not *try harder*. Not *pretend you have more than you do*. Just bring what you have — the small, embarrassing amount — and put it in His hands.

> "And he commanded the multitude to sit down on the grass, and took the five loaves, and the two fishes, and looking up to heaven, he blessed, and brake, and gave the loaves to his disciples, and the disciples to the multitude. And they did all eat, and were filled: and they took up of the fragments that remained twelve baskets full." — Matthew 14:19–20 (KJV)

He blessed it. He broke it. He gave it back to them to give away. And somewhere in that passing from His hands to theirs to the crowd's, there was more than enough — twelve baskets left over, one for every doubtful disciple to carry home.

### For your Friday

Maybe today you're the one holding five loaves and looking at a crowd. A family that needs more of you than you've got. A job that's asking for one more push at the end of a long week. Someone who keeps reaching out when you feel empty yourself.

Or maybe you're in the crowd — the hungry one, wondering if you should just quietly leave before you become a bother.

Either way, hear Him: *They need not depart.* You don't have to leave. You don't have to send anyone away. Bring it to Him first.

Maybe pray something like this:

*Lord Jesus, here is what I have this morning. It isn't much, and You know that better than I do. I bring it to You anyway — my time, my strength, my patience, my worries. Bless it, break it however You need to, and give it back to me to give away. And where I'm the hungry one, help me not to walk away from You. Amen.*

The math still won't add up on paper today. It didn't on that hillside either. But the One who looked up to heaven and blessed a boy's lunch is the same One standing in your morning. Bring Him what you've got. Let Him decide what it's enough for.
