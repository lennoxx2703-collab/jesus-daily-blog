Maybe today brought a message you didn't want. A phone call, a test result, an email that changed the shape of the week. Or maybe it's quieter than that: a hope you'd been carrying for a long time finally looked, this afternoon, like it wasn't going to happen. And now it's evening, and part of you is wondering whether there's any point in still praying about it.

Jairus knew that feeling better than most.

He was a ruler of the synagogue, a man with standing, and he had fallen at Jesus' feet in front of everyone because his little daughter was dying. Jesus went with him. But on the way the crowd pressed in, and a woman reached for His garment, and Jesus stopped to find her and speak with her. Every minute of that delay must have felt like an hour to a father counting breaths.

Then the news came.

> "While he yet spake, there came from the ruler of the synagogue's house certain which said, Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further? As soon as Jesus heard the word that was spoken, he saith unto the ruler of the synagogue, Be not afraid, only believe." (Mark 5:35–36, KJV)

*Why troublest thou the Master any further?* That's the sentence that lands on us too. It sounds reasonable, even kind. It's over. Don't bother Him. Let Him get on with people whose stories can still be fixed.

But look at what Jesus does. Mark says He heard the word "as soon as" it was spoken. He didn't let it sit in Jairus' chest unanswered for even a moment. Before the father could absorb it, before grief could close the door, Jesus turned to him and spoke first. The bad news didn't get the last word because Jesus wouldn't let it get the first word for long.

And notice He doesn't argue with the messengers or explain anything. He just says, *Be not afraid, only believe.* Not "believe harder," not "believe enough." Only. As if to say: you don't have to carry the outcome. Keep your eyes on Me and keep walking.

They kept walking. At the house there was noise and weeping, and when Jesus said the child was only sleeping, "they laughed him to scorn." So He put them all out. He took the father and mother and three disciples into the quiet room where the girl lay.

> "And he took the damsel by the hand, and said unto her, Talitha cumi; which is, being interpreted, Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." (Mark 5:41, KJV)

Mark keeps the words in the language Jesus actually spoke, the way you'd remember the exact words someone said on the most important day of your life. *Talitha cumi.* "Little girl, get up." It's the kind of thing a parent says on an ordinary morning. Jesus brings resurrection in the gentlest, most familiar voice there is. And then, because He's like this, He tells them to give her something to eat.

I can't promise that the thing you heard today will turn around by morning. Jesus didn't raise every child in Galilee, and faith isn't a lever we pull. But this story tells us something about Him that's true tonight whatever happens next: news that says "don't trouble Him anymore" is never His news. He hears the hard word the moment you do. He doesn't leave you alone with it. He keeps walking with you toward the house, and He goes into the room with you, past the noise, to where the hardest thing is.

So bother Him. Tell Him exactly what you heard today, in whatever words you have. You're not interrupting the Master; He came along this road on purpose.

And then, if you can, put the phone down and let Him have the night. As the psalmist prayed:

> "I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety." (Psalm 4:8, KJV)

*Be not afraid, only believe.* He's still on the road with you. Rest now.
