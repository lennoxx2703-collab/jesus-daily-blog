Friday mornings have a way of narrowing our eyes. The week is almost done, the list is still long, and we start moving through the day like we're walking down a hallway — head down, eyes on the door at the end. Get through it. Get home.

But the people Jesus talked about are usually standing in that hallway.

A few days before the cross, sitting on the Mount of Olives with His disciples, Jesus told them what the last day would look like. And in the middle of all that glory — the King on His throne, the nations gathered — He said something astonishingly small:

> "For I was an hungred, and ye gave me meat: I was thirsty, and ye gave me drink: I was a stranger, and ye took me in: naked, and ye clothed me: I was sick, and ye visited me: I was in prison, and ye came unto me." (Matthew 25:35–36, KJV)

Bread. A cup of water. A spare coat. A visit. Nothing on that list would make the news.

### They didn't even notice

The part that gets me is the reaction of the righteous. They aren't proud. They're confused:

> "Lord, when saw we thee an hungred, and fed thee? or thirsty, and gave thee drink? When saw we thee a stranger, and took thee in? or naked, and clothed thee? Or when saw we thee sick, or in prison, and came unto thee?" (Matthew 25:37–39, KJV)

They weren't keeping score. They didn't know it was Him. They just saw a person in need and did the next kind thing. And Jesus answers:

> "Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me." (Matthew 25:40, KJV)

*Unto me.* Not "it was a bit like doing it for me." He says He was there. The King of glory chose to stand in the line at the food bank, sit in the hospital bed, wait in the cell with no visitors. He takes the overlooked so personally that a kindness done to them lands on Him.

### This is not a ladder to climb

It would be easy to hear this as pressure — *do enough good things or else.* But that isn't the heartbeat of it. The righteous in this story didn't earn their way in; they were simply people whose hearts had been changed by a King who served first. Jesus fed us when we were hungry for God. He took us in when we were strangers to Him. He came to us while we were bound. Everything on that list, He did for us before we ever lifted a finger.

So serving the least isn't how we get His attention. It's what happens when His love starts leaking out of us. And here is the gift hidden inside it: the moments you think are interruptions may be the places He is closest.

### Where He might be today

He might be the coworker who's been quiet all week. The neighbor who never gets visitors. The person at the bus stop who's clearly having a bad morning. The relative in the care home you keep meaning to call. The kid nobody picks.

You don't have to fix their life. Jesus didn't say "I was hungry and you solved world hunger." He said, *you gave me meat.* One meal. One cup.

> "And whosoever shall give to drink unto one of these little ones a cup of cold water only in the name of a disciple, verily I say unto you, he shall in no wise lose his reward." (Matthew 10:42, KJV)

A cup of cold water is about as small as kindness gets. And Jesus says it is never wasted.

### A Friday morning prayer

*Lord Jesus, You served me long before I ever thought to serve anyone. Thank You for coming to me when I was hungry, lost, and shut in. Open my eyes today. Slow me down in the hallway. Show me the one person You've put in my path, and give me the courage to offer what I have — a meal, a call, a few minutes, a kind word. Let me meet You in them, even if I don't notice it's You. Amen.*

Look up today. Someone in the hallway may be Him.
