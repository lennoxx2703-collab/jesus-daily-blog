This time of year, a lot of us start thinking about tables. Who will be around them. Who won't. Which chairs will be full of noise and which will sit quietly empty. For some people, family is the warmest word they know. For others it's complicated — a mix of love and old wounds, of people who should have understood you and didn't. And for some, it's just a word that describes something they never really had.

Jesus knew about family being complicated. And one morning in a crowded house, He said something about family that is still opening doors for people today.

> "But he answered and said unto him that told him, Who is my mother? and who are my brethren? And he stretched forth his hand toward his disciples, and said, Behold my mother and my brethren! For whosoever shall do the will of my Father which is in heaven, the same is my brother, and sister, and mother." — Matthew 12:48–50 (KJV)

### The family standing outside

Jesus was teaching, the house was full, and word came: *Your mother and your brothers are outside. They want to speak with You.* Mark tells us that around this time some of His own people thought He had gone too far — "He is beside himself" (Mark 3:21). His brothers, John says, didn't yet believe in Him (John 7:5).

So even Jesus knew what it was like to be misunderstood by the people closest to Him. If your family doesn't quite get your faith, or doesn't quite get *you*, He is not unfamiliar with that ache.

### A hand stretched out

But notice what Jesus does. He doesn't push anyone away. He *widens* the circle. He stretches out His hand toward the people sitting at His feet — fishermen, a former tax collector, ordinary folks with rough edges and slow faith — and says, *Look. Here is My family.*

This isn't Jesus rejecting Mary. At the cross, in His own agony, He made sure she was cared for: "Woman, behold thy son!" and to John, "Behold thy mother!" (John 19:26–27). Her place was secure. What He's doing here is opening the door of His family far wider than blood could ever reach.

"Whosoever." That word should land softly this morning. Whosoever does the will of the Father. Not whosoever has the right last name. Not whosoever had a happy childhood. Not whosoever gets invited to a big dinner this week. Whosoever comes to the Father through the Son and wants what He wants — *that one* is His brother, His sister, His mother.

### What this means for your table

If your family is warm and full, thank God for it today — it's a gift, and not everyone has it. Look around at those faces and remember that Jesus loves every one of them more than you do.

If your family is complicated, you don't have to pretend it isn't. But you also don't have to let it be the last word on who you belong to. Your deepest belonging isn't decided at that table. It was decided at a cross and an empty tomb.

And if you're alone this season, hear this clearly: you are not without family. The psalmist says, "God setteth the solitary in families" (Psalm 68:6). Jesus is stretching out His hand toward you right now, as surely as He did in that crowded room. *Behold My brother. Behold My sister.* You are included.

### For your morning

Maybe today is a chance to be that kind of family for someone else too — the extra chair, the text to a person who'll be on their own, the patience with a relative who's hard to love. The family of Jesus grows through people who stretch out their hands the way He did.

Maybe pray something like this:

*Lord Jesus, thank You for making room for me in Your family. Thank You for the people You've given me, and help me love them well — even the hard ones. Where I feel alone, remind me that I'm Yours. And show me someone today who needs a seat at the table. Amen.*

Wherever you eat today, and whoever is or isn't beside you, you belong to Him. He looked at His followers and called them family. He's looking at you the same way.
