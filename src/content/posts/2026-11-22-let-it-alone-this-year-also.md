The middle of a Sunday can be strangely honest. The morning service is over, or the morning slipped by without one. Lunch is on the table, and somewhere in the quiet a thought comes up: *I should be further along by now.*

Further along in faith. In patience. In getting over that habit, that temper, that fear. You look at your life and see a lot of leaves and not much fruit.

Jesus told a story for that exact feeling.

> "A certain man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard; and he came and sought fruit thereon, and found none. Then said he unto the dresser of his vineyard, Behold, these three years I come seeking fruit on this fig tree, and find none: cut it down; why cumbereth it the ground? And he answering said unto him, Lord, let it alone this year also, till I shall dig about it, and dung it: and if it bear fruit, well: and if not, then after that thou shalt cut it down." — Luke 13:6–9 (KJV)

## The honest part

Let's not soften it. The story is serious. Jesus tells it right after calling people to repent (Luke 13:3–5). Fruit matters. A tree is planted to bear figs, and a life is made to bear love, trust and goodness. The owner isn't cruel for asking. Three years is a fair wait.

So if you've felt that ache of *nothing is changing*, Jesus doesn't call it silly. He takes it seriously.

## The gardener steps in

But look who speaks next. Not the tree. The tree has no defense. It's the dresser of the vineyard, the one who works the soil, who says: *Lord, let it alone this year also.*

He doesn't argue that the tree is secretly doing fine. He asks for time and offers his own work. *Till I shall dig about it, and dung it.* He'll loosen the hard ground around the roots. He'll feed it with what's messy and humble and real. The tree's next year doesn't depend on the tree trying harder. It depends on the gardener getting his hands dirty.

That's Jesus. He's the one who stands between us and the verdict we half expect, and He says, *Give me this one. Let me work.* Scripture says He "ever liveth to make intercession" for those who come to God by Him (Hebrews 7:25). He's still saying it about you today.

## What digging feels like

Here's the part nobody likes. Being dug around doesn't always feel like grace. It can feel like hard seasons, things pulled loose, roots exposed. Sometimes the "dung" is exactly what it sounds like: humbling, unpleasant, the stuff you'd never choose.

But a gardener doesn't dig to hurt a tree. He digs because he wants figs. If your life feels stirred up right now, it may not mean God is done with you. It may mean He's working on you.

## "This year also"

Notice the word *also*. This isn't the first patient year. There were three before it. And the gardener asks for one more.

Peter wrote that the Lord "is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance" (2 Peter 3:9). Patience isn't God being slow. It's God being kind on purpose.

So this Sunday, instead of grading your own branches, you might pray something simple:

*Jesus, I don't see much fruit. Thank You for asking for more time for me. Dig where You need to. Feed what's starving. I'm Yours.*

You don't have to make the figs appear by tonight. You only have to stay planted, and let the Gardener work. "He which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ" (Philippians 1:6).

One more year. In His hands, that's not a threat. It's a promise.
