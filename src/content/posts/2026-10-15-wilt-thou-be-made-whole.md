Midday can feel like a long corridor. Morning resolve has thinned. The afternoon still stretches ahead. You may be sitting with something that has gone on so long you have almost stopped expecting change — a body that hurts, a habit that will not loosen, a relationship stuck in the same loop, a quiet ache you have learned to work around.

Jesus once walked into a place full of people just like that.

## By the sheep gate

Near the temple in Jerusalem there was a pool called Bethesda, with five porches. The sick, the blind, the lame, and the withered lay there, waiting for the water to stir. Tradition said the first one in would be healed. Hope, for many of them, had become a long line and a thin chance.

One man had been there thirty-eight years.

> "When Jesus saw him lie, and knew that he had been now a long time in that case, he saith unto him, Wilt thou be made whole?" (John 5:6, KJV)

Jesus did not begin with a lecture. He saw him. He knew how long it had been. And then He asked a question that sounds almost obvious — until you live with a weary heart.

*Wilt thou be made whole?*

## The honest answer

The man's reply is not a clean yes. It is an explanation of why healing has always been just out of reach.

> "The impotent man answered him, Sir, I have no man, when the water is troubled, to put me into the pool: but while I am coming, another steppeth down before me." (John 5:7, KJV)

He has a system, and the system keeps failing him. No helper. Always someone faster. Thirty-eight years of almost. Midday often sounds like that: *I would, but I have no one. I would, but I keep arriving late. I would, but I have tried this before.*

Jesus does not argue with the story. He does not scold the man for focusing on the pool. He simply speaks a word that does not depend on the pool at all.

> "Jesus saith unto him, Rise, take up thy bed, and walk. And immediately the man was made whole, and took up his bed, and walked." (John 5:8–9, KJV)

## What His question means this afternoon

*Wilt thou be made whole?* is not a quiz about positive thinking. It is an invitation to want what only He can give — and to stop treating the old workaround as the only hope left.

Maybe you have your own Bethesda: a place you keep returning to because it is familiar, even when it never quite delivers. Maybe you have grown skilled at managing the limp instead of bringing it to Him. Maybe the honest answer today is closer to the man's than you would like: *Lord, I have no one. I keep arriving late. I am tired of almost.*

Bring that to Him. He already knows how long it has been.

Wholeness in His hands is not always instantaneous drama. Sometimes it begins as a quiet willingness: *Yes, Jesus. I want to be made whole — not only patched enough to get through Thursday, but restored by You.* Then, like the man on the porch, take the next step He names. Rise. Pick up what you have been lying on. Walk into the rest of this day as someone who has met Him.

You do not have to be the first into the water. You only need the One who walks past the porches and stops for you.

## A midday prayer

*Lord Jesus, You see how long I have carried this. You know the places where I have almost stopped hoping. Ask me again today, and give me grace to answer You honestly. Make me whole where I am broken, and help me rise and walk with You through this afternoon. Amen.*
