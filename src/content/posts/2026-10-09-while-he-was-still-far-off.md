By the middle of the day, most of us have already collected a few things we wish we hadn't said or done. A sharp word at breakfast. A promise we let slide. Something older, too, that we keep carrying around because we're not sure what else to do with it.

Jesus told a story for exactly that kind of weight.

A younger son takes his inheritance early, leaves home, and spends it all. Then a famine hits, and he ends up feeding pigs, hungry enough to envy what they eat. And there, at the very bottom, something shifts:

> "But when he came to himself he said, 'How many hired servants of my father's have bread enough to spare, and I'm dying with hunger! I will get up and go to my father, and will tell him, "Father, I have sinned against heaven, and in your sight. I am no more worthy to be called your son. Make me as one of your hired servants."'" (Luke 15:17–19)

## The speech on the road

I love how honest this is. He doesn't just turn around. He prepares a speech. He practices it, probably over and over, every mile of the way. *I'm not worthy. Just let me work. Just let me earn my bread.*

That's what a lot of us do with God. We come back, but we come back with a plan to make it up to Him. We'll pray more, try harder, be better, and maybe then He'll let us back in, at least as far as the servants' quarters.

But listen to what happens next:

> "He arose, and came to his father. But while he was still far off, his father saw him, and was moved with compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him." (Luke 15:20)

*While he was still far off.* The father was watching the road. He had been watching it for a long time. And the moment he saw that familiar shape on the horizon, he didn't wait with his arms folded. He ran. An older man in that culture didn't run, it wasn't dignified. He ran anyway.

## He never got to finish

The son starts his speech. He gets through the part about not being worthy. But he never gets to the line about being a hired servant, because the father interrupts him:

> "But the father said to his servants, 'Bring out the best robe, and put it on him. Put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet... for this, my son, was dead, and is alive again. He was lost, and is found.' Then they began to celebrate." (Luke 15:22, 24)

A robe, a ring, shoes. Not a servant's clothes. A son's. The father doesn't negotiate terms. He throws a party.

This is the heart of what Jesus wants us to know about God. Not that He tolerates us when we come back, but that He's been looking down the road for us. That He runs. That our best rehearsed apology is less than He's already decided to give.

## For the middle of your day

Maybe you're not in a far country. Maybe you're just a little distant today, a few bad choices down a road you didn't mean to take. It doesn't matter how far. The direction is what matters. One turn toward home, and you'll find the Father is already closer than you thought.

So take a minute right now. You don't need a polished speech. You can say it plainly: *Father, I wandered. I'm coming back.* And then let Him interrupt you.

He has a robe for you. He's already running.
