A Church Where the Storm Stopped the Boat

Source International Mission Board (Ruth Bowles), First Person: God is faithful and just to forgive (CC BY-NC 4.0)

Recent decades · 1 John 1:9 · November 1, 2026 · 3 min read

Ruth Bowles grew up in a small town in the Amazon region of South America, where her family serves with the International Mission Board. She was sixteen when she wrote this story down, and she starts it plainly: alcoholism has its claws wrapped tightly around the souls of that town. Very few lives there go untouched by it.

Mario’s life was one of them. He would spend days out drinking, leaving his wife, Nayli, and their six children alone and without support. Ruth calls them what they were at the time: a lost family.

But there was one light in that family. Mario’s mother, Miriam, was, in Ruth’s words, “a woman on fire for the Lord.” For years she tried to help her son come to know Jesus. Nothing pulled him away from the bottle.

Then, in early 2018, Ruth’s father and another pastor were traveling upriver by boat when a strong storm hit. They had to stop, and the place they stopped was Miriam’s farm. She invited them in, and as soon as she learned they were both pastors, she started talking about what she wanted most: a church on her property for her family.

Eight months later, the church was built. Every Sunday, Ruth’s family took their boat to Miriam’s house for Bible study.

Nayli and the children came every week. Mario would not. Some Sundays Nayli arrived with bruises and black eyes from Mario getting drunk and beating her. The missionary kids couldn’t understand everything that was happening in that home, so they did the one thing they could: they loved Mario’s children and became their friends.

After a couple of months, Mario began to come too. Ruth isn’t sure whether it was obligation or conviction at first. But slowly his heart softened. He started wanting to be there, becoming “more eager and thirstier for the Word.” The family watched his eyes change, from darkness and despair to hope. After a year, Mario put his faith in Christ.

Today, Ruth writes, he won’t even touch a can of beer. He works and provides for his family, and his wife and children no longer live in fear of him. Within four years, Nayli and their three oldest children had also come to the Lord, and Mario baptized his two daughters himself. A chain of alcoholism that had held that family was broken.

There is a lot in this story for people who are still waiting. Miriam prayed for her son for years with nothing to show for it. Then a storm, the kind of thing that ruins a trip, brought exactly the right people to her door. If you are praying for someone who seems too far gone, keep praying. God is not short of ways to get a boat to your riverbank.

And there is something here for anyone who thinks they have gone too far. Ruth draws the lesson herself: no matter how big our sins are, God still has plans for us. “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and righteous to forgive us the sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9, WEB). No one is too far for that.

One honest word, too: if you are living with someone whose drinking puts you in danger, your safety matters to God. Reaching out for help is not a lack of faith. Pray for Mario, Nayli, and their children as they keep following Jesus, and for the little church at Miriam’s farm. If Jesus has turned your life around, you are welcome to share your story.
