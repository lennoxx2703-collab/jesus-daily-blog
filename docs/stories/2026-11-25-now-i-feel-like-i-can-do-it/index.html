Now I Feel Like I Can Do It

Source International Mission Board (Chris Doyle), 'Only the beginning' for IMB ministry partner in South Asia (CC BY-NC 4.0)

Recent decades · 2 Timothy 1:7 · November 25, 2026 · 4 min read

The conversation began on a dusty road in rural South Asia. Siraj, a Christian, told Derrick Waters what he wanted more than anything: to share the gospel with his Muslim neighbors. His question was simple and honest — how do I even begin?

Derrick and his wife, Rosemary, serve with the International Mission Board in a strongly Muslim region of South Asia. Derrick had spent his early days there riding on the back of a national partner’s motorcycle, making contacts village by village. (His partner insisted he was a safe driver and Derrick didn’t need a helmet. Derrick wore one anyway — a good thing, since they drove into the back of a truck with a metal bar hanging out.) That slow, relational groundwork is what eventually led him to Siraj.

Standing beside, then standing up

At first, Siraj introduced Derrick to Muslim friends and watched him share. “At first, that’s how it was with Siraj,” Derrick said. “But what I really wanted was for him to get involved in the work himself.”

A few months later, Derrick and local partners came to Siraj’s village to lead evangelism training. They talked about God’s love for all people, learned key vocabulary, and practiced a straightforward way to share a personal testimony and the story of Jesus.

Then came the hard part. Derrick began asking Siraj to lead the conversations. Siraj stumbled. He searched for words, sprinkled in the terms he had just learned, and nervously worked his way through the message.

Slowly, confidence replaced fear. After another season of ministry together, Siraj said something Derrick would never forget:

“Before you came, I didn’t know how to share the gospel with Muslims. I wanted to, but I didn’t know how. Now I feel like I can do it.”

The night his voice came

The change became visible at a gathering arranged by a local teacher. Dozens of parents came to listen. Siraj stood in front of them, visibly nervous. His first sentences faltered — he started, stopped, and started again.

Then something shifted. He found his rhythm. He spoke clearly and naturally, using the words he had practiced, explaining the message of Jesus with growing confidence. By the end, the listeners asked the believers to pray for their sick family members. They served snacks and chai, thanked them for coming, and invited them back.

A farmer sowing seed

Siraj’s circumstances make his eagerness all the more striking. He supports his wife, three daughters and his mother on the equivalent of about US$4 a day, farms a small plot of land, and studies for exams in hopes of pursuing a Ph.D. Still, he makes time. He reads a Bible in his local language, gives Bibles away, talks about Jesus with coworkers, and visits people in surrounding villages.

His goal now is to share the gospel with 100 Muslims. “I know that at least a few will believe,” he says. But the number isn’t really the point. The fearful believer who once stood silently at the edge of gospel conversations now sees opportunities everywhere. Like the crops in his fields, he is sowing seed — among neighbors, coworkers, teachers, and families across the villages around his home.

What fear doesn’t get to decide

“For God didn’t give us a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and self-control” (2 Timothy 1:7, WEB). Paul wrote that to a young believer who needed courage, not a lecture. Siraj’s story is a picture of how that courage often arrives: not as a sudden absence of nerves, but as faltering first sentences that God carries into steady ones.

Maybe there is someone you have wanted to tell about Jesus, and you have held back because you don’t know the words. Siraj didn’t either. He started anyway — and kept starting.

Derrick asks for prayer: “We need more like him, not just one or two. Please pray for this story to be multiplied 1,000 and 10,000 times so that 6 million Muslims can hear the truth about Jesus and will follow Him.” Pray for Siraj, his family, and his neighbors today. And if God has ever turned your fear into a word spoken for Him, you are welcome to share your story.
