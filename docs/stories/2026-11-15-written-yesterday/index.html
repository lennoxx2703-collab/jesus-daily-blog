How Can This Book Feel Like It Was Written Yesterday?

Source International Mission Board (Tessa Sanchez), IMB missionaries use home, cafes, apps to open doors for gospel conversations in Spain (CC BY-NC 4.0)

Recent decades · Ecclesiastes 3:11 · November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Spain is full of churches and cathedrals. There are processions in the streets. But according to Caleb and Carina Beaty, International Mission Board missionaries in Madrid, the country has secularized fast since the 1990s, and “the average person on the street does not know anything about the gospel and does not know anything about Jesus.”

So they meet people where they already go looking for conversation. Through a website called Meetup, they host a group at a café by the river. The description says it’s about spiritual topics. People come expecting to talk about purpose, beauty and “what’s wrong with the world.” Caleb brings a list of questions that start broad and narrow toward things like “If there were a God …” Sometimes the evening gets all the way to a clear gospel presentation. Sometimes it stays at what Caleb calls pre-evangelism.

Many of the people who come have never met anyone who actually believes God exists. “I’ve had people completely shocked that we believe in God,” Carina said. “‘You really believe that God exists?’ They can’t wrap their mind around that.” One friend told them she had heard that Jesus died but had never heard that He rose from the dead.

Five atheists from the Meetup now come to a Bible study in the Beatys’ home. They are students, busy with exams, and still they make time. Caleb says they can’t stop reading the Bible and asking questions.

Two of them are Raphael and Santiago. They first came to a Thanksgiving outreach, where they heard a Spanish believer their own age tell how he came to Christ. “We wish we had faith like you guys,” they often told Carina. “But we’re too smart to be Christians.”

Then came one particular week. Santiago and another atheist were the only two who showed up. The group had been reading through the Gospel of Luke, and the two men asked for something else. “We’re bored with the Gospels. What’s the opposite of this?”

Carina admits she was offended. Caleb, feeling led by the Holy Spirit, suggested Ecclesiastes. Carina shot him a look: “Why are you taking these guys to Ecclesiastes? We’ve got to read about Jesus. He’s the main character.”

Caleb walked them through the whole book, start to finish: the man who tried pleasure, work, wisdom and wealth and found it all vapor under the sun.

“Honestly, this is infuriating,” Santiago said, “because how can this book that is so old feel like it was written yesterday?”

“I think he realized this is not an irrelevant, stupid book,” Carina said. “There’s something to it.”

That was the moment they finally took the Bibles the Beatys had been offering them. When Santiago finished Ecclesiastes, he asked what to read next. The Beatys suggested Job.

The story isn’t finished. Santiago and Raphael haven’t yet said they follow Jesus, and the Beatys are honest that this is slow work. They pray the reading will produce more than curiosity and lead to a real commitment to follow the Lord. “He’s going to work how He chooses,” Carina said. “We just pray that we can be part of that.”

There’s something beautiful in how it happened. Two young men who thought they were too smart for faith asked for the opposite of the Gospels and were handed the Bible’s most honest book about emptiness. The Preacher writes that God “has made everything beautiful in its time. He has also set eternity in their hearts” (Ecclesiastes 3:11, WEB). Santiago felt that ache and was annoyed at how accurate it was. Ecclesiastes doesn’t answer the ache. It points beyond itself. And the One it points to is the main character Carina was worried they’d skip. He said, “Seek, and you will find. Knock, and it will be opened for you” (Matthew 7:7, WEB).

Please pray for Raphael, Santiago and the other students in the Beatys’ home, that their questions would lead them to Jesus himself. Pray for the Meetup conversations by the river and for the young believers from the Beatys’ church who are learning to lead them. If an honest question once led you toward God, you are welcome to share your story.
