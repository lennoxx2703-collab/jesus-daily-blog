You Can't Climb Your Way There

Source International Mission Board (Grace Thornton), God remains faithful through IMB missionaries in Italy (CC BY-NC 4.0)

Recent decades · Ephesians 2:8–9 · November 8, 2026 · 4 min read

In Rome there is a staircase called the Holy Steps. Pilgrims climb it on their knees, praying the rosary on every step, while a mural of Jesus on the cross looks down from the top.

Jason and Lizzy Davis, International Mission Board missionaries in the city, think about those steps often. They see real longing in the people who climb them. But they also see a picture of something the gospel turns upside down. “The gospel is Jesus saying, ‘No, you can’t do it … no matter how good you think you are,’” they said. We don’t climb up to Him. He came down to us.

Their neighborhood holds about 130,000 people and one of the city’s four major basilicas, the Papal Basilica of Saint Paul. Thousands of visitors pass through each year on spiritual journeys. Yet the church the Davises serve with, a new church plant of about fifteen people, is the only evangelical congregation there. Lizzy describes many of her neighbors as people who have never really heard the gospel.

So the Davises do something ordinary. They open their home for Bible studies and for simple meals. Jason lets his walks down the street be interrupted. He knows the man at the newspaper stand, the man at the fruit stand, and the women at the grocery store, because he stops and asks about their families. “You can get into spiritual conversations very quickly with people,” he said, “especially if you’re just open and honest with them.”

That is how they came to know Luca and Elena.

Luca was an atheist. Elena was a Catholic in name. Lizzy says the “before” picture of their life was “complete and utter hopelessness and darkness,” and their marriage was teetering on the edge of ending. There was no dramatic scene in a basilica. There was a friendship with the couple down the road, kept up over time. And over time, Luca and Elena decided to follow Jesus.

Lizzy’s summary is short: “The Lord has worked in their lives.” Their lives, she says, have been “totally transformed by the gospel.”

You can see the change in what they do now. Luca, the former atheist, preaches and leads worship in the little church. Elena works with children. On some Saturdays they invite the neighborhood to an event called Stories from the Heart, where Elena reads a children’s book with gospel themes. The last one was about refugees and how God loves people from all over the world. Afterward the church helps neighborhood kids with their homework. Their son, Pablo, is part of it all.

A couple that was nearly finished with each other is now opening its doors to other families.

Paul, whose name is on the great basilica in that neighborhood, wrote it plainly: “For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, that no one would boast” (Ephesians 2:8–9, WEB). Luca and Elena didn’t climb their way out of hopelessness. Grace reached them at a kitchen table, through neighbors who took the time to stay.

The work there is not glamorous. The church is small, and a family recently moved away. Lizzy admits that language learning and slow ministry, with no quick results, can feel lonely and discouraging. “You just have to trust that the Lord is doing that work,” she said. She leans on the words Jesus spoke to Paul: “My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness” (2 Corinthians 12:9, WEB).

If you have been trying to earn your way to God, step by step, it may be time to stop climbing and look up. The One on the cross at the top of those stairs is not waiting for you to arrive. He already came all the way down. And if your marriage or your home feels like it is at the edge, Luca and Elena’s story says that nothing is too far gone for Him.

Please pray for the small church in that Rome neighborhood to stay united and encouraged, for more neighbors like Luca and Elena to be saved, and for Jason and Lizzy as they keep opening their home. If Jesus has met you somewhere you didn’t expect, you are welcome to share your story.
