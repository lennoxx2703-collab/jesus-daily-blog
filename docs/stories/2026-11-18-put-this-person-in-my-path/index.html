God, Put This Person in My Path

Source International Mission Board (Jaclyn Mains), Sustained giving to Lottie Moon offering helps provide gospel access for Deaf communities in Middle East (CC BY-NC 4.0)

Recent decades · Proverbs 16:9 · November 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Noah Lewis is a young, single International Mission Board missionary in the Middle East. He is hearing, and he has spent most of his first year and a half there learning a sign language and making friends in the local Deaf community. There is no sign language school where he lives, so he learns the way you learn a family’s language: at their tables, in their conversations, by asking. “I want to genuinely learn about their language,” he said. “I want to be friends with them. I want to know who they are.”

One evening, Deaf friends were over at his apartment for dinner. While food was being prepared, a worship song was playing in the background, and Noah couldn’t help himself. He began signing along in American Sign Language. “I just love worshiping in sign language because it’s a full-body experience,” he said.

Across the room, one of his Muslim friends was watching closely. When the song ended, he told Noah it was beautiful and asked to see another one. He compared it to hearing the Islamic call to prayer. That opened the door for Noah to explain what Christian worship is, and why Christians worship Jesus. His friend asked for more, so Noah kept signing through the playlist.

“He was just really captivated by it,” Noah said. “It really felt like in that moment the Spirit was working in his heart.”

That night did not end with a profession of faith. It became one more step in a friendship where Noah has watched curiosity slowly grow. During Ramadan, the same friend asked about fasting. Another night, he asked how Christians pray. Each question has been a small open door to talk about Jesus.

When the plan disappeared

Noah hopes one day to serve in Central Asia, and recently he went there on a trip to learn about the Deaf communities. He had a plan: visit a Deaf fellowship and meet people through it. But a few days after he arrived, increased government scrutiny made the gathering unsafe for local believers, and he was asked not to go.

So he tried everything practical he could think of. He searched social media. He walked the city. He messaged people he found online. Three days later, with nothing promising, he told his supervisor he had run out of leads.

His supervisor’s answer was simple: go walk and pray around the neighborhood.

“It was like, of course, just go and pray,” Noah said. “Everyone can do that, but I was too caught up thinking, ‘What can I do practically?’”

He went. After twenty or thirty minutes of walking and praying, he found Wi‑Fi and saw that a Deaf man had replied to one of his messages. The man called him and invited him to a home where he was spending the evening with friends. Around 10 p.m., Noah walked in not knowing what to expect and found about eight Deaf people around a table with bread, tea and coffee.

“I couldn’t find one person,” he said, “and suddenly one person led me to a massive group of people.”

As they introduced themselves, Noah realized he was looking at answers to prayers he had prayed before he ever left for the trip. He had asked God to connect him with Deaf people from particular areas on his heart. One man was from a city he had prayed for. Another came from an area tied to where he hopes to serve. “I was just blown away that all of these things were being answered all at once.”

That evening turned into several days together: a trip into the mountains, and introductions to more Deaf people in a village, at a bazaar, even at a local car wash. He also met Deaf Christians there and heard how God had changed their lives, which, after serving where there are few Deaf followers of Jesus, gave him “a renewed passion and fire for what we do.”

“God answered every prayer that I had for that trip and then more,” he said. “I saw it so many times — simply stopping and saying, ‘God, put this person in my path,’ and He did.”

Prayer is the work

There’s a quiet lesson here for anyone who has ever exhausted every practical option. Noah had been learning it since he arrived: prayer is not separate from the work. It is the foundation of it. “A man’s heart plans his course, but Yahweh directs his steps” (Proverbs 16:9, WEB). And the One who directs our steps also knows the heart of a Muslim friend watching a song in a language of hands, asking to see one more.

Please pray for Noah’s friend, that his questions about worship, fasting and prayer would lead him to Jesus himself. Pray for the Deaf communities in the Middle East and Central Asia, for the Deaf believers there who live under pressure, and for the new friends Noah met around that table. If God has ever answered when you ran out of ideas, you are welcome to share your story.
