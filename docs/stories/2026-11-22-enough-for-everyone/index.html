Double the Guests, Enough for Everyone

Source International Mission Board (Chris Doyle), A Thanksgiving in Poland to remember (CC BY-NC 4.0)

Recent decades · Philippians 4:19 · November 22, 2026 · 3 min read

Poland celebrates Christmas and Easter on a grand scale. Thanksgiving is not a Polish holiday at all. But for Cal and Erin Hiebsch, International Mission Board missionaries in Poland, that is exactly what makes it useful. Many Poles know a little about the American holiday and are curious about it, so every year the Hiebsch family opens their doors.

“Thanksgiving is probably one of our bigger entrées into American culture,” Cal said.

They invite friends, neighbors and people from their English conversation clubs to a traditional meal. For many guests it is their first taste of stuffing, green bean casserole, sweet potato casserole and roast turkey — and even getting a whole turkey can be a challenge, since Polish stores more often sell breasts and other cuts than the whole bird.

The night the doors kept opening

One year in particular, the family still talks about. With volunteers helping, Erin and a crew planned carefully. “We had people in four or five different kitchens across the city cooking,” she said. Past events had drawn 20 to 30 people, so they made “double batches” of each dish, hoping everyone could try a little of everything.

Then the evening began, and guests kept arriving.

More came through the doors. Then more. The room filled until it was standing-room only. Instead of a few dozen people, nearly 70 showed up.

“I quietly asked a couple of the volunteer ladies to be praying that there would be enough for everyone,” Erin recalled.

Cal was the emcee, welcoming guests and telling the story of the first Thanksgiving — how God had provided for the Pilgrims in hard circumstances. Meanwhile, plate after plate went out. Every new face at the door made the math look worse.

And yet the food held. People kept receiving full plates. Everyone ate. Conversations flowed around the tables. (“The pumpkin pie was especially popular,” Erin said.) And woven through the evening, every guest heard the gospel.

When it was over, the team compared notes. “We doubled our crowd and had enough food for everyone to have a good Thanksgiving feast,” Cal said. They had prepared for one number and served more than twice as many. To this day, they find it hard to explain outside of God’s provision.

What an open table can hold

The IMB’s account doesn’t overclaim it: miraculous, or simply an extraordinary provision — either way, more people than anyone planned for were welcomed, fed and told about the hope of Christ. An ordinary meal, an open door and a willingness to invite people in became a place where strangers met the God who provides.

“My God will supply every need of yours according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus” (Philippians 4:19, WEB). That promise was first written to a church that had shared what little it had. It still tends to show up around tables where people are giving more than they think they can.

This week, as Thanksgiving comes around again, pray for Cal and Erin and their two sons, and for the Polish friends and neighbors who have sat at their table and heard about Jesus — that the seed planted over pumpkin pie would grow into faith. And if you have ever watched God stretch something you were sure would run out, you are welcome to share your story.
