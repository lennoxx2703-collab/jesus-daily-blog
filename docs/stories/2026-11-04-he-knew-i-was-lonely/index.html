He Knew I Was Lonely

Source International Mission Board (Emily Hall), IMB missionary sees God at work through new believer and local church (CC BY-NC 4.0)

Recent decades · John 4:13–14 · November 4, 2026 · 4 min read

The sun was going down and the park was full of men. Sophia, a missionary with the International Mission Board, had come with another missionary and with Maryam, a South Asian Christian, hoping to talk with someone about Jesus. In their Muslim-majority city, walking up to a man was out of the question. Twenty minutes went by and they hadn’t found a single woman.

Maryam herself is part of this story. She grew up distrusting Muslims, and when Sophia first invited her to come along, she never imagined a Muslim could come to know Jesus. But the more they went out together, the more God softened her heart. One day Sophia found her sharing in the park on her own. These days she talks about Jesus with classmates, rickshaw drivers, and women in the park whenever she can find them.

So that evening they stopped and prayed: “God, give us just one woman to talk to.”

Then they saw her, a girl sitting alone on a bench, dressed like the other conservative Muslim women in the area, covered in black except for her face. It was Eid al-Adha, one of the biggest holidays of the year there. They greeted her and asked about her holiday plans.

Her name was Tara, and she wasn’t intimidated at all. “You may think I’m a Muslim from the way I dress,” she said, “but actually in my heart, I have left Islam.”

Tara was a student at a conservative Islamic university, and she had grown disillusioned. She had looked into Buddhism and Hinduism through friends. She had even met with atheists, who struck her as unhappy, following only themselves. Without knowing God, she had no one to pray to, and it left her lonely.

“Do you know any Christians?” Sophia asked. “Would you like to hear what we believe?”

“Sure, please share.”

They read about Jesus’ birth, and Tara wanted more. She wasn’t ready to visit a church, but every week Sophia and Maryam met her wherever they could talk without “too many ears and eyes”: coffee shops, tea shops, Sophia’s home. They read through the Gospel of John together.

One November day in a coffee shop, they reached the story of the Samaritan woman at the well, and Sophia noticed Tara was crying. “Tara, I see you have tears. Let’s stop. Let’s talk about it.” That day, Tara prayed to receive Christ.

It makes sense that it was that passage. A woman comes alone to a well in the heat of the day, and Jesus is already sitting there waiting for her. He tells her, “Everyone who drinks of this water will thirst again, but whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never thirst again” (John 4:13–14, WEB). Tara had been drinking from one well after another. Then she met the One who had come looking for her.

Soon she wanted to visit the church, though she worried how believers would receive a woman in a hijab. “They will love you,” Sophia told her. They did. That Christmas, eighty people packed Sophia’s apartment, singing and dancing in circles, and there was Tara, who grew up in a home where music wasn’t allowed, wearing red Christmas garland like a scarf and smiling across the room.

That spring she was baptized in a river behind a Hindu village, where the church has baptized believers for years. Looking back to that evening in the park, she told Sophia: “I really feel this God is so personal. He knew I was lonely, and He brought you to me.”

Her story isn’t tidy. When Tara went home for the next Eid, word got around that she had been learning about Christianity. For two weeks, uncles, aunts, grandparents, and parents came every day to tell her to stay on the path of Islam. They still don’t know she has been baptized. Sophia prays hard for her, because, as she says, in that region “it can be so hard to remain in faith.” Meanwhile Tara keeps bringing university friends to church, and one young man among them says he wants to follow Jesus and be baptized.

If you feel alone today, with no one to pray to or no one who would understand, notice how this story starts. Two women asked God for just one person, and a lonely student was already sitting on the bench. He sees you on yours too. And if you have been waiting a long time to talk to someone about Jesus, Maryam’s story is for you as well: the one who once couldn’t imagine it is now the one who goes looking.

Please pray for Tara to stand firm and keep growing whatever the coming year brings, for wisdom and courage as she thinks about telling her family, for the student who keeps coming to church, and for the villagers near the baptism river who have heard the gospel. If Jesus has met you in a lonely place, you are welcome to share your story.
