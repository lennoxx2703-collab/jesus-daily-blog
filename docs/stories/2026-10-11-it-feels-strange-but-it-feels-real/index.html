It Feels Strange, but It Feels Real

Source International Mission Board (Chris Doyle), From broken bones to unshakable faith, Pedro’s light shines in Mexican village (CC BY-NC 4.0)

Recent decades · Matthew 11:28 · October 11, 2026 · 3 min read

Life in Pedro’s village has a hard, practical rhythm. Children finish eighth grade and go straight to the fields or the factories. Most men walk a mile or more to catch the one truck that takes them to work. There is no permanent church, no resident pastor, no congregation. Faith there is a blend of cultural Catholic devotion and folk belief: saints, the Virgin Mary, and the spirits of ancestors.

When Pedro was about ten, his bones began to break without warning. Doctors eventually named it: a brittle bone disease. It meant surgery after surgery, limbs that stayed fragile, and crutches for every step. He is now in his late twenties. His family opened a tiny storefront in their home, selling tortillas, chips and canned soda. But the pain never left, and neither did the gossip that his condition was a “curse.”

In 2024 the weight became too much. Pedro sank into a deep depression. For fifteen days he stopped eating; when every day was a struggle just to stand, even food felt meaningless. His family was terrified and called anyone who might help. One name kept coming up: Pastor Jacob, a Mexican evangelist who had spent a decade driving from village to village, simply getting to know people.

Jacob drove the hour-long road. He came with a warm smile, a Bible and a tender heart. Over a plain meal of homemade tortillas and beans, he prayed for Pedro and gently told him the story of Jesus — how He suffered, died and rose again.

Within a few weeks, something in Pedro began to shift. He let go of the ancestral folklore he had clung to and turned his attention to the gospel. Jacob kept visiting, kept encouraging, kept telling Bible stories. Before long, International Mission Board missionary Jimmy Peebles started coming along.

One evening Jacob told the story of the Last Supper — twice — and they talked about what Jesus and His disciples shared at that table the night before the cross. Pedro answered with honesty: “I’ve never heard that story before. It feels strange, but it feels real.” That night he texted Peebles to thank him for coming. “He’s hungry for Christian interaction,” Peebles said.

In July 2025, Pedro made a profession of faith in Christ. When believers from a nearby city came out to the village for worship and Bible study — songs, simple meals, shared stories — “it became the first true Christian community Pedro had ever known,” Peebles said.

Pedro is still the only evangelical Christian in his village. His body is still fragile, the road is still dusty, and his family has resisted his new faith, though they remain kind and grateful to the men who brought hope when he had none. And Pedro lets his light shine quietly. He prays for a grieving neighbor. When believers visit, he meets them with open arms and a fresh batch of tortillas.

Jacob once told Peebles, “There is no church here, but there are people yearning for truth.” Pedro is what that yearning looks like when Jesus answers it.

If you are carrying something that never seems to heal — in your body, your mind, or the way people look at you — Jesus did not wait for Pedro to be strong before He came close. He came to a man on crutches, at a kitchen table, through a friend who was willing to drive an hour and listen. His invitation is the same for you: “Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest” (Matthew 11:28, WEB). And if you are in a dark place right now, please reach out to someone today — a friend, a pastor, or a local crisis line. If Jesus has met you in a surprising way, you are welcome to share your story.
