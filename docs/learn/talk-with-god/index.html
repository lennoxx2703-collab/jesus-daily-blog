Learning to pray

Lesson 3 · About 8 minutes

Prayer in plain words — how to speak with God when you feel awkward, dry, or unsure where to start.

Before you begin

If prayer feels strange, you are not alone. Many people worry they will say the wrong thing, or that nothing is happening, or that they need special words. You do not. Prayer is speaking with God — honestly, simply, as you are.

You can pray with eyes open or closed, walking or sitting, in a crowd or alone. Posture can help, but honesty matters more.

What prayer is

Prayer is relationship, not performance. God already knows your mind. Still, He invites you to bring your voice. Parents love to hear a child speak even when they already know the need. In a similar way, prayer draws your heart toward the One who loves you.

Jesus taught His friends to pray. He also prayed Himself — early in the morning, in lonely places, at meals, in anguish. If the Son of God made room to talk with the Father, we can take courage to begin.

You can use patterns if they help. Many people lean on four simple movements:

  • Thank You — name a gift, even a small one.
  • Sorry — tell the truth about what went wrong.
  • Please — ask for help for yourself and others.
  • I am here — sit quietly and remember you are not alone.

You do not have to cover all four every time. One true sentence is a real prayer.

When you have no words

Some days your mind is loud. Some days it is empty. On those days, borrowed words are a kindness, not a cheat. Use a short written prayer. Pray a verse slowly. Or sit in silence and let your breathing be the only offering: I am here. You are here.

The World English Bible keeps Jesus’ encouragement close:

“Ask, and it will be given you. Seek, and you will find. Knock, and it will be opened for you.” — Matthew 7:7

Asking is not demanding. Seeking is not frantic. Knocking is simply coming again to the door.

If you feel dry for a long time, tell God that too. Dryness does not mean you have failed. Sometimes faith is staying in the room when feelings are quiet.

Growing without pressure

Keep prayer small enough to repeat. Two minutes of honesty beats twenty minutes of pretending. Link prayer to something you already do — morning coffee, a walk, the end of the day. Over time, conversation becomes more natural, the way speaking with a friend does.

Listen as well as speak. Listening in prayer is often quiet attention to Scripture, or noticing a nudge toward kindness, patience, or courage. You will not hear a booming voice every day. You may notice peace, clarity, or a soft correction. Test what you sense against Jesus’ character in the Gospels.

Try this

Today, pray for three minutes using plain sentences:

  1. Father, thank You for…
  2. I need help with…
  3. Please be near to… (name one person)
  4. Sit in silence for thirty seconds. End with: Amen — I trust You with this day.

If you want company finding words, open Pray. If you want quiet before words, open Sit. Both are doorways, not homework.
