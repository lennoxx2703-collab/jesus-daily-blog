How to read a Gospel

Lesson 2 · About 10 minutes

A gentle way to begin with Mark or John — slow reading, simple questions, no rush.

Before you begin

You do not need to read the whole Bible this week. Start with a Gospel — one of the four books that tell the story of Jesus’ life, death, and rising: Matthew, Mark, Luke, or John. For beginners, Mark is short and clear. John is rich and close to the heart. Either is a good door.

Bring a pencil if you like. Bring honesty more than that. Read as a guest at a table, not as a student taking a test.

Why a Gospel first

The Gospels put you face to face with Jesus. You watch Him walk, speak, heal, and welcome. You hear His questions. You see how He treats the tired and the proud. Doctrine matters, but friendship with Jesus often begins by watching Him in the story.

Mark moves quickly. People meet Jesus and must decide what to do with Him. John slows down and lingers — light and dark, living water, a shepherd’s voice, a friend who stays. Choose one and stay with it for a while before hopping around.

A simple way to read

Pick a short section — half a chapter is plenty. Read it once without hurry. Then read it again. On the second pass, ask three quiet questions:

  1. What is Jesus doing or saying here?
  2. What does this show about His heart?
  3. What might this mean for my day?

You do not need clever answers. One honest sentence for each question is enough. If a verse feels heavy or confusing, mark it and keep going. You can return later. Confusion is not failure; it is often the start of seeking.

When Scripture quotes Jesus, stay close to the words themselves. Here is an invitation from the World English Bible:

“Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest.” — Matthew 11:28

Notice the tone. He does not say, “Come when you are strong.” He says come when you are tired.

Tips that keep it gentle

  • Small and steady beats long and rare. Ten focused minutes most days will teach you more than an exhausted hour once a month.
  • Read aloud sometimes. Hearing the words slows you down and helps them land.
  • Pray one line before you start: Lord, open my ears.
  • Do not panic about parts you do not understand yet. Keep the main thing in view: who Jesus is and how He loves.
  • Use a clear translation. On this site, Readings use the World English Bible so you can sit with the text freely.

If you miss a day, begin again without drama. Walking with Jesus is not a streak to protect. It is a relationship to return to.

Try this

This week, choose Mark 1 or John 1. Read one section a day — even a few paragraphs. After each reading, write three short lines answering the questions above.

When you want a longer sit with Jesus’ teaching, open Readings on this site and stay with one chapter without multitasking. Let the words be enough for that sitting.
