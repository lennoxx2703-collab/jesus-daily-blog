The Things Which Belong unto Thy Peace

October 27, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture Luke 19:41–42

  • evening
  • peace
  • tears-of-jesus
  • jerusalem
  • reflection
  • tuesday

There’s a moment on the road into Jerusalem that’s easy to rush past. The crowds have just been shouting “Blessed be the King that cometh in the name of the Lord.” Palm branches, garments on the road, the whole procession buzzing. And then the road crests the Mount of Olives, the city comes into view, and Jesus stops.

“And when he was come near, he beheld the city, and wept over it, saying, If thou hadst known, even thou, at least in this thy day, the things which belong unto thy peace! but now they are hid from thine eyes.” (Luke 19:41–42, KJV)

In the middle of a parade in His honour, Jesus cries.

Not because He’s being cheered too little. He’s crying because He can see what the noise is covering up. The city He loves is about to miss the very thing it’s been longing for. Peace was riding in on a donkey, right in front of them, and they couldn’t see it.

He weeps before He warns

Notice the order. Before any word about what’s coming, there are tears. Jesus doesn’t look at Jerusalem with the cool eyes of a judge reading a verdict. He looks at it the way a parent looks at a child who keeps running toward the road. Elsewhere He says it plainly:

“O Jerusalem, Jerusalem… how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not!” (Matthew 23:37, KJV)

How often would I have gathered. That’s the heart behind the tears. He wasn’t hoping to catch them out. He was hoping to gather them in.

It matters to know that tonight, because when we look back over a day and see the places we missed Him, it’s tempting to imagine Jesus standing over us with folded arms. Luke shows us something else. When He sees what we’ve missed, His first response is grief, and His grief is a kind of love.

“In this thy day”

There’s a tender little phrase in the middle of His lament: at least in this thy day. Jerusalem had a day. A specific, ordinary-looking day when peace came near.

So do we. Today was one of them. It was a Tuesday, nothing special on the calendar. But somewhere in it there were things that belonged to your peace. Maybe a minute when you could have stopped and breathed a prayer. A person who needed a gentle word more than a quick reply. A worry you could have handed over instead of carrying it through every meeting. Some of those moments you probably caught. Some of them slipped by in the rush, the way they slipped by for a city full of good, busy, religious people.

That’s not a reason for shame. It’s a reason to look again.

A short evening review

Before you sleep, try sitting with Jesus on that hillside for a few minutes and letting Him show you the day.

  • Where was peace near today? Not the dramatic moments. The small ones. A kindness, a pause, a sentence of Scripture that stuck.
  • Where did the noise drown it out? Just notice. You don’t have to fix it tonight.
  • What would you like to hand Him now? The things that are still hid from your eyes, the worries you can’t see past, give them to the One who wept to gather you.

The best news in this passage is that Jesus didn’t stop at the hill. He kept walking into the city. He went into the temple, into the arguments, all the way to the cross, for the very people who missed Him. The peace they couldn’t see, He went and made for them with His own body.

“For he is our peace.” (Ephesians 2:14, KJV)

So whatever you missed today, He hasn’t missed you. Tomorrow is another this thy day. For tonight, let the One who cried over a city gather you under His wings, and rest there.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Luke 19:41–42, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
