O Woman, Great Is Thy Faith

October 20, 2026 · Midday · 3 min read

Scripture Matthew 15:22–28

  • prayer
  • persistence
  • faith
  • mothers
  • when-god-is-silent

Somewhere around the middle of the day, a lot of us carry a prayer we’ve been praying for a long time. A child who is struggling. A diagnosis that hasn’t changed. A situation at home that never seems to move. You prayed it this morning, you’ll probably pray it again tonight, and at lunchtime it sits quietly in the back of your mind: Is He even hearing this?

There’s a mother in Matthew’s Gospel who knew that feeling better than most.

“And, behold, a woman of Canaan came out of the same coasts, and cried unto him, saying, Have mercy on me, O Lord, thou son of David; my daughter is grievously vexed with a devil. But he answered her not a word.” (Matthew 15:22–23, KJV)

She came anyway

She wasn’t supposed to be there. She was an outsider, a Canaanite, from a people with a long and painful history with Israel. She had no claim on this Jewish rabbi and she knew it. But her daughter was suffering, and love doesn’t wait for permission. So she came out, and she cried after Him.

And then the hardest line in the story: “But he answered her not a word.”

If you’ve ever prayed and heard nothing back, that sentence will land somewhere deep. The disciples made it worse. “Send her away,” they said, “for she crieth after us.” She was noise to them. An interruption to their schedule.

The shortest prayer in the Bible

What she did next is the part I want to hold onto today.

“Then came she and worshipped him, saying, Lord, help me.” (Matthew 15:25, KJV)

She didn’t leave. She came closer. She knelt. And she stopped explaining and simply said three words: Lord, help me.

Sometimes we think prayer has to be eloquent or well-argued. This mother had run out of arguments. All she had left was the bare truth of her need and the person she believed could meet it. That’s not a weak prayer. That might be the purest prayer there is.

When Jesus answered with a saying about the children’s bread, she didn’t take offence and walk away. She leaned in further: “Truth, Lord: yet the dogs eat of the crumbs which fall from their masters’ table.” In other words, I’m not asking for the whole table. I know who You are. Even a crumb from You is enough.

What the silence was hiding

Here’s how it ends:

“Then Jesus answered and said unto her, O woman, great is thy faith: be it unto thee even as thou wilt. And her daughter was made whole from that very hour.” (Matthew 15:28, KJV)

Great is thy faith. Jesus said that to very few people, and one of them was a foreign mother the disciples wanted to get rid of. His silence wasn’t rejection. By the end of the conversation it’s clear He had been drawing her faith out into the open, so that everyone standing there, including His own disciples, could see what real trust looks like. She walked away with more than a healed daughter. She walked away having been seen, named, and praised by the Son of God.

I can’t promise that every long prayer will be answered the way we hope, or on our timeline. But this story tells us something true about Jesus: His quiet is never indifference. Elsewhere He told His followers “that men ought always to pray, and not to faint” (Luke 18:1, KJV). He wants us to keep coming.

For the middle of your day

So if there’s a prayer you’ve been carrying since morning, don’t put it down. You don’t need new words. Take hers.

Lord, help me.

Say it at your desk, in the car, over the sink, in the two minutes before your next meeting. Say it for the person you love who is hurting. Come a little closer, even when it feels quiet. The same Jesus who stopped for a mother at the edge of Tyre and Sidon has not grown tired of listening. Even His crumbs are more than enough, and He rarely gives only crumbs.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in Matthew 15:22–28, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
