Seek the Things Above

October 4, 2026 · Morning · 3 min read

Scripture Colossians 3:1–2

  • discipleship
  • gospel
  • prayer
  • invitation

Sunday morning often arrives with a choice of altitude. You can wake into the low weather of the week — the unfinished list, the lingering worry, the quiet shame that somehow hitchhiked through the night. Or you can wake into something higher: the truth that, in Jesus, your life is not defined by what sits on the ground in front of you.

Paul writes to people who already belong to Christ, and he begins the day for them with a gentle reorientation:

“If then you were raised together with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated on the right hand of God. Set your mind on the things that are above, not on the things that are on the earth.” (Colossians 3:1–2)

Not if you feel spiritual. Not if you finally got your habits right. If you were raised with Christ — and in the gospel, that is the settled fact for everyone who trusts Him — then seek what is above. Set your mind there. Begin Sunday by remembering where Jesus is, and who you are in Him.

Raised means the story already turned

The word raised is Easter language. It assumes a death and a rising. In baptism and faith, you were joined to Jesus — buried with Him, raised with Him. Sunday is not the day you try to climb into resurrection. It is the day you remember that resurrection has already claimed you.

That changes how morning pressure lands. Earthly things still matter: work, family, bills, health, the hard conversation waiting after lunch. Paul is not asking you to float away from real life. He is asking you not to let the earthbound set the ceiling of your hope. Christ is seated at God’s right hand. Your life is hidden with Him (Colossians 3:3). The highest fact of this Sunday is not your mood. It is His place — and your belonging there.

Seeking is a morning practice

To seek the things above is not a vague spirituality. It is attention. It is where you point the first quiet minutes. Before the phone rehearses every urgency, you look toward Jesus — His mercy, His kingdom, His nearness by the Spirit, His promise to be with you always.

Setting your mind is active and kind. You may need to gently lift it more than once. Thoughts drift earthward; that is human. Seeking above is the gracious habit of returning. A short prayer can be the hinge:

Lord Jesus, I was raised with You. Lift my mind this morning. Help me seek what is Yours — love, peace, faithfulness, the courage to walk with You through ordinary hours.

That is discipleship in plain clothes. Not escape from the day, but entrance into it with a different center of gravity.

Sunday’s open invitation

This morning invites you to worship not as performance, but as orientation. Whether you gather with others or sit alone with Scripture, you are practicing Colossians 3: looking up to the One who already went ahead through death and into life.

If the week left you feeling small, hear this again: you seek from resurrection, not toward it as an unpaid debt. Jesus does the heavy lifting. Your part is to receive and to follow — to set your mind where He is, and then to carry His kindness into the earthly things you must touch today.

So begin Sunday higher than fear. Higher than self-scoring. Higher than the news cycle’s grip. Seek the things above, where Christ is. Let that gaze steady your hands for whatever the day asks. The One seated at the right hand of God is not distant from your morning. He is the reason you can walk into it already raised — already loved — already invited to look up.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Colossians 3:1–2, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
