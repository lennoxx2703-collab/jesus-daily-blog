The Truth Shall Make You Free

November 21, 2026 · Midday · 3 min read

Scripture John 8:31–32, 36

  • saturday
  • midday
  • freedom
  • truth
  • identity
  • abiding

Saturday afternoon is supposed to feel free. No meetings, no boss, no alarm. And yet a lot of us spend it chained to something we can’t quite see.

A sentence someone said to you years ago that still plays on repeat. You’re too much. You’re not enough. You always ruin things. A habit you swore you’d quit last month. A fear that quietly decides what you will and won’t try. The weekend gives your body a break, but the old voices keep working overtime.

If that’s where you are as you eat lunch today, listen to what Jesus said to a crowd that had just started believing in Him:

“If ye continue in my word, then are ye my disciples indeed; and ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free.” — John 8:31–32 (KJV)

Free from what?

The people listening bristled. We’ve never been in bondage to anyone, they said. Which was a strange claim from a nation that had been ruled by Egypt, Babylon, and now Rome. But we do the same thing. We’d rather say “I’m fine” than admit there’s a chain.

Jesus doesn’t argue about politics. He goes deeper: “Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin” (John 8:34). The real captivity isn’t out there. It’s the stuff in here that we keep doing even when we hate it, and the lies we keep believing even when we know better.

Then He says something beautiful. A servant doesn’t get to stay in the house forever, but a son does (John 8:35). And then:

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.” — John 8:36 (KJV)

Freedom has a face

Notice that Jesus doesn’t hand out a method. He doesn’t say, “Try harder, think positive, and you’ll break free.” He says the Son sets you free. Freedom isn’t a technique you master. It’s a Person who opens the door, and then, astonishingly, invites you to stay in the house as family instead of slipping back out as a hired hand.

And notice how the truth reaches us: “if ye continue in my word.” Not a one-time insight. Continuing. Abiding. Coming back to what He says about you, again and again, until it gets louder than the old sentence.

That’s slow work, and it’s meant to be. Most of the lies we carry weren’t installed in a day, and Jesus is patient enough to replace them one at a time. Too much? He calls you His friend. Not enough? He gave Himself for you. You ruin everything? He makes all things new. Every time you hold His word up against the old voice, a link in the chain loosens.

A Saturday kind of freedom

Here’s a small idea for the rest of today. Think of one sentence that has been running your life lately, the one you’d never say out loud but believe anyway. Write it down if you can. Then ask Jesus, honestly, “Is this true?”

Then open the Gospels and sit with Him for ten minutes. Read how He treats people who are ashamed, tired, overlooked, or stuck. That’s how He sees you too. Let His words have the last word this afternoon.

You don’t have to fix yourself before you come. The door is already open. The Son is the one holding it, and He isn’t asking you to earn your place. He’s asking you to come in, and to stay.

Lord Jesus, You are the truth. Show me the lies I’ve been living under, and speak louder than they do. Make me free indeed, not just for a weekend, but for good. Amen.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in John 8:31–32, 36, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
