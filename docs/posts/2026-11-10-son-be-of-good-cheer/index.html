Son, Be of Good Cheer

November 10, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture Matthew 9:2, 6–7

  • tuesday
  • evening
  • forgiveness
  • grace
  • healing
  • rest

By Tuesday evening the week has found its rhythm, and so have our failures. The sharp word you said in a meeting. The thing you promised and didn’t do. The little dishonesty you told yourself didn’t count. The way you looked past someone who needed you. None of it is dramatic. It’s just there, lying in the corner of the day, and you notice it most when the house gets quiet.

Matthew tells a short story that fits this hour well.

Brought on a bed

“And, behold, they brought to him a man sick of the palsy, lying on a bed: and Jesus seeing their faith said unto the sick of the palsy; Son, be of good cheer; thy sins be forgiven thee” (Matthew 9:2, KJV).

Picture it. A man who cannot walk is carried in by others. Everyone in the room knows what he needs. His legs are the obvious problem. His friends didn’t haul him across town for a conversation about his soul.

And Jesus looks at him and says Son.

Not “sir.” Not “you there.” Son. Then: be of good cheer. Take heart. Lift your face. And then the words nobody expected: thy sins be forgiven thee.

Jesus went straight past the problem everyone could see to the one only He could fully see. He wasn’t ignoring the man’s body. A few verses later He tells him, “Arise, take up thy bed, and go unto thine house,” and the man gets up and walks home (Matthew 9:6–7). But first, before anything else, Jesus deals with the weight underneath.

The heavier load

I think many of us carry our evenings the way that man lay on his bed. We know about the visible problems: the bills, the tiredness, the health worries, the things we’re waiting on. We pray about those, and we should.

But underneath is often a quieter load. A low sense that we are not right with God. That we’ve disappointed Him again. That if He looked at our day closely, He would frown.

Jesus knows that load is real, and He doesn’t pretend it isn’t there. He also doesn’t leave it there. Notice the order: be of good cheer comes before thy sins be forgiven, but both come in the same breath. He doesn’t make the man earn the cheer by confessing well enough first. The comfort and the forgiveness arrive together, from the same mouth, because they come from the same heart.

What the evening is for

So here is a simple practice for tonight.

Before you sleep, let the day come back to you, not to beat yourself up, but to be honest. Name the things that weren’t right. Say them plainly to Jesus, the way you’d tell a friend who already knows and loves you anyway. The promise is solid: “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9, KJV).

Then listen for His answer. It is the same one the man on the bed heard. Son. Daughter. Be of good cheer. Thy sins be forgiven thee.

Not “try harder tomorrow.” Not “we’ll see.” Forgiven. Finished. Sent away “as far as the east is from the west” (Psalm 103:12, KJV).

Go unto thine house

The story ends almost plainly. The man got up and went home. No speech, no ceremony. He walked back into his ordinary life with the bed under his arm and a clean heart inside him.

That is what Jesus offers you tonight. You don’t need to carry today’s failures into tomorrow. You can set them down with Him, receive His word over you, and go home to rest, forgiven and free.

Lord Jesus, You see what I can’t hide and what I don’t even notice. Thank You that You call me Your child and speak forgiveness before I have earned anything. I give You today, the good and the broken parts. Let me sleep tonight with Your word in my heart: be of good cheer. Amen.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Matthew 9:2, 6–7, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
