Come and Have Breakfast

September 18, 2026 · Morning · 5 min read

Scripture John 21:12

  • gospel
  • invitation
  • mercy
  • discipleship

Some mornings begin with effort and little fruit. You try. You work. You return to familiar patterns because they feel safer than waiting. And still the net comes up empty — not only in work or plans, but in the quiet places of the heart.

John’s Gospel places the disciples in that kind of dawn. After the resurrection, Peter says, “I am going fishing,” and the others go with him (John 21:3). They fish all night and catch nothing. Then the story turns:

“Just as day was breaking, Jesus stood on the shore…” (John 21:4)

They do not recognize Him at first. He asks if they have any fish. They answer no. He tells them to cast the net on the right side of the boat — and suddenly it is full. Only then does the beloved disciple cry out, “It is the Lord!” (John 21:7). Peter plunges into the water. The others drag the net ashore. And there, waiting, is a charcoal fire with fish already on it, and bread.

Before they can explain themselves, Jesus says:

“Come and have breakfast.” (John 21:12)

Mercy that finds you first

Notice the order. Jesus does not wait for a polished apology, a five-year plan, or a renewed résumé of discipleship. He stands on the shore while they are still empty-handed. He provides the catch. He has already prepared the meal. Then He invites them to eat.

That is how grace often arrives in the morning: not as a reward for spiritual productivity, but as presence that meets us where our own strength ran out overnight.

Peter knew failure in a particular way. Not long before, he had warmed himself by another charcoal fire and denied Jesus three times (John 18:18, 25–27). Now he finds another fire — and instead of accusation, breakfast. Later Jesus will restore him with questions of love (John 21:15–17). But first comes food. First comes nearness. First comes an invitation that does not require him to have everything figured out.

If you woke today still carrying yesterday’s emptiness — a conversation you mishandled, a prayer that felt thin, a hope that did not deliver — hear the shore-voice of Jesus. He is not pacing the beach with a checklist. He is cooking. He is calling. He is saying, in effect: Sit down. Be fed. Begin again with Me.

The day that starts with receiving

We are trained to begin mornings by producing: check the phone, scan the tasks, prove we are useful. Jesus trains His friends differently here. The first movement of the day by the sea is to receive — fish they did not invent, bread they did not bake, fellowship they did not earn.

“Come and have breakfast” is pastoral kindness wrapped in ordinary hospitality. God does not only speak in thunder. Sometimes He speaks in a warm meal and a place at the fire.

And notice: Jesus eats with them. “Jesus came and took the bread and gave it to them, and so with the fish” (John 21:13). The risen Lord does not hover above their hunger. He shares it. Discipleship is not a lonely climb toward a distant God; it is breakfast with a Savior who stays close enough to pass the bread.

When recognition comes slowly

The disciples have already seen the risen Jesus, and still, in this grey morning light, recognition takes time. That detail is a mercy for us. Faith does not always snap into focus at sunrise. Sometimes we labor in the old boat for a while before we see that He has been standing there all along.

If your morning prayer feels foggy, you are not failing. You may simply be in the boat — tired, honest, still learning to notice Him. Ask for eyes: Lord, help me see that it is You. Then do the next small obedient thing. Cast the net where He says. “Apart from me you can do nothing” (John 15:5) is not a threat; it is an invitation back to dependence — the kind that ends at a shared meal rather than in shame.

A gentle way to begin this day

You do not need a perfect morning routine to answer Jesus’ invitation. Try these three simple movements:

Admit the empty net. Tell Him, without performance, where you feel spent or stuck. Empty-handed honesty is welcome on His shore.

Receive breakfast. Before you rush into producing, receive something from Him: a short reading of John 21:1–14, a quiet breath of gratitude, a remembered mercy. Let the day start with gift, not grind.

Stay near the fire. After He feeds them, Jesus tends Peter’s heart and then says, “Follow me” (John 21:19). Breakfast leads to vocation. Being loved leads to loving. Ask Him: Where are You inviting me to follow You today — in one concrete, kind step?

The invitation of daybreak

Just as day was breaking, Jesus stood on the shore. He still does — for anyone whose night felt long and whose morning feels unfinished. He does not shout from a distance. He prepares what we need and then opens His hands: Come and have breakfast.

So come. Leave the empty boat for a moment. Bring your hunger, your fatigue, your unfinished plans. The risen Christ is already here — nearer than your first worry, ready to feed you for the road ahead. Sit first. Receive first. Then rise, warmed by mercy, and follow.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in John 21:12, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
