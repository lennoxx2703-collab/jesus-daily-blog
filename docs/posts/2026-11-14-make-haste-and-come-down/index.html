Make Haste, and Come Down

November 14, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture Luke 19:5–6

  • saturday
  • morning
  • zacchaeus
  • grace
  • belonging
  • seeking

Saturday mornings have a different pace. No alarm, or a later one. Coffee that gets to stay warm. And sometimes, in that slower space, a quiet honesty shows up: I want to be closer to Jesus than I actually am. Maybe you’ve been watching Him from a little distance lately — reading about Him, listening to others talk about Him, curious but careful.

There was a man in Jericho who knew that feeling well.

“And Jesus entered and passed through Jericho. And, behold, there was a man named Zacchaeus, which was the chief among the publicans, and he was rich. And he sought to see Jesus who he was; and could not for the press, because he was little of stature. And he ran before, and climbed up into a sycomore tree to see him: for he was to pass that way.” — Luke 19:1–4 (KJV)

A man with a reputation

Zacchaeus wasn’t just a tax collector. He was the chief tax collector — the man who profited from everyone else’s cheating. In a town like Jericho, people knew his name and probably said it with a sneer. He had money, but he didn’t have friends in the crowd. When Jesus came through, nobody was going to make room for him at the front.

So he did something undignified. A wealthy grown man ran ahead and climbed a tree. Not to be seen — he was probably hoping the leaves would hide him — but just to see. He wanted to know who this Jesus was. That was all he dared to hope for: a glimpse from a branch.

Some of us live our faith from that branch. We want to see Jesus, but we’d rather He didn’t see us too closely. We know what’s in our record.

Jesus looked up

“And when Jesus came to the place, he looked up, and saw him, and said unto him, Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house. And he made haste, and came down, and received him joyfully.” — Luke 19:5–6 (KJV)

Out of a whole crowd pressing in, Jesus stopped at that exact tree. He looked up. And He said the man’s name.

Nobody had to introduce them. Jesus already knew who was hiding in the sycomore. And He didn’t say, “Clean up your business and then we’ll talk.” He didn’t give a lecture from the road. He invited Himself over: today I must abide at thy house. That word “must” is beautiful. Jesus speaks as though this visit was always part of the plan — as though He came through Jericho for this.

Zacchaeus came down joyfully. Not nervously, not ashamed. Joyfully. Being wanted by Jesus will do that.

Grace goes first

The crowd, of course, grumbled: “He’s gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner” (Luke 19:7). And they weren’t wrong about the sin. But they were wrong about what Jesus does with sinners.

Look at the order of the story. Jesus didn’t wait for Zacchaeus to change before He came to his house. He came first, and the change followed: “Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold” (Luke 19:8). Nobody demanded that. It poured out of a man who had just been loved before he deserved it.

That’s how it still works. We don’t climb down out of the tree because we’ve finally become good enough. We come down because He called our name, and His welcome changes us from the inside.

“For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.” — Luke 19:10 (KJV)

Zacchaeus thought he was the one seeking. It turned out he was the one being sought.

Come down this morning

If you’ve been watching from a distance, hear this as gently as it was first said: make haste, and come down. You don’t need a better reputation first. You don’t need to fix the week before you talk to Him. He knows your name, and He wants to be at your table today — at your actual house, in your actual Saturday.

Maybe coming down looks like a simple, honest prayer before the day gets busy: Jesus, here I am. Come in. Maybe it’s opening one of the Gospels and reading slowly, letting Him look at you as you look at Him. However it looks, He isn’t passing by. He has stopped at your tree.

A prayer for this morning: Lord Jesus, thank You for seeing me when I’d rather hide. Thank You for calling me by name and wanting to stay. I come down to You today, not because I’m ready, but because You asked. Come into my home, my plans, and my heart, and change whatever You find there. You came to seek and to save the lost — thank You for finding me. Amen.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Luke 19:5–6, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
