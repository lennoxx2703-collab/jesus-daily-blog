He Sat Down by the Well

October 5, 2026 · Midday · 3 min read

Scripture John 4:6–7

  • gospel
  • mercy
  • invitation
  • comfort

Noon is a strange hour. The morning’s good intentions have worn thin, the afternoon hasn’t started, and you’re usually in the middle of something ordinary: an errand, a lunch eaten standing up, a task you’d rather not do. It’s not the hour we expect anything holy to happen.

John tells us that’s exactly when Jesus sat down by a well in Samaria.

“Jacob’s well was there. Jesus therefore, being tired from his journey, sat down by the well. It was about the sixth hour. A woman of Samaria came to draw water. Jesus said to her, ‘Give me a drink.’” (John 4:6–7)

The hour nobody comes

The sixth hour is midday, the hottest part of the day. Women usually drew water in the cool of the morning or evening, together, talking as they went. This woman came alone, at noon. We learn later why: she had a history the whole town knew about (John 4:17–18). Noon was the hour she could get her water without the looks, the whispers, the conversations that stopped when she walked by.

Maybe you know something of that. Not necessarily the same story, but the same instinct: arranging your day so you don’t have to be fully seen. Keeping the hard parts of your life on a schedule where no one runs into them.

And there, in the hour she picked to be alone, was Jesus.

Tired, and waiting

I love that John says Jesus was tired. The Son of God sat down because His feet hurt and the road had been long. He doesn’t meet her from a throne. He meets her as someone who also needs something, and He starts by asking her for a favor: Give me a drink.

That small request crossed every line. A Jewish man, a Samaritan woman, alone, in public. She’s startled (John 4:9). But this is how Jesus often begins with us. Not with a lecture, but with a conversation. Not by demanding we clean up first, but by sitting down where we already are.

Seen all the way through

As they talk, Jesus gently names the part of her life she came at noon to hide. He doesn’t shame her. He doesn’t walk away. He simply tells the truth, and then keeps talking with her — about worship, about God, about Himself. To this woman, of all people, He says plainly who He is: “I am he, the one who speaks to you” (John 4:26).

Here is the miracle of that midday: she is fully known and not rejected. Most of us fear that if anyone saw everything, they’d leave. Jesus sees everything, and stays.

Watch what that does to her. She leaves her water jar behind and runs back into the very town she’d been avoiding:

“So the woman left her water pot, and went away into the city, and said to the people, ‘Come, see a man who told me everything that I did. Can this be the Christ?’” (John 4:28–29)

The thing she dreaded most, being told everything she ever did, became her good news. Not because her past disappeared, but because it had been brought into the light by Someone full of grace. Shame kept her hiding at noon. Being known by Jesus sent her back out in the daylight.

Your sixth hour

So wherever you are in the middle of this Monday, at a desk, in a car, in a kitchen, it’s worth knowing that Jesus has a habit of showing up at ordinary wells at inconvenient times. He isn’t waiting for you to arrive at the morning hour with everyone else, looking put together. He’s already sitting down, a little tired from the road, ready to talk.

You can tell Him the part you schedule around. He already knows it. What He offers isn’t exposure; it’s welcome.

Take a minute before the afternoon pulls you along. Something simple will do: Jesus, You see all of me. Thank You for staying. Help me stop hiding from You.

Then go back into your day the way she went back into her town, lighter, a little surprised, and no longer alone.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in John 4:6–7, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
