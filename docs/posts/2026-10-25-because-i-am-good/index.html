Because I Am Good

October 25, 2026 · Evening · 4 min read

Scripture Matthew 20:6–9, 15

  • evening
  • grace
  • generosity
  • comparison
  • the-eleventh-hour
  • sunday

Sunday evening has a way of making you count. The weekend is nearly gone. Monday is lining up its demands. And somewhere in the back of your mind a quiet ledger opens: what you got done, what you didn’t, who is further along than you, how late you are to everything that matters.

Jesus once told a story about exactly that kind of counting. It ends at evening, too.

“And about the eleventh hour he went out, and found others standing idle, and saith unto them, Why stand ye here all the day idle? They say unto him, Because no man hath hired us. He saith unto them, Go ye also into the vineyard; and whatsoever is right, that shall ye receive. So when even was come, the lord of the vineyard saith unto his steward, Call the labourers, and give them their hire, beginning from the last unto the first. And when they came that were hired about the eleventh hour, they received every man a penny.” (Matthew 20:6–9, KJV)

“Because no man hath hired us”

The eleventh hour is about five in the afternoon. The light is going. Anyone still standing in the marketplace at that time has spent the whole day being passed over. Picked last, then not picked at all.

Listen to their answer. They aren’t lazy. They’re unwanted. No man hath hired us. It’s one of the loneliest sentences in the Gospels, and it may sound familiar. The job that didn’t come through. The friendship that drifted. The sense that everyone else found their place in life hours ago, and you’re still standing at the edge of the square.

And notice who keeps going back out. Not a foreman. The owner himself. Early morning, the third hour, the sixth, the ninth, and then once more, when it hardly makes sense anymore. The God Jesus describes doesn’t stop looking for people just because the day is almost over.

Paid first, and paid in full

Then evening comes, and the owner does something odd on purpose. He pays the latecomers first, where everyone can see. One full day’s wage for one hour’s work.

That’s not a payroll mistake. It’s the point. The latecomers didn’t get a little bit of grace in proportion to their little bit of effort. They got the whole thing. That’s how God gives Himself to people: not in slices measured to our performance, but whole.

“Is thine eye evil, because I am good?”

The ones who worked all day grumbled, and if we’re honest, most of us understand them. Fairness feels like something we should be able to count on.

The owner answers gently. He calls one of them “friend.” He paid exactly what was agreed. Then he asks the question that sits at the heart of the story:

“Is it not lawful for me to do what I will with mine own? Is thine eye evil, because I am good?” (Matthew 20:15, KJV)

The trouble wasn’t that the all-day workers got too little. It was that they couldn’t bear someone else getting so much. Comparison had turned a full day’s pay into an insult.

That’s the trap of the Sunday-evening ledger. Whether you feel ahead or behind, the counting itself steals the joy. Either you resent the latecomer, or you are the latecomer and assume there’s nothing left for you. Jesus takes the ledger out of both sets of hands. The vineyard runs on the owner’s goodness, not on our hours.

For tonight

So if you feel late this evening, late to faith, to healing, to getting your life in order, hear the owner’s words: Go ye also. There’s still daylight enough. There’s still a place for you in the vineyard, and what He gives won’t be a leftover.

And if you’ve been working since dawn and feel the old grumble rising, try letting it go. Your penny was never in danger. Being with the owner all day was its own gift.

Before you sleep, put the ledger down. You could pray something simple:

Lord, You came looking for me when no one else did. Thank You that You give whole, not in pieces. Take my counting and my comparing, and let me rest tonight in Your goodness. Amen.

“So the last shall be first, and the first last” (Matthew 20:16, KJV). In His kingdom, that’s not a threat. It’s good news for everyone who ever stood waiting at the end of the day.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Matthew 20:6–9, 15, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
