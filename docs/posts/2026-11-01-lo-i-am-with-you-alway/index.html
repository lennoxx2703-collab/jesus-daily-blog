Lo, I Am with You Alway

November 1, 2026 · Evening · 4 min read

Scripture Matthew 28:16–20

  • sunday
  • evening
  • presence
  • doubt
  • new-week
  • great-commission

Sunday evenings have a feeling all their own. The weekend is folding itself up. Somewhere in the back of your mind, Monday is already clearing its throat: the inbox, the alarm, the list you’ve been not-quite-thinking about all day. And tonight it isn’t just a new week. It’s a new month, with November’s shorter days stretching out ahead.

Matthew ends his Gospel on a hillside, with a small group of people who were also standing at the edge of something new:

“Then the eleven disciples went away into Galilee, into a mountain where Jesus had appointed them. And when they saw him, they worshipped him: but some doubted. And Jesus came and spake unto them, saying, All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth. Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost: Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen.” (Matthew 28:16–20, KJV)

“But some doubted”

I love that Matthew left this in. These are the last verses of his book. He could have ended with a clean picture of eleven unwavering men on their knees. Instead he tells the truth: they worshipped, and some of them doubted. In the same moment, on the same mountain, looking at the risen Jesus.

Maybe that’s where you are tonight. You believe, mostly. You came to Him today, in church or in a quiet corner or just in a passing thought. And there’s still a part of you that isn’t sure, that wonders whether any of it will hold up when Monday morning arrives.

Notice what Jesus does with those doubting disciples. He doesn’t send them away until they’re more certain. He doesn’t sort the worshippers from the wonderers. Matthew says simply, “Jesus came and spake unto them.” He comes closer. He speaks to all of them, doubters included, and He gives all of them the same work and the same promise.

The work is real, and so is the help

What He asks of them is enormous: all nations, all things He commanded. Eleven ordinary people, one of them so recently a denier, sent out into the whole world. If you’ve ever looked at the week ahead and felt it was too much for you, imagine hearing that.

But the commission is sandwiched between two things that make it bearable. Before it: “All power is given unto me.” The weight doesn’t rest on them. After it: “Lo, I am with you alway.” They won’t carry it alone.

Your week probably doesn’t involve all nations. It might involve a difficult colleague, a sick parent, a child who won’t sleep, a decision you’ve been putting off, or just the slow grind of ordinary faithfulness. Whatever it is, the same two things are true. He holds the authority, and He comes along.

“Alway”

The old King James word is lovely: alway. Literally, all the way. Not just on the mountain. Not just on Sundays. Not just on the days you feel spiritual or the weeks that go well. All the way, every day, “even unto the end of the world.”

That includes tomorrow at 7 a.m. It includes the meeting you’re dreading and the drive home in the dark. It includes the moments you forget about Him entirely, because His being with you never depended on your remembering. Years later, another writer put it this way: “for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee” (Hebrews 13:5, KJV).

Before you close the weekend

So tonight, before the week begins, try this. Name the one thing about the coming days that worries you most. Say it to Him honestly, doubts and all, the way those disciples stood on that hill. Then hear His last words as if they were spoken to you, because they were: Lo, I am with you alway.

You don’t have to feel ready for Monday. You only have to walk into it with Him. And He has already promised to be there when you arrive.

Good night. He’s with you, all the way.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Matthew 28:16–20, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
