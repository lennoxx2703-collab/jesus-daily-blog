Afterward He Repented, and Went

November 16, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture Matthew 21:28–31

  • monday
  • morning
  • repentance
  • obedience
  • grace
  • new-start

Monday mornings can feel like a verdict. The week ahead is already lined up, and somewhere in the back of your mind is a list of things you meant to do last week and didn’t. Prayers you skipped. A conversation you avoided. A quiet “no” you gave God when He nudged you.

Jesus told a very short story for mornings like this one.

“But what think ye? A certain man had two sons; and he came to the first, and said, Son, go work to day in my vineyard. He answered and said, I will not: but afterward he repented, and went. And he came to the second, and said likewise. And he answered and said, I go, sir: and went not. Whether of them twain did the will of his father? They say unto him, The first.” — Matthew 21:28–31 (KJV)

Two answers, two roads

The first son is blunt. I will not. No excuse, no polite softening. If you were the father, it would sting.

The second son is everything you’d want on the surface. I go, sir. Respectful, quick, agreeable. And then he simply doesn’t show up.

Jesus asks the people listening which one actually did what the father wanted, and the answer is obvious even to His critics: the one who went. Not the one with the better words. The one whose feet eventually found the vineyard.

The word that changes everything

There is one small word in this story that I keep coming back to: afterward.

“He answered and said, I will not: but afterward he repented, and went.”

The first son’s “no” was real. Jesus doesn’t pretend it didn’t happen. But it wasn’t the last word. Something shifted in him — maybe on his walk, maybe lying awake — and he turned around. That is what repentance is: not a feeling of being terrible, but a change of mind that turns into a change of direction.

And notice what the father does not do in the story. He doesn’t stand at the gate refusing to let the son in because of what he said earlier. The vineyard is still open. The work is still there. The invitation still stands — “go work to day.”

If you said “no” to God last week, or last year, or for most of your life, hear this: your first answer doesn’t have to be your final one. There is an “afterward” available to you this morning.

Not just nice words

The story also has a gentle warning for those of us who are good at saying the right things. It’s possible to sing every song on Sunday, nod at every sermon, and say “Yes, Lord” with real sincerity — and then never quite go.

Jesus isn’t asking for a better performance. He’s asking for the walk out to the vineyard. James puts it plainly: “But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only” (James 1:22, KJV). Not to earn the Father’s love, but because love naturally wants to show up where the Father is working.

Hear the Father’s first word

Before the command, before the vineyard, before either son answered, the father said one word: Son.

That’s how this story starts, and it’s how your Monday starts too. You are not walking into this week as a hired hand trying to prove yourself. You’re a child being invited into your Father’s work — with Him, not for Him at a distance. And His mercy is fresh for exactly this kind of morning: “his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness” (Lamentations 3:22–23, KJV).

For today

Maybe there’s one thing you’ve been saying “no” or “later” to. A person to call. A habit to lay down. A few minutes of honest prayer you keep postponing. You don’t have to fix the whole week. Just take the first few steps toward the vineyard today.

Jesus, I’ve said “no” to You more than I like to admit, and sometimes I’ve said “yes” and not meant it. Thank You that my first answer isn’t the end of the story. Turn me around this morning, and walk with me into whatever work You have for me today. Amen.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Matthew 21:28–31, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
