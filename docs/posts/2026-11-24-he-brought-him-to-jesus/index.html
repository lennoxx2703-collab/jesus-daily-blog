He Brought Him to Jesus

November 24, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture John 1:40–42

  • tuesday
  • morning
  • identity
  • calling
  • witness
  • new-name

Some mornings you look in the mirror and see exactly who you’ve always been. Same habits. Same temper. Same tired patterns you promised to leave behind last year. And a small voice says: This is just who you are.

There’s a short scene at the very beginning of John’s Gospel that answers that voice. It takes three verses. It has two brothers in it. And it ends with Jesus saying something about a man that the man himself could not yet see.

The brother who went first

“One of the two which heard John speak, and followed him, was Andrew, Simon Peter’s brother. He first findeth his own brother Simon, and saith unto him, We have found the Messias, which is, being interpreted, the Christ.” (John 1:40–41, KJV)

Notice how John introduces him: Andrew, Simon Peter’s brother. Even in his own story, Andrew is described by someone else. He’s the one who will always be mentioned second.

But Andrew is the one who met Jesus first. He spent an afternoon with Him, and something in him was settled. And the very first thing he did with that discovery was go looking for his brother.

Not a crowd. Not a stage. His own brother — the loud one, the impulsive one, the one everybody noticed.

“And he brought him to Jesus.” (John 1:42, KJV)

Six words. That’s Andrew’s whole ministry in one line. He doesn’t preach a sermon. He doesn’t fix Simon. He just brings him to Jesus and steps back.

We meet Andrew a few more times in John, and he’s always doing the same thing. When five thousand people are hungry, Andrew is the one who notices a boy: “There is a lad here, which hath five barley loaves, and two small fishes” (John 6:9, KJV). When some Greeks want to see Jesus, Philip tells Andrew, and “Andrew and Philip tell Jesus” (John 12:22, KJV). Andrew is a bringer. He keeps walking people, and small things, toward the One who can do something with them.

If you’ve ever felt like the background person — the one who isn’t gifted with words, who doesn’t lead the meeting — Andrew is good company. Some of the most important things in the Kingdom happen in six-word sentences: He brought him to Jesus.

The look that sees ahead

Then comes the moment I can’t get past:

“And when Jesus beheld him, he said, Thou art Simon the son of Jona: thou shalt be called Cephas, which is by interpretation, A stone.” (John 1:42, KJV)

When Jesus beheld him. Not glanced. Beheld. He looked at Simon long enough to see all of him — and then He said two things.

First: Thou art Simon the son of Jona. I know exactly who you are. Your name, your father, your history. Nothing hidden.

Second: Thou shalt be called Cephas. A stone. A rock.

Now, Simon was not a rock. Read on through the Gospels and you’ll find a man who sinks in the water, says the wrong thing at the wrong time, swings a sword in a garden, and denies Jesus three times by a fire. Rock is the last word anyone would have picked for him.

But Jesus didn’t name him by his track record. He named him by what grace was going to make of him. Thou art — and then — thou shalt be.

What He sees in you this morning

That’s the shape of how Jesus looks at you, too. He sees thou art without flinching: the habits, the history, the things you’d rather nobody knew. And He doesn’t stop there. He speaks a thou shalt be over you that you haven’t grown into yet.

Paul put it this way: “being confident of this very thing, that he who began a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ” (Philippians 1:6, WEB).

So when the mirror says this is just who you are, you can answer: That’s who I was when He found me. It isn’t the last word. The last word belongs to the One who renamed a fisherman before the fisherman had done a single thing to deserve it.

And maybe today there’s someone you could be an Andrew for. Not to fix them. Just to say, gently, I’ve found Someone — and walk with them toward Him.

A prayer for this Tuesday

Lord Jesus, You see who I am, all of it, and You still speak a new name over me. Help me believe Your “shalt be” more than my “always have been.” Make me a bringer like Andrew — someone who quietly walks others toward You. Shape me today into what You already see. Amen.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in John 1:40–42, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
