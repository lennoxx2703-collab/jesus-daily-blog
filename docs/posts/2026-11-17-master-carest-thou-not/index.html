Master, Carest Thou Not?

November 17, 2026 · Midday · 4 min read

Scripture Mark 4:37–40

  • tuesday
  • midday
  • storms
  • fear
  • honesty
  • peace

Some days the storm doesn’t wait for evening. It blows in at midday — a phone call, an email, a test result, a sharp word at work — and suddenly the calm morning feels like it belonged to someone else.

The disciples knew that feeling. In Mark 4, the day had started with Jesus teaching by the sea. As evening came, He said something simple: “Let us pass over unto the other side” (Mark 4:35). Notice that. The trip was His idea. They got into the boat because He told them to.

And then:

“And there arose a great storm of wind, and the waves beat into the ship, so that it was now full. And he was in the hinder part of the ship, asleep on a pillow: and they awake him, and say unto him, Master, carest thou not that we perish?” (Mark 4:37–38, KJV)

Several of these men were fishermen. They knew this lake. If they were terrified, the storm was real. Water was filling the boat. And there was Jesus — asleep, on a cushion, in the back.

The rawest question

“Master, carest thou not that we perish?”

That’s not a polite prayer. It’s not even really a request. It’s an accusation wrapped in fear: Don’t You care?

Maybe you’ve asked it too. Maybe not out loud, but somewhere underneath: Lord, I’m doing what You asked, I’m in the boat You put me in, and it’s filling with water. Are You even paying attention?

What I love about this passage is that the Bible keeps the question in. Mark doesn’t clean it up. The disciples said it, Jesus heard it, and the story goes on. Your honest, frightened, half-angry prayer is not too much for Him either.

He arose

“And he arose, and rebuked the wind, and said unto the sea, Peace, be still. And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm.” (Mark 4:39, KJV)

Jesus doesn’t argue with them first. He doesn’t lecture them about tone. He gets up and deals with the storm.

Three words: Peace, be still. The wind that had ruled the night stopped at the voice of a carpenter from Nazareth. And Mark says there was not just calm, but “a great calm.” A great storm, met by a greater calm.

Only after that does Jesus turn to them:

“And he said unto them, Why are ye so fearful? how is it that ye have no faith?” (Mark 4:40, KJV)

It’s a real question, but it’s asked in the quiet, not in the storm. He’s not scolding drowning men. He’s inviting rescued men to think about who is in the boat with them.

What His sleep was saying

Here’s the thing the disciples couldn’t see in the moment: Jesus asleep wasn’t Jesus careless. It was Jesus at rest in His Father’s hands. He had said, “Let us pass over unto the other side.” He meant to arrive. The storm was not going to cancel His word.

So the answer to “Carest thou not?” turned out to be: I care so much that I’m in the boat with you. And I’m not worried about how this ends.

Peter, who was almost certainly in that boat, later wrote: “Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you” (1 Peter 5:7). That sounds like a man who learned something on the water.

For the middle of your day

If a storm has come up since this morning, here is a small invitation:

  1. Ask your real question. Whatever it is. He can hear it.
  2. Remember whose idea the boat was. If you’re following Him, you’re not out here alone or by accident.
  3. Listen for His word over the wind. Sometimes He stills the storm outside. Sometimes He stills the one inside first. Either way, His voice is stronger than the noise.

You don’t have to pretend the waves aren’t real. You only have to remember Who is in the back of the boat.

Jesus, there’s a storm in my day, and honestly, sometimes I wonder if You care. Thank You that You let me ask. Arise in this, Lord. Speak peace over what I can’t control, and quiet my heart while I wait. You are in the boat with me. Amen.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in Mark 4:37–40, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
