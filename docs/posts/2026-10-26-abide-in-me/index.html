Abide in Me

October 26, 2026 · Evening · 4 min read

Scripture John 15:4–5, 9

  • evening
  • abiding
  • rest
  • fruitfulness
  • the-true-vine
  • monday

The first day of the week has a particular kind of tired. Not the heavy tiredness of Friday, but the strained kind. You spent Monday trying to get ahead: answering, fixing, planning, pushing. And now the evening is here and part of you is still clenched, as if the day’s results depend on how hard you can keep squeezing.

On the last night before the cross, Jesus said something to His friends that speaks straight into that clench.

“Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me. I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.” (John 15:4–5, KJV)

Words for the walk at night

It is worth remembering when He said this. The supper was over. Judas had gone out into the dark. A few verses earlier Jesus had said, “Arise, let us go hence” (John 14:31), and many think He spoke these words on the way, perhaps passing vines along the road toward the Kidron valley. His disciples were frightened, confused, and about to fail Him.

He didn’t give them a strategy. He didn’t hand them a list of things to do harder. He gave them a picture: a branch resting in a vine.

A branch doesn’t strain

Have you ever watched a grape branch try? Of course not. It doesn’t. A branch doesn’t grit its teeth to make fruit. It doesn’t lie awake worrying about its yield. It simply stays connected, and the life of the vine flows through it. The fruit comes as a result of the connection, not as a result of the effort.

That is a startling thing to hear at the end of a productive-or-not Monday. So much of our day runs on the opposite idea: that everything good in our lives is something we have to generate on our own. Jesus is gentle but blunt about it: without me ye can do nothing. Not “a little less.” Nothing.

That sounds hard at first. Actually, it’s freeing. If the fruit was never supposed to come from your own straining, then you can stop straining. Your job tonight isn’t to produce. It’s to stay.

What abiding looks like at 9 p.m.

“Abide” is a homely word. It means to remain, to dwell, to stay put. It’s the word for living somewhere, not visiting.

So abiding in Jesus this evening may look very ordinary:

  • Telling Him plainly how the day went, the good parts and the parts you’d rather forget.
  • Handing over the things you couldn’t finish, instead of rehearsing them in bed.
  • Reading a few of His words slowly, and letting them be the last thing you think about.
  • Simply saying, “Lord, I’m staying here with You,” and meaning it.

None of that is impressive. That’s the point. A branch is not impressive. It just stays where life is.

Continue in my love

A few verses later Jesus tells them where exactly they are meant to stay:

“As the Father hath loved me, so have I loved you: continue ye in my love.” (John 15:9, KJV)

The vine is not a principle or a program. It is His love. The same love the Father has for the Son, Jesus has for you. You don’t abide in your performance, or in how the week is shaping up, or in what other people think of your Monday. You abide in being loved.

And notice who keeps the branches. Jesus says His Father is the husbandman (John 15:1), the one who tends the vineyard. You are not the gardener of your own life. You are a branch, cared for by Someone who knows what He’s doing.

Tonight

So let the clench go. Whatever got done today, whatever didn’t, the fruit was never going to come from squeezing harder. It comes from staying close to Him.

Before you sleep, try praying something simple:

Jesus, I’m tired of trying to make everything happen on my own. I’m staying with You tonight. Let Your life run through mine tomorrow. Amen.

Then rest. Branches grow while they’re resting too.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in John 15:4–5, 9, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
