Sir, We Would See Jesus

October 31, 2026 · Morning · 3 min read

Scripture John 12:20–26

  • saturday
  • seeking
  • desire
  • cross
  • morning

Saturday mornings are slower. No alarm, maybe. No inbox you have to answer before coffee. The house is quieter, or louder in a different way, and there’s room to ask what you actually want from the day.

John tells us about some people who knew exactly what they wanted.

It was the week of Passover, the last one before the cross. Jerusalem was packed with pilgrims, and among them were a few outsiders:

“And there were certain Greeks among them that came up to worship at the feast: The same came therefore to Philip, which was of Bethsaida of Galilee, and desired him, saying, Sir, we would see Jesus.” (John 12:20–21, KJV)

That’s the whole request. Not “we’d like to debate Him.” Not “we have a list of questions.” Just we would see Jesus.

These were people who didn’t belong, not quite. They weren’t from Galilee or Judea. They had come to worship a God whose temple courts mostly kept them at a distance. And yet they had heard enough about this Man that they came looking for someone who might bring them close. They chose Philip, maybe because he had a Greek name, maybe because he looked approachable. Philip wasn’t sure what to do, so he found Andrew, and the two of them went to Jesus together.

I love that small, clumsy chain of people. Someone wants to see Jesus, and ordinary disciples who don’t have it all figured out simply pass the request along. That’s still how it works. Most of us came to Him because somebody, somewhere, walked a question over to Jesus on our behalf.

The answer they didn’t expect

Then Jesus answers, and it doesn’t sound like a reply at first:

“The hour is come, that the Son of man should be glorified. Verily, verily, I say unto you, Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit.” (John 12:23–24, KJV)

They asked to see Him, and He talks about a seed going into the ground.

Here’s what I think He was saying: You want to see Me? Then look at what’s coming. The clearest view of Jesus isn’t a quick meeting in a crowded courtyard. It’s the cross. A few verses later He says, “And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me” (John 12:32, KJV). All men. Greeks included. Outsiders included. You and me included.

The seed had to fall so that the harvest could reach people far beyond the walls of that city. Those Greeks were the first trickle of a crowd that would come from every nation. Their request was a sign that the hour had arrived.

Where to see Him today

Then Jesus adds an invitation:

“If any man serve me, let him follow me; and where I am, there shall also my servant be: if any man serve me, him will my Father honour.” (John 12:26, KJV)

Seeing Jesus, it turns out, isn’t a one-time glimpse. It’s following. It’s going where He goes, which means staying close to Him in the slow places, the costly places, the places where something in us has to die so something better can grow.

So on a slower Saturday morning, maybe the best prayer is the oldest one: Sir, I would see Jesus. Not more information about Him. Him.

You might see Him in a Gospel page you read with your coffee. In a person you serve today without being thanked. In a habit you let go of, a grudge you bury, a small surrender that feels like a seed going into the dark. That’s where He promised to be.

And if you feel like an outsider, like someone who came to the feast but is standing at the edge of the courtyard, remember that those were exactly the people whose request made Jesus say the hour is come. You are not too far away. He was lifted up to draw you in.

Ask today. He still answers.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in John 12:20–26, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
