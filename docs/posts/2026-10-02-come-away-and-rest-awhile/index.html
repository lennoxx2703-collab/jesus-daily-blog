Come Away and Rest Awhile

October 2, 2026 · Midday · 4 min read

Scripture Mark 6:31

  • rest
  • gospel
  • discipleship
  • invitation

Friday midday has a particular weight. The week has already spent a lot of you. Work is still open. Messages still arrive. Good intentions from Monday may feel thinner now. You might be doing faithful things and still feel the quiet drain of coming and going — movement without margin, effort without a clean pause.

Mark’s Gospel knows that kind of day. The apostles have just returned from ministry. They gather to Jesus and report everything they have done and taught. The work was real. The fruit was real. And still, the pace had outrun their bodies:

“He said to them, ‘Come away into a deserted place, and rest awhile.’ For there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat.” (Mark 6:31)

Jesus does not congratulate them into one more push. He invites them out of the traffic — with Him.

The Lord sees the fatigue under the fruit

Notice what Jesus notices. The disciples are not failing; they are full of report. Yet Mark tells us the crowds kept coming and going until even a meal was hard to protect. Success had not protected their souls. Usefulness had not guaranteed rest.

Friday can feel the same. You may have accomplished much this week and still feel thin. You may have loved people well and still have no quiet left. Jesus is not impressed only by output. He sees the person who produced it. Before He multiplies bread for thousands later in the chapter, He first tends the ones who already spent themselves for others.

That is kindness with authority. The Shepherd does not merely send; He also gathers. He does not treat your tiredness as a character flaw. He names it and makes room.

Rest awhile is not escape from love

“Come away” can sound like luxury in a world that rewards constant availability. But Jesus is not offering escape from love. He is offering love that knows how to stop. A deserted place, in Mark, is simply space apart — room to breathe, to be with Him, to remember that discipleship is companionship before it is productivity.

“Rest awhile” is a gentle phrase. It is not forever. It is not quitting the mission. It is a holy interval. The kingdom does not advance by burning out its servants. The same Jesus who later feeds the multitude first feeds His friends with permission to pause.

So midday rest with Christ is not laziness dressed in religious language. It is obedience. It is trusting that the world will not collapse if you step aside long enough to be with the One who holds it.

What coming away can look like today

You may not have a boat or a shoreline. You may have fifteen minutes, a closed door, a walk around the block, or a lunch eaten without a screen. Coming away is less about geography than about attention. Leave the “many coming and going” — even briefly — and let Jesus have you.

Tell Him the unfinished list without pretending it is finished. Bring the fatigue without polishing it. Then receive His word again: Come away… rest awhile. Sit with that invitation until your shoulders drop a little. Read Mark 6:30–32 slowly. Pray one honest sentence: Jesus, I have been coming and going. Come away with me now.

Afterward, return to the afternoon — but as someone who has been invited, not merely driven. Answer the next message as a disciple who has already been received. Carry the remaining work knowing the Lord of the harvest also guards the rest of His workers.

A hopeful takeaway

Carry these three small steps into the rest of Friday:

  1. Name the traffic. Admit what has been “coming and going” in you — tasks, people, noise, self-pressure.
  2. Accept the invitation. Take a short, concrete pause with Jesus: Scripture, silence, a walk, a simple prayer of return.
  3. Re-enter with Him. Do the next needed thing from companionship, not from the fear that rest was irresponsible.

The middle of Friday does not have to be a corridor you sprint alone. The apostles were busy with holy work, and Jesus still said, Come away. He still knows when you have had no leisure even to eat. He still makes a place beside Himself for tired friends.

Come away into a quieter place — even a small one — and rest awhile. The unfinished week is safer in His hands than in your clenched ones.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in Mark 6:31, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
