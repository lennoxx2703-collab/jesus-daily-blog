He Turned Back

November 21, 2026 · Evening · 4 min read

Scripture Luke 17:15–16

  • saturday
  • evening
  • gratitude
  • healing
  • worship
  • rest

There’s a moment on Saturday evening when the week finally stops moving. The errands are done or abandoned, the inbox can wait until Monday, and for the first time in days you can actually look back at where you’ve been.

Most of us skip that moment. We go straight from the rush into the next thing — a screen, a plan, a worry about next week. But tonight, I want to sit with ten men on a road outside a village, because one of them did something the other nine didn’t.

“And as he entered into a certain village, there met him ten men that were lepers, which stood afar off: and they lifted up their voices, and said, Jesus, Master, have mercy on us.” — Luke 17:12–13 (KJV)

They stood afar off. That’s what leprosy did — it put distance between you and everyone else. You lived at the edges, you called out from a safe distance, you learned that nobody came close. And still, they cried out to Jesus by name.

Jesus didn’t touch them this time. He simply said, “Go shew yourselves unto the priests.” No visible healing yet. Just a word, and a road to walk. “And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed” (Luke 17:14).

As they went. Somewhere along the way, someone looked down at his hands and saw clean skin. Then another. Then all ten.

The one who stopped

“And one of them, when he saw that he was healed, turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan.” — Luke 17:15–16 (KJV)

I don’t think the nine were bad men. They were doing exactly what Jesus told them to do. They had a priest to see, families to go home to, a whole life to restart. Who could blame them for hurrying?

But one of them stopped in the middle of the road. He saw that he was healed — really saw it — and it turned him around. The man who used to stand afar off now ran close, loud and unashamed, and fell on his face at Jesus’ feet.

That’s what gratitude does. It turns you back toward the Giver. The nine received the gift. The one received the gift and the Giver.

“Where are the nine?”

“And Jesus answering said, Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?” — Luke 17:17 (KJV)

There’s something tender in that question. Jesus isn’t keeping score. He’s missing people. He healed ten because He loved ten, and He’d have welcomed all ten back at His feet. He notices who comes back — not because He needs our thanks, but because He wants us.

And then He says something He didn’t say to the other nine: “Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole” (Luke 17:19). All ten were cleansed. One was made whole. The skin was healed on the road; the heart was healed at His feet.

Turning back tonight

So here’s a small invitation for this Saturday evening. Before the week slips into the past, turn around and look at it.

Where did He carry you that you hardly noticed at the time? The conversation that went better than you feared. The bill that got paid. The friend who texted at exactly the right moment. The strength to get out of bed on the day you didn’t want to. Some of those healings happened “as you went” — so quietly you walked right past them.

You don’t need a loud voice tonight (though you’re welcome to one). Just name a few of them to Him. Thank You for this. And this. And for being with me in that.

And if this week mostly hurt — if you’re still standing afar off, still waiting for clean skin — you can turn back, too. The Samaritan came to Jesus as an outsider, and Jesus didn’t send him away. Bring Him the week as it really was. “O give thanks unto the LORD; for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever” (Psalm 107:1) isn’t only for good weeks. His mercy is the reason we can come back at all.

Tomorrow is the Lord’s day. Let tonight be the turning around — back down the road, back to His feet, where wholeness lives.

Lord Jesus, You’ve been good to me this week in more ways than I saw. I’m turning back to say thank You. Make me not only healed, but whole. Amen.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Luke 17:15–16, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
