Your Names Are Written in Heaven

November 17, 2026 · Morning · 3 min read

Scripture Luke 10:17–20

  • tuesday
  • morning
  • identity
  • joy
  • belonging
  • rest

Tuesday morning has a particular feel. Monday’s fresh start is already behind you, the week is fully underway, and somewhere in the back of your mind a quiet scoreboard is running: how did yesterday go, how will today go, am I keeping up?

There’s a moment in Luke 10 that speaks right into that.

Jesus had sent out seventy of His followers, two by two, ahead of Him into the towns He was about to visit. They went with almost nothing — no purse, no bag, no backup plan. And it went better than any of them could have imagined. Luke tells us:

“And the seventy returned again with joy, saying, Lord, even the devils are subject unto us through thy name.” (Luke 10:17, KJV)

You can almost hear them, out of breath, talking over each other. It worked. People were healed. Darkness actually ran. This was the best report any disciple had ever brought back.

And Jesus doesn’t brush it off. He shares their joy. He even tells them what He saw: “I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven” (Luke 10:18). What they did mattered.

But then He says something that must have surprised them:

“Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.” (Luke 10:20, KJV)

Think about the timing. He doesn’t say this after a failure, to comfort them. He says it after their greatest success. Right at the moment when it would be easiest to build their joy on results, Jesus gently moves the foundation.

Why move the foundation?

Because results change. The seventy would have other days — days when nobody listened, when doors stayed shut, when they shook the dust off their feet and walked on. If their joy rested on “it worked,” then their joy would rise and fall with every town.

Jesus wanted them to have a joy that didn’t go up and down with the scoreboard. So He pointed them to the one thing about them that was not going to change on a bad Tuesday: their names were written in heaven.

Not earned there. Written there. Written by Someone else, before they ever set out. The success was real, but it was the fruit. The name was the root.

What this means for your Tuesday

Maybe today will go well. The meeting lands, the kids cooperate, the work flows, the conversation you’ve been dreading turns out kind. Thank God for that — Jesus rejoiced with the seventy, and He rejoices with you.

But maybe today won’t go well. Maybe you’ll get to the evening feeling like you accomplished nothing anyone would write down.

Here is what’s true either way: if you belong to Jesus, your name is already written down. Not on a performance review, not on a to-do list you finished, but in heaven. The Father knows it. He wrote it. A hard day can’t erase it, and a great day can’t add to it.

That’s not a reason to stop caring about the day. It’s the thing that sets you free to give the day your best without being crushed by it. You don’t have to win today in order to be His. You get to walk into today as His.

Right after this, Luke says something lovely: “In that hour Jesus rejoiced in spirit” (Luke 10:21). The Lord Himself was glad — not mainly about what His disciples had done, but about the Father revealing Himself to ordinary, “babes”-like people. People like them. People like us.

A small practice

Before the day speeds up, try this. Say your own name quietly, and then say: “Written in heaven.” Let that be the first fact of your Tuesday, before the scoreboard starts running.

Then go and do the work in front of you, gladly.

Lord Jesus, thank You that my worth today is not on the line. Thank You that my name is held by You, not by how this day goes. Let me rejoice in that first. Then send me out, and let whatever good I do today be fruit from that root. Amen.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Luke 10:17–20, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
