I Saw Thee

October 28, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture John 1:47–50

  • wednesday
  • being-known
  • honesty
  • faith
  • morning

Wednesday is the middle of the week, the point where nobody is really watching anymore. The Monday energy is gone, the weekend is still far away, and most of what you do today will happen without an audience. Emails answered, dishes done, a quiet worry carried from one room to the next.

John tells us about a man who thought he was unseen, too.

His name was Nathanael. His friend Philip came running with big news: “We have found him, of whom Moses in the law, and the prophets, did write, Jesus of Nazareth” (John 1:45, KJV). And Nathanael, honest to a fault, said what he was thinking: “Can there any good thing come out of Nazareth?” (John 1:46, KJV).

It’s not a very spiritual first response. It’s skeptical, a little snobby even. But Philip didn’t argue. He just said, “Come and see.” So Nathanael came.

“Jesus saw Nathanael coming to him, and saith of him, Behold an Israelite indeed, in whom is no guile! Nathanael saith unto him, Whence knowest thou me? Jesus answered and said unto him, Before that Philip called thee, when thou wast under the fig tree, I saw thee.” (John 1:47–48, KJV)

I love that Jesus doesn’t start by correcting the Nazareth comment. He starts by naming what is good in Nathanael: no guile. No pretending. The man who had just doubted Him out loud is praised for being honest. Jesus would rather have an honest skeptic than a polished pretender.

And then comes the line that undoes him: under the fig tree, I saw thee.

We don’t know what Nathanael was doing under that fig tree. Praying, maybe. Thinking. Sitting with a question he’d never said to anyone. In those days a fig tree was a common place for quiet reflection and reading the Scriptures, a little patch of shade where a person could be alone with God. Whatever happened there, it was private. Nobody else knew.

But Jesus knew. Before Philip called him, before Nathanael took one step toward Him, Jesus had already seen him.

That’s the part to carry into this ordinary Wednesday. You were seen before you came. Before you prayed this morning, before you opened this page, before you got your thoughts in order, Jesus saw you under your own fig tree: the quiet places, the unspoken questions, the things you think about when nobody’s around.

David put it this way centuries earlier:

“O LORD, thou hast searched me, and known me. Thou knowest my downsitting and mine uprising, thou understandest my thought afar off. Thou compassest my path and my lying down, and art acquainted with all my ways.” (Psalm 139:1–3, KJV)

For some of us, being fully known sounds frightening. If He sees everything, He sees the doubts and the mess too. But look at how Jesus actually uses what He knows. He doesn’t expose Nathanael. He welcomes him. The knowledge comes with a smile, not a file of charges.

Nathanael’s response is immediate: “Rabbi, thou art the Son of God; thou art the King of Israel” (John 1:49, KJV). The skeptic becomes a worshipper in one sentence, not because he was argued into it, but because he was seen.

And Jesus answers with a promise:

“Because I said unto thee, I saw thee under the fig tree, believest thou? thou shalt see greater things than these.” (John 1:50, KJV)

Being seen is only the beginning. There’s more ahead.

So today, you don’t have to perform for God. You can bring Him your honest Wednesday self, even the “can any good thing come out of this?” questions. He already knows them. He’d rather hear them from you, without guile, than not at all.

And as you go through the quiet, unwatched hours, remember that they aren’t unwatched. Not in a stern, surveillance kind of way, but the way a friend notices you across the room and is glad you’re there.

Here’s a short prayer for the morning:

Jesus, You saw me before I came to You. You know my fig tree, my quiet questions and my honest doubts. Thank You that being known by You is safe. Help me come to You without pretending today, and open my eyes to the greater things You still want to show me. Amen.

Go gently into the middle of the week. Whatever shade you sit under today, He sees you there.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in John 1:47–50, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
