For Joy Thereof

October 19, 2026 · Midday · 4 min read

Scripture Matthew 13:44–46

  • midday
  • joy
  • kingdom
  • parables
  • treasure

By the middle of a Monday, most of us are somewhere in the field. Not a field of wheat, maybe, but the ordinary ground of the day: the emails, the errands, the meeting that ran long, the dishes from last night. It’s work, and it can feel like it belongs to somebody else.

Jesus told a tiny story about exactly that kind of afternoon.

“Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto treasure hid in a field; the which when a man hath found, he hideth, and for joy thereof goeth and selleth all that he hath, and buyeth that field.” (Matthew 13:44, KJV)

Picture the man. He isn’t on a treasure hunt. He’s probably a hired hand, turning soil in a field he doesn’t own, and his plough or spade hits something that isn’t a rock. He brushes off the dirt, and there it is. In one ordinary moment, in the middle of an ordinary workday, everything changes.

Then Jesus tells a second story, almost the opposite:

“Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto a merchant man, seeking goodly pearls: who, when he had found one pearl of great price, went and sold all that he had, and bought it.” (Matthew 13:45–46, KJV)

The merchant has been searching for years. He knows pearls. He has seen hundreds of good ones. And then he finds the one that makes all the others look ordinary.

One man stumbles onto the treasure. The other searches his whole life for it. Jesus seems to be saying that both roads are real. Some of us were found by Him when we weren’t looking at all. Some of us went looking, through questions and doubts and long seasons of seeking. Either way, the ending is the same: when you actually see what He is worth, everything else gets rearranged.

The two words that matter most

It would be easy to read this parable as a story about cost. “Sold all that he had” sounds heavy. But Jesus tucks two words into the middle of the sentence that change everything: for joy.

The man doesn’t sell his things through gritted teeth. He isn’t sighing about everything he’s giving up. He’s practically running to the market, because he knows what’s under the ground. To everyone watching, he looks reckless. To him, it’s the best deal he’ll ever make.

That’s the difference between religion as a burden and following Jesus as a discovery. If you think of faith mainly as a list of things you have to give up, it will always feel like loss. But if you’ve caught even a glimpse of Jesus Himself, His mercy, His nearness, the way He looks at people the world walks past, then letting go of lesser things starts to feel less like sacrifice and more like making room.

Paul, who gave up a lot, put it this way: “Yea doubtless, and I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord” (Philippians 3:8, KJV). He wasn’t bragging about his discipline. He was describing a treasure.

In the middle of your field

So here’s a small midday practice. Wherever you are right now, pause for one honest minute and ask: what am I holding onto today as if it were the treasure? Maybe it’s being right in an argument, or a reputation at work, or a worry you keep turning over like a coin. Maybe it’s a habit that has quietly become the thing you run to instead of Him.

You don’t have to fix it all before lunch is over. Just name it, and then remember what’s actually in the field. Jesus is not a pearl you have to earn. He came looking for you first, and He gave everything He had, joyfully, to make you His (Hebrews 12:2 says it was “for the joy that was set before him” that He endured the cross).

The afternoon will still have its emails and errands. But the ground under your feet is different now. There’s treasure in it.

Lord Jesus, You are the treasure I keep forgetting I’ve found. Show me what I’m gripping too tightly today, and give me the kind of joy that lets it go. Thank You that You found me first. Amen.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in Matthew 13:44–46, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
