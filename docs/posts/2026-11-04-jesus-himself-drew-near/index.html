Jesus Himself Drew Near

November 4, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture Luke 24:15–17, 32

  • wednesday
  • emmaus
  • disappointment
  • presence
  • scripture
  • morning

Wednesday is the hump of the week, and for a lot of us it’s also the morning when we notice how tired we already are. The plans from Monday have bent out of shape. Something we hoped would work out hasn’t. We get up and walk into the day carrying a conversation in our heads that we’ve been having for a while now: why didn’t that go the way I thought it would?

Two of Jesus’ followers had that exact conversation on a road out of Jerusalem.

“And it came to pass, that, while they communed together and reasoned, Jesus himself drew near, and went with them. But their eyes were holden that they should not know him. And he said unto them, What manner of communications are these that ye have one to another, as ye walk, and are sad?” (Luke 24:15–17, KJV)

It was Sunday, the third day, but for Cleopas and his friend it still felt like Friday. They had heard rumors from the women about an empty tomb, but rumors are hard to hold onto when your heart is broken. So they were walking home to Emmaus, seven miles of dusty road, going over it all again. One line from their talk says everything: “But we trusted that it had been he which should have redeemed Israel” (Luke 24:21). We trusted. Past tense. Hope that had already started packing its bags.

And right there, in the middle of their sad reasoning, “Jesus himself drew near.”

I love that Luke doesn’t say an angel came, or a messenger, or a sign. Jesus himself. The risen Lord of all creation, on the morning after defeating death, spent His afternoon walking beside two discouraged people who weren’t even among the twelve. He didn’t wait for them to come back to Jerusalem and get their faith sorted out. He came to the road they were already on, going the wrong direction.

Notice, too, that they didn’t recognize Him. Their eyes were held. That’s an honest picture of how it often goes for us. Jesus is closer than we think, but sadness narrows our vision. We are so busy replaying what went wrong that we don’t see who’s walking with us. If you feel this morning like God is far away, it’s worth asking gently whether He might be nearer than your eyes are letting you see.

Then He asks a question: “What manner of communications are these?” He already knew, of course. But He let them tell Him. He let them pour out the whole disappointment, the cross, the dashed hopes, the confusing reports. Jesus isn’t impatient with our sad stories. He wants to hear them from us, in our own words, before He answers.

And when He did answer, He didn’t hand them a quick fix. “Beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself” (Luke 24:27). He took their broken story and set it inside God’s bigger one. What looked like the end of everything turned out to be the very path the Messiah had to walk. Their disappointment wasn’t erased; it was explained, and then it was redeemed.

Later, after He broke bread with them and they finally saw Him, they said this:

“Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures?” (Luke 24:32, KJV)

By the way. On the road. In the walking, not only at the table. Looking back, they could see that He’d been warming their hearts the whole time, even before they knew His name.

So here’s a small invitation for this Wednesday morning. Before the day speeds up, tell Him honestly what you’ve been talking about in your head. The thing that didn’t work out. The hope you’ve started speaking of in the past tense. Then open a few verses of Scripture and let Him walk with you through them. You might not feel anything dramatic. That’s all right. The two on the road didn’t either, at first. But Jesus himself draws near to people on sad roads, and He’s still in the habit of walking the whole seven miles.

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.” (Psalm 34:18, KJV)

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Luke 24:15–17, 32, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
