Not Where to Lay His Head

November 12, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture Luke 9:57–58

  • thursday
  • evening
  • rest
  • home
  • belonging
  • weariness

Thursday evening has its own kind of tired. The week is almost done, but not quite. You come through the door, put your bag down, and for a moment you just stand there. Home. Or at least the place where you finally get to stop.

Tonight I want to sit with a short line Jesus said on the road, because it says something about Him that is easy to miss.

A man who wanted to follow

Luke tells us that as Jesus and His disciples walked, a man came up full of eagerness:

“And it came to pass, that, as they went in the way, a certain man said unto him, Lord, I will follow thee whithersoever thou goest. And Jesus said unto him, Foxes have holes, and birds of the air have nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head.” (Luke 9:57–58, KJV)

Jesus wasn’t trying to scare the man off. He was being honest. Following Him would not come with a guaranteed bed. And in that honesty, He tells us something about His own evenings.

The fox has its den. The sparrow has its nest. But the Son of God, through whom all things were made, walked the roads of Galilee without a place to call His own. He slept in a boat in the middle of a storm. He slept in other people’s houses. Luke says that in His last week, “in the day time he was teaching in the temple; and at night he went out, and abode in the mount that is called the mount of Olives” (Luke 21:37, KJV). Under the olive trees. Outside.

He knows what tired feels like

That means something on a night like this one.

When you sink onto the couch with nothing left, Jesus is not looking at you from a distance, puzzled by your weariness. He has had days that ran long and nights with nowhere soft to land. He knows the ache in the feet after a day of walking, and the heavier ache after a day of giving yourself away to people who needed more than you had.

The letter to the Hebrews puts it this way: “For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities” (Hebrews 4:15, KJV). Touched. Moved. He feels it with you.

So you don’t have to pretend tonight. You don’t need to tidy your tiredness before you bring it to Him. He’s been there.

The homeless One makes a home

Here’s the wonder of it. The One who had nowhere to lay His head turns around and offers us a place to lay ours.

On the night before He died, Jesus said: “If a man love me, he will keep my words: and my Father will love him, and we will come unto him, and make our abode with him” (John 14:23, KJV). He didn’t own a house in Capernaum, but He promises to make His home with you. Not a visit. An abode.

Long before, the psalmist had already learned this: “Lord, thou hast been our dwelling place in all generations” (Psalm 90:1, KJV). Whatever your home looks like tonight — full and noisy, quiet and a little lonely, or not really feeling like home at all — there is a deeper home you can go to. It’s not a building. It’s Him.

For tonight

Before you sleep, try this. Lie down, and before you reach for the phone, just say: Lord, You know what tired is. Thank You for walking this day with me. I’m home in You.

Then let the words of an old evening psalm carry you the rest of the way:

“I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety.” (Psalm 4:8, KJV)

He had nowhere to lay His head, so that you would always have somewhere to lay yours. Rest well tonight. You are not out on the road alone.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Luke 9:57–58, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
