Help Thou Mine Unbelief

October 16, 2026 · Midday · 4 min read

Scripture Mark 9:23–24

  • faith
  • doubt
  • honesty
  • prayer
  • midday

By Friday midday, faith can feel a little worn at the edges. You started the week believing certain things would turn out well. Some did. Some didn’t. Now you are somewhere in between, still trusting Jesus, still praying, but if someone asked you how sure you feel, you might hesitate before answering.

There is a man in Mark’s Gospel who lived in exactly that in-between place. He gave us one of the most honest prayers in all of Scripture.

When the helpers could not help

Jesus came down from the mountain and found a crowd arguing. In the middle of it stood a father whose son had suffered terribly since he was a child. He had brought the boy to the disciples, and it had not gone the way he hoped.

“And I spake to thy disciples that they should cast him out; and they could not.” (Mark 9:18, KJV)

And they could not. Some of us know that sentence well. We went to the people who were supposed to have answers, and they didn’t. We tried the things that were supposed to work, and they didn’t. By the time this father reached Jesus, his hope had been knocked around.

You can hear it in the way he asks.

“But if thou canst do any thing, have compassion on us, and help us.” (Mark 9:22, KJV)

If thou canst. He isn’t being rude. He is tired. Disappointment has taught him to hedge.

Jesus turns the “if” around

“Jesus said unto him, If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth.” (Mark 9:23, KJV)

Jesus gently hands the “if” back. The question was never whether He is able. The question is whether this father will entrust himself to the One standing in front of him.

And then comes the answer. It isn’t polished, and it isn’t confident. It’s real.

“And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.” (Mark 9:24, KJV)

Lord, I believe. That is true. He came, didn’t he? He is still here, still asking.

Help thou mine unbelief. That is true too. Part of him is braced for one more letdown.

He doesn’t wait until the doubt is gone before he speaks. He brings both halves of his heart to Jesus at once, with tears, and asks Him to make up the difference.

Faith that is honest enough to ask for more

Notice what Jesus doesn’t do. He doesn’t send the father away to come back when his faith is stronger. He doesn’t grade the prayer. He responds to it. The boy is set free, and then comes one of the tenderest lines in the story:

“But Jesus took him by the hand, and lifted him up; and he arose.” (Mark 9:27, KJV)

The power was never in the size of the father’s faith. It was in the One his small, shaky faith reached for.

That is good news for a Friday afternoon. You don’t have to work your way up to some impressive certainty before you pray about the thing that scares you. You can say what is actually true: Jesus, I trust You. And part of me doesn’t. Please help the part that doesn’t.

That isn’t a failure of faith. It is faith, the kind that stays near Him instead of pretending.

Maybe there is a situation you have quietly stopped praying about because the “if” got too heavy. A diagnosis. A child who has wandered. A job that keeps not opening up. A sorrow you have started managing instead of bringing to Him. Bring it back to Him this afternoon, mixed feelings and all. He has never asked you to arrive already strong. He asks you to come.

And when you can’t lift yourself, remember how this story ends. Jesus takes the hand and lifts.

A midday prayer

Lord Jesus, I believe You are good and able. And there are places in me that are tired, doubtful, and afraid to hope again. I bring You both. Help my unbelief. Take me by the hand this afternoon, lift what I can’t lift, and keep me close to You through the rest of this week. Amen.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in Mark 9:23–24, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
