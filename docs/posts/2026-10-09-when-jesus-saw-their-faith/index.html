When Jesus Saw Their Faith

October 9, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture Mark 2:5

  • friendship
  • mercy
  • prayer
  • gospel

Some mornings you can walk to Jesus. You open the Bible, you pray, you feel close. Other mornings you can’t get there at all. You’re too tired, too low, too stuck to take a single step toward Him.

Mark tells a story for both kinds of mornings.

“When he entered again into Capernaum after some days, it was heard that he was at home. Immediately many were gathered together, so that there was no more room, not even around the door; and he spoke the word to them.” (Mark 2:1–2)

No room at the door

The house was packed. People filled the doorway and spilled into the street. Somewhere outside that crowd was a man lying on a mat, unable to move his legs, unable to push his way through anything.

“Four people came, carrying a paralytic to him. When they could not come near to him for the crowd, they removed the roof where he was. When they had broken it up, they let down the mat that the paralytic was lying on.” (Mark 2:3–4)

I love these four. We don’t know their names. We don’t know if they were brothers, neighbors, or old friends from the fishing boats. We only know they carried him, and when the door was blocked, they didn’t shrug and go home. They climbed up, dug through the roof, and lowered their friend right down in front of Jesus.

It must have been messy. Dust falling on the crowd, a hole where there wasn’t one before, a mat swinging down on ropes. Love often looks a little like that: not tidy, just determined.

“Seeing their faith”

Then comes the line I want you to carry into today:

“Jesus, seeing their faith, said to the paralytic, ‘Son, your sins are forgiven you.’” (Mark 2:5)

Their faith. Jesus looked up at four sweaty, dusty friends peering through a hole in the roof, and He counted their trust on behalf of the man on the mat.

If you’re the one lying still right now, that’s good news. Your faith doesn’t have to be strong enough to carry you. Sometimes God lets others carry you for a while, and He honors their love as if it were your own prayer. You aren’t failing Him because you can’t walk this stretch alone. You’re being carried, and that’s allowed.

Deeper than his legs

Notice what Jesus said first. Not “get up,” but “Son, your sins are forgiven.” Everyone in the room could see the man’s legs. Only Jesus could see the deeper paralysis, the weight he couldn’t name.

Jesus went straight to the heart before He touched anything else. And then, so no one would doubt He had authority to forgive, He said the rest:

“I tell you, arise, take up your mat, and go to your house.” He arose, and immediately took up the mat, and went out in front of them all; so that they were all amazed, and glorified God, saying, “We never saw anything like this!” (Mark 2:11–12)

The man came in through the roof on a mat. He left through the front door carrying it. That’s what Jesus does with what has been carrying us.

Be someone’s four

The story also asks a gentle question: is there someone you could carry this week?

Paul put it simply: “Bear one another’s burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ” (Galatians 6:2). It might look like a text to a friend who’s gone quiet, a meal dropped at a door, or a name you bring to Jesus in prayer because they can’t bring themselves. You don’t have to fix them. You only have to get them to Him, even if it means digging through a roof or two.

Here’s a prayer for your Friday:

Lord Jesus, thank You for seeing faith even when mine is small. On the mornings I can’t reach You, thank You for the people who carry me. Forgive what I can’t name, and lift what has been holding me down. And show me one person today I can carry to You. Give me the stubborn love of those four friends, the kind that won’t stop at a crowded door.

Whether you’re on the mat or holding one of its corners this morning, you’re in the right place. Jesus is in the room, and He’s looking up.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Mark 2:5, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
