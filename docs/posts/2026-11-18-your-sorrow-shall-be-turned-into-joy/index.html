Your Sorrow Shall Be Turned into Joy

November 18, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture John 16:20–22

  • wednesday
  • evening
  • sorrow
  • joy
  • hope
  • waiting

Some evenings arrive with a weight that the day didn’t fix. Maybe it’s grief that still catches you off guard. Maybe it’s a prayer you’ve prayed for months. Maybe it’s just a tiredness in your soul you can’t quite name. You made it through the hours, and now, in the quiet, the ache is still there.

Jesus knew His friends were about to walk into a night like that — the darkest one they would ever know. And on the way, He told them the truth:

“Verily, verily, I say unto you, That ye shall weep and lament, but the world shall rejoice: and ye shall be sorrowful, but your sorrow shall be turned into joy. A woman when she is in travail hath sorrow, because her hour is come: but as soon as she is delivered of the child, she remembereth no more the anguish, for joy that a man is born into the world. And ye now therefore have sorrow: but I will see you again, and your heart shall rejoice, and your joy no man taketh from you.” — John 16:20–22 (KJV)

He didn’t pretend

Notice what Jesus doesn’t say. He doesn’t tell them it won’t hurt. He doesn’t tell them to cheer up or to look on the bright side. He says plainly: ye shall weep and lament. Within hours they would watch Him arrested, beaten, and nailed to a cross. They would hide behind locked doors. The world around them would carry on as if nothing had happened — some would even celebrate.

There is something deeply kind about that honesty. Jesus never asks us to fake being fine. If you’re sorrowful tonight, you’re not failing at faith. You’re standing exactly where His closest friends stood.

Turned, not replaced

But listen to the shape of His promise. He doesn’t say, “your sorrow will be replaced by joy,” as if God would simply erase the hard chapter and start a new one. He says your sorrow shall be turned into joy.

That’s what happened on Easter morning. The very thing that broke their hearts — the cross — became the reason for their joy. The wounds in His hands didn’t disappear; He showed them to Thomas. What had looked like the end became the doorway.

Jesus reaches for the most human picture He can find: a mother in labor. The pain is real. It is not imaginary, and it is not small. But it is not pointless pain. It is pain that is bringing something. And when the child is born, the joy doesn’t just sit next to the anguish — it swallows it up.

I can’t tell you what your sorrow is carrying. But I can tell you that in the hands of Jesus, no tear is wasted. “They that sow in tears shall reap in joy” (Psalm 126:5).

“I will see you again”

The center of the whole promise is a Person. Not “things will get better,” but I will see you again. Their joy would not come from circumstances improving. It would come from His face.

That’s why He could add the most remarkable line of all: your joy no man taketh from you. Joy that depends on how things are going can be taken by the next phone call. Joy that rests on a risen Savior who has seen you, and will see you again, is out of everyone’s reach. Rome couldn’t take it. Prison couldn’t take it. Death itself couldn’t take it.

For tonight

You may still be in the “travail” part of your story. That’s okay. Morning didn’t come for the disciples in a single hour either — there was Friday, and then the long silence of Saturday.

So tonight, you don’t have to manufacture joy. Just bring Him the sorrow, honestly, the way it is. Tell Him what hurts. And then rest on His word, not your feelings: your sorrow shall be turned. Not maybe. Shall.

“Weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning” (Psalm 30:5).

He will see you again. And when He does, your heart will rejoice — with a joy no one can take away.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in John 16:20–22, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
