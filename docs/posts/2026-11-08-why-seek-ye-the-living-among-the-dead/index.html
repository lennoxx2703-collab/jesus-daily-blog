Why Seek Ye the Living Among the Dead?

November 8, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture Luke 24:1–8

  • sunday
  • morning
  • resurrection
  • empty-tomb
  • hope
  • following-jesus

Sunday mornings carry a particular quiet. The week has stopped, or at least slowed. Some of us dress for church. Some of us sit with coffee and a tired mind. Some of us wake already holding a grief that did not take the day off. The first day of the week arrives whether we feel ready for it or not.

Luke takes us to a Sunday even earlier than ours — very early in the morning, while the city is still dim, and a handful of women walk toward a grave.

“Now upon the first day of the week, very early in the morning, they came unto the sepulchre, bringing the spices which they had prepared, and certain others with them. And they found the stone rolled away from the sepulchre. And they entered in, and found not the body of the Lord Jesus. And it came to pass, as they were much perplexed thereabout, behold, two men stood by them in shining garments: And as they were afraid, and bowed down their faces to the earth, they said unto them, Why seek ye the living among the dead? He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee, Saying, The Son of man must be delivered into the hands of sinful men, and be crucified, and the third day rise again. And they remembered his words.” (Luke 24:1–8, KJV)

Bringing the spices

They come with what love can still do when hope has run out. Spices, prepared the night before. A last kindness for a body they expect to find. They have not come to celebrate. They have come to honor someone they think is gone.

There is something tender about that. Jesus does not despise the people who show up with heavy hands. These women are faithful in the dark. If your Sunday faith feels more like duty than joy — if you are keeping a habit of devotion because you don’t know what else to do with your love — you are not far from them. Come anyway. Bring what you have.

Why seek ye the living among the dead?

The stone is already rolled away. The body is not there. They are perplexed, which is a kind word for the swirl that hits when the story you braced for is not the story you find.

Then the question, gentle and world-changing: “Why seek ye the living among the dead?”

They were looking in the right place for Friday’s Jesus — the crucified friend, the finished chapter. But it is Sunday now. He is not among the dead things. He is not a memory to perfume. He is not trapped in the worst day you have ever lived through.

That question still finds us. We look for Jesus among old failures we keep rehearsing, a prayer we decided He didn’t answer, a faith that froze years ago, a hope we buried too soon. We visit those places with spices — with regret, with habit, with the quiet assumption that the living Lord could not possibly meet us here, today.

He can. He does. But not as a body in a tomb. As the risen One.

He is not here, but is risen

The messengers do not leave the women in the question. They give them news: “He is not here, but is risen.” Then they do something kind. They send them back to words Jesus had already spoken. Remember how he spake unto you. The resurrection was not a scramble to fix a disaster. It was the third day He had promised.

And they remembered His words.

Sunday morning is a good time to remember — not only the big claims of the faith, but the sentences of Jesus that have been waiting while you walked toward something that felt like an ending. He told the truth about the cross. He told the truth about the third day. He is still telling the truth about Himself.

So if you woke today treating Jesus as a beautiful past — a story to respect, a figure to admire, a loss to tend — hear the news again. He is not in the place of death. He is risen. The first day of the week belongs to a living Lord.

You may go to church later, or you may sit at your own table. Either way, do not look for Him only among what has ended. Open the Scriptures He already spoke. Remember. Then walk into the rest of Sunday knowing the tomb is empty on purpose, and that the One you came to honor is alive.

“For his anger endureth but a moment; in his favour is life: weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning.” (Psalm 30:5, KJV)

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Luke 24:1–8, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
