Moved with Compassion

October 27, 2026 · Morning · 3 min read

Scripture Matthew 9:35–38

  • tuesday
  • compassion
  • weariness
  • prayer
  • morning

Tuesday doesn’t get much attention. Monday has its drama and Friday has its relief, but Tuesday is just the day the week settles onto your shoulders. The coffee is the same, the to-do list is longer, and somewhere in the crowd on the train or in the queue of messages, you’re one more tired face.

Matthew has a short scene for a morning like this.

“And Jesus went about all the cities and villages, teaching in their synagogues, and preaching the gospel of the kingdom, and healing every sickness and every disease among the people. But when he saw the multitudes, he was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd.” (Matthew 9:35–36, KJV)

Notice how busy Jesus is. Cities and villages, teaching, preaching, healing every sickness. If anyone had a reason to see people as a workload, it was Him. Crowds pressed in everywhere He went. And yet when He looked up and saw the multitudes, the first thing that happened inside Him wasn’t irritation or fatigue. He was moved with compassion.

The word Matthew uses points to something deep, a feeling in the gut. This wasn’t polite concern. Jesus looked at the crowd and it hurt Him to see them like that.

And look at what He saw: they “fainted, and were scattered abroad.” Worn out and pulled in every direction. Not wicked, not lazy, not a disappointment. Just tired and without anyone to gather them. That’s how Jesus reads a crowd. That’s how He reads you this morning.

I think many of us assume God looks at the multitudes, and at us in them, with a sigh. Again? Still struggling with that? But the Gospel keeps showing us the opposite. When Jesus sees weariness, His heart moves toward it. He doesn’t wait for the crowd to get organized before He cares about them.

The psalmist knew this long before:

“Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him. For he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust.” (Psalm 103:13–14, KJV)

He remembers what we’re made of. He isn’t surprised that you’re tired by Tuesday.

Then Jesus turns to His disciples and says something that might seem like a change of subject:

“Then saith he unto his disciples, The harvest truly is plenteous, but the labourers are few; Pray ye therefore the Lord of the harvest, that he will send forth labourers into his harvest.” (Matthew 9:37–38, KJV)

It isn’t a change of subject at all. His compassion doesn’t stop at a feeling. He looks at the scattered, fainting crowd and calls it a harvest: people ready to be gathered, not a problem to be managed. And the first thing He asks His friends to do is not to work harder. It’s to pray.

That’s a gentle invitation for today. Before you try to fix everyone around you, or yourself, start where Jesus started. Let Him look at you with compassion first. Then, as you move through the day, try to see the faces around you the way He saw the crowd: not as interruptions, but as people who are tired and maybe a little scattered, just like you.

Maybe it’s the colleague who snaps in the morning meeting. The cashier who doesn’t smile. The family member who sends a short reply. Tired, scattered, without someone to gather them. You don’t have to carry them all. You can simply pray, Lord of the harvest, send someone to them. And if it’s me, give me Your heart for them.

Here’s a short prayer for the morning:

Jesus, You see me in the crowd today, and You are moved with compassion. Thank You that You don’t sigh at my weariness. Gather what’s scattered in me. Let me see the people around me the way You see them, and use me, if You want, to be a small part of Your harvest. Amen.

Go gently this Tuesday. The One who looks out over the crowd has already seen you, and His heart moved toward you first.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Matthew 9:35–38, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
