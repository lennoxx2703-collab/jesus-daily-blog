He Shall Gird Himself, and Serve Them

November 26, 2026 · Evening · 4 min read

Scripture Luke 12:35–37

  • thursday
  • evening
  • waiting
  • hope
  • faithfulness
  • second-coming

There’s a certain kind of faithfulness that only happens after dark. Nobody claps for it. It’s the parent still up with a feverish child. The nurse halfway through a night shift. The person who keeps praying for someone who stopped answering their calls years ago. The one who simply refuses to let the light go out, even when it’s late and nothing seems to be happening.

Jesus told a little story about people like that.

Lights burning

“Let your loins be girded about, and your lights burning; and ye yourselves like unto men that wait for their lord, when he will return from the wedding; that when he cometh and knocketh, they may open unto him immediately.” — Luke 12:35–36 (KJV)

Picture a big house at night. The master has gone to a wedding feast, and in those days a wedding could run for hours — even days. No one knows exactly when he’ll be back. So the servants have a choice. They can blow out the lamps, loosen their robes, and assume he isn’t coming tonight. Or they can keep their clothes tucked up for work and their lamps trimmed, listening for footsteps on the road.

It’s not glamorous. It’s mostly waiting. Refilling the oil. Fighting off sleep. Listening.

And if we’re honest, that’s what a lot of the Christian life feels like in the evening hours. We’re waiting for prayers to be answered, for wounds to heal, for the world to be put right, for Jesus Himself to come back like He promised. Some nights the waiting feels long, and the road outside stays empty.

The twist nobody expected

Then Jesus says something that would have made His listeners sit up:

“Blessed are those servants, whom the lord when he cometh shall find watching: verily I say unto you, that he shall gird himself, and make them to sit down to meat, and will come forth and serve them.” — Luke 12:37 (KJV)

That’s not how masters behaved. Ever. A master coming home late would expect his supper to be ready and his feet washed. But this master walks through the door, sees his tired servants still waiting with their lamps lit — and he ties up his own robe. He pulls out the chairs. He sits them down. And he brings the food himself.

Jesus is telling us what He is like.

The one we’re waiting for isn’t coming home to inspect us with a clipboard. He’s coming home to serve us. The same Lord who knelt with a towel on the night He was betrayed is the one who, at the end of all things, will pull up a chair for His weary people and say, Sit down. You’ve waited long enough. Let Me bring you something.

Scripture gives that moment a name:

“Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb.” — Revelation 19:9 (KJV)

Second watch, third watch

Jesus adds one more line that I find quietly comforting:

“And if he shall come in the second watch, or come in the third watch, and find them so, blessed are those servants.” — Luke 12:38 (KJV)

The second watch was late. The third watch was the dead middle of the night — the hours when everything feels heaviest. Jesus isn’t pretending the wait will be short. He’s saying the blessing doesn’t shrink because the night got long. The servant who kept the lamp lit until three in the morning isn’t forgotten. If anything, he’s the one the Master most wants to sit down at the table.

So if you’re in the second or third watch of something tonight — a long illness, a long grief, a long season of praying with no visible answer — your waiting is not wasted. It’s seen. It’s leading somewhere. There’s a table at the end of it.

For your evening

You don’t have to manufacture some heroic burst of faith before bed. Keeping the light on can look very small: one honest prayer. One verse read slowly. Choosing not to give up on someone. Going to sleep trusting that He hasn’t forgotten the way home.

“My soul waiteth for the Lord more than they that watch for the morning: I say, more than they that watch for the morning.” — Psalm 130:6 (KJV)

Maybe pray something like this:

Lord Jesus, it’s late, and some of what I’m waiting for still hasn’t come. Keep my little light burning. Give me oil for tonight, and strength to listen for You. Thank You that You’re not coming to scold tired servants, but to serve them. When You come — in this watch or the next — find me ready, and sit me at Your table. Amen.

Keep the lamp lit, friend. The Master is on His way home, and He’s already planning to serve you supper.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Luke 12:35–37, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
