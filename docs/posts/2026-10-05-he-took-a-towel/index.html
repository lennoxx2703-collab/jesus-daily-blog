He Took a Towel

October 5, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture John 13:4–5

  • gospel
  • mercy
  • discipleship
  • comfort

Mondays leave dust. Not the dramatic kind, just the ordinary grit of a first workday: the email you answered too sharply, the conversation that went sideways, the small resentments you picked up somewhere between lunch and the commute home. By evening it’s on you, and most of us carry it straight to bed without thinking about it.

John tells us about an evening like that, around a table, and what Jesus did with the dust.

“Now before the feast of the Passover, Jesus, knowing that his time had come that he would depart from this world to the Father, having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end.” (John 13:1)

“…arose from supper, and laid aside his outer garments. He took a towel, and wrapped a towel around his waist. Then he poured water into the basin, and began to wash the disciples’ feet, and to wipe them with the towel that was wrapped around him.” (John 13:4–5)

The Lord on His knees

Picture the room. Twelve men who had walked dusty roads in sandals, reclining at a low table, their feet right there behind them. Washing feet was a servant’s job, and there was no servant. Nobody volunteered. Earlier that same evening, according to Luke, they had been arguing about which of them was the greatest (Luke 22:24).

So Jesus got up. John is careful to tell us what He knew when He did it: that the Father had put all things into His hands, that He had come from God and was going back to God (John 13:3). He wasn’t kneeling because He’d forgotten who He was. He knelt because He knew exactly who He was. This is what all authority looks like when it’s held by perfect love: a towel, a basin, and the dirtiest part of you held gently in His hands.

He washed Peter’s feet. He washed John’s. He washed the feet of Judas, whose plan was already set. Love to the end means love that doesn’t skip anyone at the table.

“You will never wash my feet”

Peter couldn’t stand it. “You will never wash my feet!” (John 13:8). I understand him. It’s one thing to serve Jesus. It’s harder to sit still while He serves you. We’d rather clean ourselves up first and present Him with something presentable.

But Jesus answered, “If I don’t wash you, you have no part with me.”

That’s the gospel in one sentence. We don’t come to Jesus by getting clean. We come to Him by letting Him make us clean. The cross, only hours away from that supper, is the same towel stretched as far as it can go. Peter’s pride had to give way before his feet could be washed, and so does ours.

Then Jesus said something tender: “Someone who has bathed only needs to have his feet washed, but is completely clean” (John 13:10). If you belong to Him, you aren’t starting from zero tonight. You’re not unforgiven because Monday was messy. You only need the day’s dust rinsed off, and He’s already kneeling with the basin.

Before you lie down

  1. Name the dust. What did you pick up today? A harsh word, a worry, a quiet bitterness, a failure you keep replaying. Say it plainly to Him.
  2. Let Him wash it. Don’t argue like Peter did. Picture Jesus with the towel and simply say, Lord, wash me. Then believe Him when He says you’re clean.
  3. Think of one pair of feet. Jesus said, “you also ought to wash one another’s feet” (John 13:14). Who could you quietly serve tomorrow? A colleague, a spouse, a child, someone who rubbed you the wrong way today.

This morning we pushed out at His word. At noon He sat with us by the well. Now, at the end of the day, He kneels. That’s the shape of His love: He goes ahead of you, He meets you in the middle, and He finishes the day by making you clean.

Lie down tonight with clean feet. He loved His own to the end, and you are His own.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in John 13:4–5, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
