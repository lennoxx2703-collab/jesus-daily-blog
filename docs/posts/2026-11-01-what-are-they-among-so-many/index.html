What Are They Among So Many?

November 1, 2026 · Midday · 4 min read

Scripture John 6:5–13

  • sunday
  • provision
  • enough
  • small-offerings
  • feeding-the-five-thousand
  • midday

Sunday lunch has a way of showing what we have and what we don’t. Some tables are full and loud today. Some have one plate. Some people are looking at the week ahead and quietly doing the math: the money, the energy, the patience, the hours. And the math doesn’t add up.

John tells about a hillside where the math didn’t add up either.

When Jesus then lifted up his eyes, and saw a great company come unto him, he saith unto Philip, Whence shall we buy bread, that these may eat? And this he said to prove him: for he himself knew what he would do. Philip answered him, Two hundred pennyworth of bread is not sufficient for them, that every one of them may take a little. One of his disciples, Andrew, Simon Peter’s brother, saith unto him, There is a lad here, which hath five barley loaves, and two small fishes: but what are they among so many? (John 6:5–9, KJV)

He already knew

Look at that little sentence tucked in the middle: “for he himself knew what he would do.” Jesus isn’t panicking with the disciples. He isn’t hoping the numbers work out. He asks Philip the question not because He needs an answer, but because He wants Philip to see something.

That’s worth holding onto at lunchtime on a day when you feel short. The question “how will this ever be enough?” may be loud in your head. It isn’t loud in His. He already knows what He will do.

Honest about the gap

Philip does the sums and comes back with the truth: even a fortune’s worth of bread would only give everyone a crumb. Andrew finds a boy with a lunch and says the quiet part out loud: “but what are they among so many?”

I love that Jesus doesn’t scold either of them. Their honesty isn’t unbelief. It’s just the starting point. You’re allowed to tell Him plainly that what you have is too small. Too small for the grief, too small for the bills, too small for the kids, the job, the friend who needs more than you can give. He can work with honest.

Small things, put in His hands

And Jesus said, Make the men sit down. Now there was much grass in the place. So the men sat down, in number about five thousand. And Jesus took the loaves; and when he had given thanks, he distributed to the disciples, and the disciples to them that were set down; and likewise of the fishes as much as they would. (John 6:10–11, KJV)

Barley loaves were the cheap bread, the food of ordinary people. A boy’s lunch. Nothing impressive. But it was handed over, and Jesus took it, and He gave thanks for it, as it was, before anything had changed.

There’s a gentle invitation there. Before you have more, thank Him for what’s in your hands. Your five loaves might be ten minutes, a tired heart, a small kindness, a short prayer. Small doesn’t disqualify. It’s exactly what He picked up.

And notice the people were told to sit down on the grass. Not to strive, not to earn their portion. Just to sit and receive. Some Sundays the most faithful thing you can do is rest and let Him feed you.

Nothing lost

When they were filled, he said unto his disciples, Gather up the fragments that remain, that nothing be lost. Therefore they gathered them together, and filled twelve baskets with the fragments of the five barley loaves, which remained over and above unto them that had eaten. (John 6:12–13, KJV)

Everyone ate “as much as they would.” Not a crumb each, as Philip feared, but full. And then there were leftovers, more than they started with. Jesus even cared about the fragments: “that nothing be lost.”

That’s how He is with us. He doesn’t waste the broken pieces. The leftover bits of a hard year, the scraps of faith you’re not sure count, He gathers them up.

For this afternoon

Whatever feels too small today, say it to Him honestly. Then put it in His hands, thank Him for it, and sit down on the grass for a while. He already knows what He will do.

Lord Jesus, here is what I have. It doesn’t look like much against what I’m facing. I give it to You anyway, and I thank You for it. Feed me, and let nothing be lost. Amen.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in John 6:5–13, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
