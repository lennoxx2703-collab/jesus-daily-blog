Thou Art Loosed

October 19, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture Luke 13:10–13

  • evening
  • burdens
  • healing
  • weariness
  • monday

By Monday evening, you can usually feel the week in your shoulders. The first day back always seems to carry more than its share. You sit down, and something in you stays hunched, as if the day hasn’t quite let go of you yet.

Luke tells us about a woman who knew that posture far better than we do.

“And he was teaching in one of the synagogues on the sabbath. And, behold, there was a woman which had a spirit of infirmity eighteen years, and was bowed together, and could in no wise lift up herself.” (Luke 13:10–11, KJV)

Eighteen years. Think about what that means. For eighteen years, she saw mostly the ground: dust, feet, the hem of other people’s robes. She couldn’t easily look anyone in the face. She had probably stopped expecting anyone to look at hers.

And still she came to the synagogue that day. Bent over, slow, maybe at the back. She didn’t ask Jesus for anything. Luke records no request, no reaching for His garment, no cry for mercy. She just came, the way some of us come to God at the end of a long day: tired, quiet, with nothing much to say.

Then this:

“And when Jesus saw her, he called her to him, and said unto her, Woman, thou art loosed from thine infirmity. And he laid his hands on her: and immediately she was made straight, and glorified God.” (Luke 13:12–13, KJV)

Look at the order of it. He saw her. In a room full of people, while He was teaching, His eyes found the one person who couldn’t lift her eyes to find Him. He called her. He didn’t wait for her to work up the courage or the strength. He spoke and told her she was free. He laid His hands on her. Not just a word from across the room, but a touch, on a back that had been bent for nearly two decades.

And she stood up straight, and the first thing she did with her lifted head was praise God.

When the synagogue ruler complained that this healing happened on the wrong day, Jesus answered with something tender and fierce at once:

“And ought not this woman, being a daughter of Abraham, whom Satan hath bound, lo, these eighteen years, be loosed from this bond on the sabbath day?” (Luke 13:16, KJV)

Daughter of Abraham. That’s not a word for someone on the edge of the room. That’s family language. He’s saying she belongs here, and she was always meant to stand up in it.

Maybe tonight you’re carrying something that has bent you for a long time. Not one bad Monday, but years of it: a grief that never fully lifted, a fear you’ve learned to walk around, a shame that keeps your eyes on the floor. You might not even have the words to ask for help anymore.

This story says you don’t have to start with the right words. Jesus sees the one who can’t look up. He calls before we’re ready. And He doesn’t heal from a safe distance. He comes close enough to touch.

The psalmist said it long before that sabbath in the synagogue:

“But thou, O LORD, art a shield for me; my glory, and the lifter up of mine head.” (Psalm 3:3, KJV)

So before you sleep, try this. Sit for a moment and notice where you’re bent. Name it to Him, even in one word. Then let your shoulders drop, and picture His hand there. You may not stand perfectly straight by morning. Some healing comes all at once, like hers. Some comes slowly. But you are seen, you are called, and you belong to Him.

Rest tonight, daughter or son. The One who lifts heads is not finished with yours.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Luke 13:10–13, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
