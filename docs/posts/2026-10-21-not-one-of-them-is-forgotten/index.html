Not One of Them Is Forgotten

October 21, 2026 · Midday · 3 min read

Scripture Luke 12:6–7

  • wednesday
  • fear
  • worth
  • being-seen
  • gods-care

Somewhere around the middle of a Wednesday, it’s easy to feel like a small thing. The inbox doesn’t know your name. The traffic doesn’t care. The people around you are busy with their own weeks, and if you disappeared from the afternoon, you wonder how long it would take anyone to notice.

Jesus once talked about exactly that feeling. He used the smallest, cheapest thing in the market to do it.

“Are not five sparrows sold for two farthings, and not one of them is forgotten before God? But even the very hairs of your head are all numbered. Fear not therefore: ye are of more value than many sparrows.” (Luke 12:6–7, KJV)

The bird nobody counted

In Jesus’ day, sparrows were what the poor could afford. In Matthew’s telling, two of them went for a farthing, one of the smallest coins there was. Luke adds a little market detail: buy four, and the seller tossed in a fifth for free. Two farthings, five birds.

Think about that fifth sparrow. It wasn’t even part of the price. It was the extra, the throw-in, the one nobody was really paying for.

And Jesus says, not one of them is forgotten before God. Not the four that were counted. Not the fifth that wasn’t.

If you’ve ever felt like the extra one in a room, the person invited because it would’ve been awkward not to, the one whose work gets folded into someone else’s credit, the one who sort of comes along with the rest, Jesus is talking to you here. God doesn’t do bulk pricing with His creatures. He doesn’t remember the set and lose the spare.

Numbered, not just known

Then Jesus goes further, almost playfully: even the very hairs of your head are all numbered.

Nobody counts hairs. You don’t. The people who love you most don’t. It’s the most pointless thing you could imagine keeping track of, which is exactly why He says it. If God pays attention to that, He is not overlooking the things that actually weigh on you: the conversation you’re dreading this afternoon, the test results you’re waiting for, the loneliness that shows up around lunch when everyone else seems to have someone to eat with.

You are not a face in a crowd to Him. You are known down to details you yourself have never noticed.

“Fear not therefore”

Notice the word therefore. Jesus doesn’t just tell us not to be afraid, as if we could switch fear off. He gives a reason first, then the comfort follows from it. You are remembered. You are counted. Therefore, fear doesn’t get the final word.

This was spoken to disciples who would face real danger. Just before these verses, Jesus warns them about people who can kill the body. He didn’t promise that nothing hard would ever happen to them. Matthew records the other half of the saying: “one of them shall not fall on the ground without your Father” (Matthew 10:29, KJV). Sparrows still fall. But they never fall alone, and they never fall unseen.

That’s the kind of peace Jesus offers in the middle of an ordinary, unremarkable day. Not that life will stop being heavy, but that you will never carry it outside the attention of your Father.

A midday pause

If you have a minute right now, try this. Look out a window, or just picture one, and imagine a small brown bird on a branch. Nothing special. The kind you’d walk past.

Then say, quietly, “He hasn’t forgotten that one. And He hasn’t forgotten me.”

Maybe pray something like this:

Lord Jesus, some days I feel like the fifth sparrow, the one nobody paid for. Thank You that You see me anyway. You know the things I haven’t told anyone, and You know the number of hairs on my head. Take the fear I’m carrying into this afternoon, and let me rest in being remembered by You. Amen.

Then go back to your afternoon. Same desk, same tasks, same people. But you go back as someone who is known, counted, and worth more to God than many sparrows.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in Luke 12:6–7, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
