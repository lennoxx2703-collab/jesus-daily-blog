Continued All Night in Prayer

November 2, 2026 · Evening · 4 min read

Scripture Luke 6:12–13

  • monday
  • evening
  • prayer
  • decisions
  • tomorrow
  • trust

The first day of the week is done, and if you’re like most of us, it didn’t finish so much as stop. The inbox is still there. The conversation you need to have is still ahead. Somewhere in the back of your mind a decision is waiting for you in the morning, and it’s already tapping on the door of your sleep.

Luke tells us about a night like that in Jesus’ life:

“And it came to pass in those days, that he went out into a mountain to pray, and continued all night in prayer to God. And when it was day, he called unto him his disciples: and of them he chose twelve, whom also he named apostles.” — Luke 6:12–13 (KJV)

The next morning Jesus would make one of the biggest choices of His ministry: the twelve men He would walk with, teach, trust, and one day send into the world. And the night before, He didn’t lie awake running scenarios. He went up the mountain and talked with His Father.

He didn’t carry tomorrow alone

It’s worth sitting with that for a moment. The Son of God, who knew what was in every heart, still chose to spend the night before a decision in prayer. Not because He was anxious, but because that’s what love does with the things that matter: it brings them to the Father first.

So much of our evening worry is really tomorrow, arriving early. We rehearse what we’ll say, what they’ll say, what could go wrong. We try to settle in our heads at eleven at night what was never ours to settle alone.

Jesus shows another way. Before the choosing, the praying. Before the morning, the mountain.

Prayer didn’t make it easy

Here’s the part that keeps this honest. Among the twelve names Luke lists a few verses later is “Judas Iscariot, which also was the traitor.” Jesus prayed all night, and the list He came down with still included heartbreak.

That’s not a sign the prayer failed. It’s a sign that praying isn’t a way of guaranteeing a painless tomorrow. It’s a way of walking into tomorrow held by the Father’s will instead of driven by our fear. Jesus knew the road ahead had sorrow in it, and He walked it anyway, because He’d already given it to God in the dark.

If you’ve prayed about something and it still turned out hard, you haven’t been forgotten. You’re walking a road your Savior walked first.

Bring the decision up the mountain

You don’t need a literal mountain tonight. A quiet corner of the kitchen will do, or the edge of your bed with the lights low. And you probably won’t pray all night, and that’s alright. The point isn’t the hours. The point is where tomorrow goes before you close your eyes.

Paul put it simply:

“Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.” — Philippians 4:6 (KJV)

In every thing. The meeting, the phone call, the job question, the person you’re not sure about, the choice you keep circling. Name it to Him, one thing at a time. Say what you’re afraid of. Say what you hope for. Then thank Him for something from today, even something small, because gratitude is how a worried heart remembers who it’s talking to.

When it is day

“And when it was day, he called unto him his disciples.” Notice that Jesus didn’t stay on the mountain. Prayer wasn’t a hiding place from the decision; it was the preparation for it. When morning came, He went down and acted.

That’s the promise for you too. You don’t have to solve tomorrow tonight. You only have to give it to the One who is already there. Morning will come, and you won’t walk into it alone.

A simple prayer for tonight:

Father, I’m handing You tomorrow before I sleep. You know the decision in front of me and the fear underneath it. I don’t need to see the whole road; I just need to know You’re on it. Thank You for walking me through today. Guide me in the morning, and let me rest now. In Jesus’ name, amen.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Luke 6:12–13, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
