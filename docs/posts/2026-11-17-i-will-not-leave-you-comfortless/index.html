I Will Not Leave You Comfortless

November 17, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture John 14:16–18

  • tuesday
  • evening
  • loneliness
  • holy-spirit
  • comfort
  • presence

Evenings can be the loneliest part of the day. The noise of work fades, messages slow down, and the house — or the apartment, or the room — gets quiet. For some of us that quiet is rest. For others it’s the hour when we notice who isn’t there.

If that’s where you are tonight, sit for a moment with something Jesus said on the loneliest night of His friends’ lives.

It was the evening of the Last Supper. Jesus had washed their feet. He had told them one of them would betray Him, and that He was going away. Their hearts were heavy and confused. And into that room He spoke these words:

“And I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever; even the Spirit of truth; whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, neither knoweth him: but ye know him; for he dwelleth with you, and shall be in you. I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you.” (John 14:16–18, KJV)

Not orphans

The word translated “comfortless” is the Greek word for orphans. Jesus was saying, in effect: I am not going to leave you like children with no one to come home to.

That’s a tender thing to say to grown men. Fishermen, a former tax collector, a zealot. But Jesus knew what was underneath their questions that night. They weren’t only worried about plans and kingdoms. They were afraid of being left.

Maybe you know that fear. Maybe someone did leave. Maybe you’re surrounded by people and still feel like nobody really knows you. Jesus speaks to that place directly: I will not leave you orphans. I will come to you.

Another Comforter

Notice the word “another.” Jesus had been their comforter for three years — walking the roads with them, eating with them, calming storms, answering their questions late at night. Now He promised another one, of the same kind: the Holy Spirit.

And look at the little words He uses about the Spirit: with you, in you, for ever.

Not visiting. Not occasional. Dwelling. Jesus had been with them; the Spirit would be in them. The closeness doesn’t shrink after Jesus goes back to the Father. It goes deeper.

A few verses later He adds what this Comforter does: “he shall teach you all things, and bring all things to your remembrance, whatsoever I have said unto you” (John 14:26). On a quiet evening, when a verse you’d forgotten suddenly comes back to mind, or a sense of peace settles that you didn’t produce yourself, that is not nothing. That is the Comforter keeping Jesus’ promise.

For tonight

You don’t have to feel it for it to be true. The disciples didn’t feel very comforted that night; they were about to scatter in fear. But the promise held anyway, and within weeks those same frightened men were full of the Spirit and full of courage.

So tonight, if the quiet feels heavy, try this:

  • Name the loneliness honestly. Tell Him, “Lord, I feel alone tonight.” He isn’t disappointed by that.
  • Remember His words. “I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you.” Read it slowly, twice.
  • Speak to the One who is with you. Not into the empty room, but to the Father, through Jesus, by the Spirit who dwells in you. You’re not talking to the ceiling. You’re talking to Family.

Elsewhere, Scripture holds the same promise: “I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee” (Hebrews 13:5). The quiet room isn’t empty. You are not an orphan. You are kept.

Lord Jesus, the day is ending and sometimes I feel so alone. Thank You for promising that You will not leave me comfortless. Holy Spirit, Comforter, be near to me tonight. Remind me of His words. Let me rest as a child who has a home. Amen.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in John 14:16–18, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
