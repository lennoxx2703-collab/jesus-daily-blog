The House Was Filled

November 4, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture John 12:1–3, 7

  • wednesday
  • evening
  • love
  • worship
  • bethany
  • devotion

By Wednesday evening, most of us have spent the day being useful. We answered, fixed, carried, and served. That’s good work, and God sees it. But somewhere around dinner it’s worth asking a quieter question: did I give Jesus anything today that wasn’t on a list?

John tells us about one evening in Bethany, a few days before the cross.

“Then Jesus six days before the passover came to Bethany, where Lazarus was which had been dead, whom he raised from the dead. There they made him a supper; and Martha served: but Lazarus was one of them that sat at the table with him. Then took Mary a pound of ointment of spikenard, very costly, and anointed the feet of Jesus, and wiped his feet with her hair: and the house was filled with the odour of the ointment.” (John 12:1–3, KJV)

An ordinary supper

It starts so plainly. “There they made him a supper.” Nobody is preaching. Nothing is being healed. It’s a meal at a friend’s house, with plates and bread and probably too little room at the table. Martha is serving, as Martha does. Lazarus, who not long ago was in a tomb, is just sitting there eating. Resurrection has turned into an ordinary weeknight dinner.

I love that. The places where Jesus is most at home are often not the dramatic ones. He’s at the table. He’s in the kitchen-smell of an evening meal. He’s where we are when the day winds down.

Mary’s pound of spikenard

Then Mary does something that doesn’t fit the evening at all. She takes a pound of very costly ointment, the kind of thing a family might keep for years, and pours it on Jesus’ feet. Then she wipes them with her hair.

It’s not efficient. It’s not explained. Judas, sitting at the same table, does the math out loud and calls it waste. But Mary isn’t doing math. She has sat at Jesus’ feet before and listened. She has wept at His feet when her brother died. Now she’s at His feet again, and this time she simply gives Him the most precious thing she has.

Love for Jesus isn’t always tidy or useful. Sometimes it looks like ten extra minutes with Him when you’re tired. Sometimes it’s a song sung alone in the car, a tear you don’t hide, an honest prayer that takes longer than you planned. Somebody might call it wasted time. Jesus never does.

“Let her alone”

“Then said Jesus, Let her alone: against the day of my burying hath she kept this.” (John 12:7, KJV)

Jesus defends her. He doesn’t let anyone shame her devotion. And He tells us something Mary may only half have understood: her gift was preparing Him for the cross. She gave Him love on the way to the place where He would give Himself for her.

That’s the order of things, always. Whatever we pour out for Jesus is small next to what He poured out for us. Our devotion doesn’t earn His love. It answers it.

The whole house smelled of it

Here’s the line I keep coming back to: “the house was filled with the odour of the ointment.” Mary’s act was aimed at one person, but everyone in the house could smell it. Martha at the serving table. Lazarus at his plate. Even Judas.

That’s what quiet love for Jesus tends to do. You don’t have to announce it. When someone keeps going back to His feet, it starts to change the air in their home. Patience gets a little easier. Kindness lingers. Other people notice something they can’t quite name.

So tonight, as the dishes are cleared and the house gets quiet, give Him something that isn’t on a list. It doesn’t have to be costly in money. It just has to be yours. Sit at His feet for a while. Thank Him for the supper, for the day, for the cross. Let your evening be a small, sweet-smelling offering.

“Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense; and the lifting up of my hands as the evening sacrifice.” (Psalm 141:2, KJV)

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in John 12:1–3, 7, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
