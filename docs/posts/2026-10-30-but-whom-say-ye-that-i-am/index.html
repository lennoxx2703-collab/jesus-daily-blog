But Whom Say Ye That I Am?

October 30, 2026 · Midday · 3 min read

Scripture Matthew 16:13–17

  • friday
  • identity-of-jesus
  • faith
  • peter
  • midday

Midday has a way of filling up with other people’s opinions. The news, the group chat, the comment under a post, the colleague who has a take on everything. By lunchtime on a Friday you’ve probably heard a dozen voices telling you what to think about the world, about yourself, maybe even about God.

Jesus once took His disciples a long way north, to Caesarea Philippi, a town full of temples to other gods, far from the crowds of Galilee. And there, in the middle of all that noise, He asked them a question.

“When Jesus came into the coasts of Caesarea Philippi, he asked his disciples, saying, Whom do men say that I the Son of man am? And they said, Some say that thou art John the Baptist: some, Elias; and others, Jeremias, or one of the prophets.” (Matthew 16:13–14, KJV)

That first question was easy. The disciples had been listening. They knew what people were saying, and the answers were actually respectful ones: a prophet, a great man, someone sent from God. People still say things like that about Jesus today. A good teacher. A moral example. An inspiring figure from history.

But Jesus didn’t stop there.

“He saith unto them, But whom say ye that I am?” (Matthew 16:15, KJV)

Notice the little word but. Jesus moves the question from the crowd to the circle, from “them” to “you.” It’s one thing to report what others believe. It’s another thing entirely to say what you believe, out loud, with your own mouth, when He’s standing right there looking at you.

A question that can’t be outsourced

You can borrow a lot in the life of faith. You can borrow a church’s songs, a friend’s encouragement, a pastor’s words, a parent’s prayers. All of that is good. But there’s one thing nobody can answer for you, and it’s this question. Jesus asks it personally, and He waits for a personal reply.

Peter, of course, spoke up first. He usually did.

“And Simon Peter answered and said, Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God.” (Matthew 16:16, KJV)

It’s a staggering answer. Not just a prophet, not just a teacher, but the promised one, the Son of the living God, standing in a town full of dead idols. And what strikes me most is how Jesus responds:

“And Jesus answered and said unto him, Blessed art thou, Simon Barjona: for flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven.” (Matthew 16:17, KJV)

Peter didn’t figure it out by being clever. He didn’t win an argument. The Father showed him. That’s a comfort, because it means knowing Jesus isn’t a test you pass by having every answer lined up. It’s a gift God loves to give to anyone who honestly keeps company with His Son.

And Peter wasn’t finished being Peter. A few verses later he’d get it badly wrong. Later still he’d deny Jesus three times by a fire. Yet the confession was real, and Jesus built on it. Your answer doesn’t have to come from a perfect life to be a true one.

Answering at lunchtime

So here’s a small invitation for the middle of your Friday. Step away from the noise for a minute, the way Jesus took His friends away from the crowds. Let the other voices go quiet. And let Him ask you directly: Who do you say that I am?

Maybe your answer comes easily today. Maybe it comes out as a whisper, or as Thomas’s later cry, “My Lord and my God” (John 20:28). Maybe all you can say right now is, “I want to know.” That’s an honest place to start, and He welcomes it.

Then go back into your afternoon carrying your own answer, not the crowd’s. Whatever people are saying out there, you get to know Him for yourself, and He already knows you by name.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in Matthew 16:13–17, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
