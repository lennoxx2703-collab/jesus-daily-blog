Jesus Saith unto Her, Mary

November 21, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture John 20:11–16

  • saturday
  • morning
  • grief
  • resurrection
  • known-by-name
  • presence

Saturday mornings have a way of letting things catch up with you.

All week there’s somewhere to be. The alarm, the inbox, the list. Then the weekend comes, the house is quiet, the coffee is hot, and the thing you’ve been outrunning since Monday sits down across from you. The loss. The disappointment. The person who isn’t there anymore. If that’s you this morning, there’s a woman in a garden I’d like you to meet.

“But Mary stood without at the sepulchre weeping: and as she wept, she stooped down, and looked into the sepulchre… And they say unto her, Woman, why weepest thou? She saith unto them, Because they have taken away my Lord, and I know not where they have laid him.” — John 20:11, 13 (KJV)

She stayed

The others had already gone home. Peter and John ran to the tomb, saw the linen clothes lying there, and left. Mary stayed. She didn’t have a plan. She didn’t have a theology for what was happening. She just couldn’t make herself walk away from the last place she had been close to Him.

There’s something holy about that. Grief is often just love with nowhere to go, and Mary’s love had nowhere to go. So she stood there, and she cried, and she kept looking into the empty place.

If you’re standing at an empty place this morning, you’re not doing faith wrong. Mary was the first person in the world to see the risen Jesus, and she saw Him while she was still crying.

He was closer than she knew

“And when she had thus said, she turned herself back, and saw Jesus standing, and knew not that it was Jesus.” — John 20:14 (KJV)

He was right there. Not far off, not waiting for her to pull herself together. Standing behind her in the garden. And she didn’t recognize Him. She thought He was the gardener.

I find that strangely comforting. Sorrow blurs our eyes. We can be so sure God is absent that we mistake His presence for something ordinary — a kind word from a neighbor, a verse that stops us, a quiet strength we can’t explain getting through the day. Mary’s tears didn’t keep Jesus away. They only kept her from seeing that He’d already come.

Then He said her name

“Jesus saith unto her, Woman, why weepest thou? whom seekest thou?… Jesus saith unto her, Mary. She turned herself, and saith unto him, Rabboni; which is to say, Master.” — John 20:15–16 (KJV)

Notice what He doesn’t do. He doesn’t explain the resurrection. He doesn’t give her a sermon on prophecy. He doesn’t scold her for not recognizing Him. First He asks about her tears — why weepest thou? — as if they matter to Him. Then He says one word.

Mary.

Everything turned on that. She had been looking for a body; she was found by a Person who knew her. Earlier in this same Gospel, Jesus said the Good Shepherd “calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out” (John 10:3, KJV). In a garden at dawn, He did exactly that.

That’s still how He comes. Not usually with all the answers, but with your name. Not “person who’s struggling” or “the one who should be over this by now.” Your actual name, said by Someone who has known you all along and is not leaving.

For this Saturday morning

You don’t have to stop grieving to be found. You don’t have to recognize Him first. Mary didn’t. You only have to stay near and let Him speak.

So maybe today, before the errands and the chores, sit with your coffee and say it honestly: Lord, here’s where it hurts. Here’s what I lost. I don’t know where You are in this. Then be quiet for a minute. Listen for your name.

Because the empty tomb wasn’t the end of Mary’s story. A few verses later she runs to the disciples with the best sentence anyone has ever said through drying tears: “I have seen the Lord” (John 20:18, KJV).

He’s in the garden this morning too. And He knows your name.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in John 20:11–16, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
