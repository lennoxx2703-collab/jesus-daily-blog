Neither Pray I for These Alone

November 11, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture John 17:20–21, 24

  • wednesday
  • evening
  • prayer
  • intercession
  • belonging
  • rest

Wednesday evening sits right in the middle of the week. Far enough in that you’re tired, not far enough that the end is in sight. The dishes are done, or they aren’t. The phone has gone quiet. And sometimes, in that quiet, you try to pray and find you don’t have much left to say.

If that’s where you are tonight, here is something to rest on: before you ever prayed for yourself, Jesus prayed for you.

A prayer on the last night

It was the night He was betrayed. The supper was over. He had washed their feet, told them He was going away, and promised them the Comforter. Then John tells us that Jesus “lifted up his eyes to heaven” (John 17:1, KJV) and prayed out loud, with His friends listening.

Most of that prayer is for the men in the room. He prays that they would be kept, that they would be made holy, that His joy would be fulfilled in them. They were about to scatter. Within hours one would deny Him three times. He knew all of it, and He prayed for them anyway.

And then the prayer reaches past the room.

“Neither pray I for these alone, but for them also which shall believe on me through their word; that they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us” (John 17:20–21, KJV).

Them also which shall believe. That is you. If you have trusted Jesus because the word about Him reached you, through a parent, a friend, a Bible left in a drawer, a blog post read late at night, then on the night before the cross you were on His mind. Long before you knew His name, He had already brought you to His Father.

What He asked for

Look at what He asked. Not that you would be impressive. Not that you would have an easy life. He asked that you would be one, held together with other believers and held inside the love that runs between the Father and the Son.

Then He went further:

“Father, I will that they also, whom thou hast given me, be with me where I am; that they may behold my glory” (John 17:24, KJV).

Read that slowly. The deepest desire Jesus voiced on His last night was that you would be with Him. Not tolerated near the door. With Him, where He is, seeing Him as He is.

That is how He feels about you tonight, on an ordinary Wednesday, with your unfinished list and your tired heart.

He still prays

That prayer in the upper room was not a one-time event. Scripture says the risen Jesus “ever liveth to make intercession” for those who come to God by Him (Hebrews 7:25, KJV). Paul asks, “Who is he that condemneth? It is Christ that died, yea rather, that is risen again, who is even at the right hand of God, who also maketh intercession for us” (Romans 8:34, KJV).

So when your own prayers feel thin, you are not the only one praying. The One who knows you best is speaking to the Father about you. Your faith doesn’t hang on how well you pray tonight. It rests on how faithfully He prays.

For tonight

You don’t need many words before sleep. Try this:

  • Read John 17:20–24 once, slowly. Put your own name where it says “them also which shall believe.”
  • Thank Him for praying for you. Even one sentence is enough: Lord Jesus, thank You that You prayed for me before I was born, and that You pray for me now.
  • Think of one other believer who is having a hard week, and pray for them. You’ll be joining the prayer He started: that we would be one.

Then let it go. Turn the light off. The night after He prayed, Jesus walked across the brook Cedron into a garden and toward the cross, so that His prayer for you would be answered.

You are wanted. You are prayed for. You can rest.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in John 17:20–21, 24, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
