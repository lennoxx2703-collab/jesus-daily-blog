She Hath Done What She Could

November 19, 2026 · Evening · 4 min read

Scripture Mark 14:3–9

  • thursday
  • evening
  • love
  • devotion
  • enough
  • rest

Most evenings come with a quiet audit. You look back over the day and count what didn’t get done: the call you meant to make, the patience you ran out of, the prayer that got squeezed into thirty seconds in the car. It’s easy to end the day feeling like whatever you gave Jesus was too little, too clumsy, or too late.

There’s a moment in Mark’s Gospel for evenings like that.

“And being in Bethany in the house of Simon the leper, as he sat at meat, there came a woman having an alabaster box of ointment of spikenard very precious; and she brake the box, and poured it on his head. And there were some that had indignation within themselves, and said, Why was this waste of the ointment made?” — Mark 14:3–4 (KJV)

Picture the room. A dinner just days before the cross. Men reclining around the table, talking. And a woman walks in, breaks open something worth a small fortune, and pours all of it out on Jesus. No speech. No explanation. Just the smell of spikenard filling the house.

And the room turns on her. Waste. That’s the word they reach for. Think of what that money could have done. They aren’t wrong about the math — but they’re completely wrong about the moment.

Let her alone

“And Jesus said, Let her alone; why trouble ye her? she hath wrought a good work on me… She hath done what she could: she is come aforehand to anoint my body to the burying.” — Mark 14:6, 8 (KJV)

Let her alone. Jesus steps between her and the criticism. He doesn’t let the room’s verdict be the final word on what she gave. He names it a “good work” — a beautiful thing.

Then He says the sentence I want you to carry to bed tonight: “She hath done what she could.”

Not “she did everything.” Not “she fixed the problem.” Not “she did it perfectly.” She did what she could, with what she had, while she had the chance — and Jesus received it as love.

She probably didn’t fully understand what she was doing. Jesus tells them she was anointing His body for burial ahead of time. She just knew she loved Him and the moment was now. Her devotion was bigger than her understanding, and that was enough.

What looks like waste

Some of what you give Jesus will look like waste to other people. Time spent in prayer when there was “real work” to do. Kindness to someone who will never pay it back. Faithfulness in a job or a home that no one notices. Tears poured out in a prayer that seems to change nothing.

Love given to Jesus is never wasted, even when it can’t be measured. He sees the broken box. He smells the ointment. He knows what it cost you.

And notice — He doesn’t ask her for more. He doesn’t say, “Good, but next time also feed the poor.” He simply receives her gift and defends it. That’s the heart of the One you’re coming to tonight.

Remembered

“Verily I say unto you, Wheresoever this gospel shall be preached throughout the whole world, this shall be spoken of for a memorial of her.” — Mark 14:9 (KJV)

We don’t even get her name in Mark’s telling. But Jesus made sure her small, costly act would be remembered wherever the good news goes. Two thousand years later, here we are, still talking about it.

What you did for Him today — however small, however unfinished — is not forgotten by Him either.

For tonight

Before you sleep, don’t run through the list of everything you failed to do. Instead, try this:

  • Name one thing you gave Jesus today, even if it felt tiny: a whispered prayer, a choice to be gentle, a moment of trust.
  • Hand Him the rest — the unfinished, the clumsy, the too-little. He is not the critic at the table.
  • Hear His words over you: Let her alone. Let him alone. They did what they could.

Lord Jesus, I didn’t do everything today, and some of what I did was small. But I give it to You — the little I had, the love I could manage. Thank You for receiving it, for defending me when I’m my own harshest critic, and for being worth every broken box. Let me rest tonight in Your kindness. Amen.

Sleep in peace. You did what you could, and He calls it good.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Mark 14:3–9, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
