The Lord Turned, and Looked upon Peter

November 25, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture Luke 22:60–62

  • wednesday
  • evening
  • failure
  • regret
  • grace
  • restoration

Some evenings you lie down replaying one moment. The thing you said. The thing you didn’t say. The moment you had a chance to stand with Jesus and you quietly stepped back into the crowd. It wasn’t dramatic. Nobody else even noticed. But you did. And now the house is quiet, and the memory is loud.

Peter knew that kind of night better than anyone.

“And Peter said, Man, I know not what thou sayest. And immediately, while he yet spake, the cock crew. And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. And Peter went out, and wept bitterly.” — Luke 22:60–62 (KJV)

Only a few hours earlier, Peter had promised he would go to prison and to death for Jesus. He meant it. Then came a fire in a courtyard, a servant girl, a couple of bystanders, and a fear he didn’t know was in him. Three times he said it: I don’t know Him.

And then the rooster. And then the detail only Luke gives us: the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter.

What was in that look?

We tend to imagine it the way we imagine our own failures being seen — a hard stare, a disappointed shake of the head, I knew you would. But think about who was looking. Jesus was bound, mocked, on His way to being beaten. He had every reason to be consumed by His own suffering. And in the middle of it, He turned. He looked for Peter.

This is the same Jesus who had told him that very night, “I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren” (Luke 22:32). He wasn’t surprised by Peter’s fall. He had already prayed past it. He had already planned Peter’s future on the other side of it.

So the look didn’t break Peter because it was cruel. It broke him because it was love — love he had just denied, still looking at him.

Bitter tears aren’t the end

Peter went out and wept bitterly. Those tears were real, and they were right. Grace doesn’t ask us to pretend we didn’t fail. Sometimes the most honest prayer you can pray tonight is just tears.

But notice: bitter tears were not the last word. Judas also felt regret that night, and it drove him away into despair. Peter’s sorrow, as heavy as it was, kept him close enough to be found. He was still with the disciples on Sunday morning. Still in the room. Still within reach.

And when the angel spoke at the empty tomb, the message carried two words just for him:

“But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.” — Mark 16:7 (KJV)

And Peter. Not “the disciples, minus the one who failed.” By name. As if heaven wanted to make sure he didn’t count himself out.

For your evening

Whatever you’re replaying tonight, I want you to know this: Jesus has already turned toward you. He saw the moment. He saw it before it happened. And the look in His eyes is not the one your shame has been describing to you.

You don’t need to fix it all before you sleep. Let the tears be honest if they come. Then stay close — don’t run off into the dark like the failure gets the final say. Tomorrow He goes before you, just like He went before Peter into Galilee.

Maybe pray something like this:

Lord Jesus, You saw what I did today, and You saw what I didn’t do. I’m sorry. Thank You that You turned and looked at me with love and not contempt. Thank You that You prayed for me before I fell. Keep my faith from failing tonight. Say my name again in the morning. Amen.

He hasn’t crossed you off the list. He’s still saying, and Peter — and He’s saying your name too.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Luke 22:60–62, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
