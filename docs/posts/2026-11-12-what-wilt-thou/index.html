What Wilt Thou That I Should Do unto Thee?

November 12, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture Mark 10:46–52

  • thursday
  • morning
  • prayer
  • mercy
  • bartimaeus
  • healing

Some mornings you wake up already knowing what you need. Other mornings you just feel the weight and can’t quite put a name to it. Either way, the day starts moving, the crowd of tasks gets loud, and it’s easy to decide your need can wait until things quiet down.

There was a man who couldn’t afford to wait.

“And they came to Jericho: and as he went out of Jericho with his disciples and a great number of people, blind Bartimaeus, the son of Timaeus, sat by the highway side begging. And when he heard that it was Jesus of Nazareth, he began to cry out, and say, Jesus, thou son of David, have mercy on me. And many charged him that he should hold his peace: but he cried the more a great deal, Thou son of David, have mercy on me.” (Mark 10:46–48, KJV)

Louder than the crowd

Bartimaeus sat where beggars sat, by the side of the road, with a cloak spread out for coins. He couldn’t see Jesus. He could only hear that He was passing. And he knew that if he stayed polite, the moment would walk right past him.

So he shouted. People told him to be quiet. He shouted louder.

I love that the Gospel keeps this detail. The crowd around Jesus wasn’t always a help. Sometimes the people closest to Him were the ones saying, Not now. Don’t bother Him. You’re making a scene. Maybe you hear voices like that in your own head: your problem is too small, too old, too embarrassing; you’ve already asked about this before. Bartimaeus didn’t argue with them. He just kept calling the one Name that mattered.

Jesus stood still

“And Jesus stood still, and commanded him to be called. And they call the blind man, saying unto him, Be of good comfort, rise; he calleth thee.” (Mark 10:49, KJV)

Jesus was on His way to Jerusalem, to the cross. He had every reason to keep walking. And He stopped for one blind man at the edge of the road.

That’s who He still is. The same voice that was told to hush is the voice Jesus hears over all the noise. And notice how quickly the crowd changes its tune once Jesus calls: Be of good comfort, rise; he calleth thee. That’s the best news anyone can hear on a gray Thursday morning. He’s calling you.

Bartimaeus threw his cloak aside — maybe the only thing he owned, the thing that caught his coins — and came.

Say it plainly

Then Jesus asked a question that sounds almost strange:

“What wilt thou that I should do unto thee? The blind man said unto him, Lord, that I might receive my sight.” (Mark 10:51, KJV)

Of course Jesus knew. Everyone could see the man was blind. But Jesus wanted to hear it from him. Not a speech, not a performance — just the honest want, said out loud to His face.

We sometimes pray in fog. We say bless my day and be with everyone and never quite tell Him what’s actually aching. And it’s fine to start there. But Jesus invites more. He asks you, this morning, the same question: What do you want Me to do for you? Not what you think you’re supposed to want. What you actually need. Your sight, your peace, your marriage, your next step, your courage for one hard phone call.

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need” (Hebrews 4:16, KJV). Boldly. Like a beggar who won’t be shushed.

Followed Him in the way

“And Jesus said unto him, Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole. And immediately he received his sight, and followed Jesus in the way.” (Mark 10:52, KJV)

Jesus told him to go his way, and Bartimaeus chose Jesus’ way instead. The first thing his new eyes saw was the face of the Lord, and he never wanted to look anywhere else. He left the side of the road and joined the road itself.

So before the day gets loud, try this. Find a quiet minute — in the car, over coffee, at the sink. Call His name. Then answer His question honestly, in one plain sentence. And whatever He does with your request, get up and follow Him into the day.

Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me. You stopped for Bartimaeus; I believe You stop for me. Here’s what I need today. Open my eyes, and let me follow You in the way. Amen.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Mark 10:46–52, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
