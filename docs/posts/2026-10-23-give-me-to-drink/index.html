Give Me to Drink

October 23, 2026 · Midday · 4 min read

Scripture John 4:6–14

  • friday
  • thirst
  • living-water
  • grace
  • midday

There is a particular kind of tired that shows up around midday. The morning’s energy has been spent, the afternoon still stretches ahead, and you find yourself standing somewhere, at a desk, a sink, a car park, simply wanting a drink of water and a moment to sit down.

John tells us Jesus knew that feeling.

“Now Jacob’s well was there. Jesus therefore, being wearied with his journey, sat thus on the well: and it was about the sixth hour. There cometh a woman of Samaria to draw water: Jesus saith unto her, Give me to drink.” (John 4:6–7, KJV)

The sixth hour is noon. The hottest, emptiest part of the day. Most women came to the well in the cool of the morning or evening, together, talking as they filled their jars. This woman came alone, at the hour when nobody else would be there. Whatever her reasons, she had learned that it was easier to carry her water in the heat than to carry the looks of her neighbours.

And there, sitting on the edge of the well, was a tired Jewish man who should, by every rule of the day, have ignored her.

He asked first

What strikes me is how Jesus begins. He doesn’t open with a sermon or a correction. He asks her for help. “Give me to drink.” The Son of God, wearied by the road, lets Himself need something from a person everyone else avoided.

That is often how He still comes to us. Not by towering over our lives, but by sitting down in the middle of them and starting a conversation. He meets us at the ordinary places we go every day, the routine errands and the noon breaks, and He speaks first.

She is surprised, of course. Jews and Samaritans did not share cups. But Jesus gently turns the conversation:

“If thou knewest the gift of God, and who it is that saith to thee, Give me to drink; thou wouldest have asked of him, and he would have given thee living water.” (John 4:10, KJV)

The water that runs out

She points out the obvious: He has nothing to draw with, and the well is deep. Then Jesus says something about every well we have ever leaned on:

“Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again: But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life.” (John 4:13–14, KJV)

Think about the wells you go back to. The coffee that gets you through the afternoon. The approval that feels good for an hour. The scrolling, the busyness, the relationship you hoped would finally fill the empty place. None of these are evil in themselves, but they all share the same honest limit: drink, and you will be thirsty again.

Jesus doesn’t shame her for being thirsty. He doesn’t shame you either. He simply offers a different kind of water, not a bucket you have to keep hauling up, but a spring He places inside you. Something that keeps rising even on the days you have nothing left to draw with.

She left her waterpot

Later in the conversation Jesus tells her the truth about her life, the five husbands and the man who is not her husband. And what’s remarkable is that being fully known by Him doesn’t send her back into hiding. It sets her free.

“The woman then left her waterpot, and went her way into the city, and saith to the men, Come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did: is not this the Christ?” (John 4:28–29, KJV)

The woman who came at noon to avoid people runs back to the very people she was avoiding. She forgets the jar she came for. She has found what she was actually thirsty for.

For your midday

So wherever you are as you read this, pause for a moment. Maybe take an actual glass of water and drink it slowly. And as you do, tell Jesus honestly where you are thirsty today. Which wells you keep going back to. What you have been carrying alone in the heat.

He is already sitting there, tired from the same road you are on, and He is the one who asked first. You can ask Him now:

Lord Jesus, I am thirsty in ways I can’t always name. Give me the living water You promised. Let it spring up in me this afternoon, and help me set down the jars I no longer need. Amen.

“And the Spirit and the bride say, Come. And let him that heareth say, Come. And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely.” (Revelation 22:17, KJV)

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in John 4:6–14, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
