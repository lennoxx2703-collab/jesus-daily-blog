Save Jesus Only

October 29, 2026 · Morning · 3 min read

Scripture Matthew 17:5–8

  • thursday
  • transfiguration
  • fear
  • focus
  • morning

Thursday mornings can feel crowded before they even begin. The list from Monday is still half-done, Friday is close enough to make you hurry, and your mind wakes up already holding five voices at once: what you should have said, what you need to fix, what someone might think.

Matthew tells us about a morning on a mountain that got very crowded, too.

Jesus took Peter, James, and John up a high mountain, and there He was transfigured before them. “His face did shine as the sun, and his raiment was white as the light” (Matthew 17:2, KJV). Then Moses and Elijah appeared, talking with Him. The law and the prophets, the two greatest names in Israel’s story, standing on the same ground as the carpenter from Nazareth.

Peter, as Peter does, felt he had to do something. “Lord, it is good for us to be here: if thou wilt, let us make here three tabernacles; one for thee, and one for Moses, and one for Elias” (Matthew 17:4, KJV). It was a kind thought. It was also a very human one: when something holy happens, we want to build around it, organize it, keep it, and give everyone equal space.

But the Father interrupted him.

“While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them: and behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him. And when the disciples heard it, they fell on their face, and were sore afraid. And Jesus came and touched them, and said, Arise, and be not afraid. And when they had lifted up their eyes, they saw no man, save Jesus only.” (Matthew 17:5–8, KJV)

Three small things in this passage are worth carrying into today.

God answered Peter’s busyness with one word: Him. Not three tents. Not a plan. “Hear ye him.” Of all the voices on that mountain, and of all the voices in your head this morning, the Father points to one. You don’t have to sort every thought before breakfast. You only have to turn toward the Son He loves.

Jesus came to them on the ground. The disciples fell on their faces, terrified. And Matthew doesn’t say a voice told them to get up. He says, “Jesus came and touched them.” The same hands that had just been shining like the sun reached down to frightened fishermen lying in the dirt. The glory didn’t make Him distant. It made Him come closer. If you start today already low, already tired or afraid, that is exactly where His hand finds people.

When they looked up, the crowd was gone. Moses had left. Elijah had left. The cloud had lifted. “They saw no man, save Jesus only.” I don’t think that was a loss. I think it was the whole point. The bright moment faded, and what remained was not an experience but a Person, the same Jesus who would now walk down the mountain with them into a hard week.

Because that’s what happened next. “And as they came down from the mountain” (Matthew 17:9, KJV), they walked straight into a crowd, a desperate father, and disciples who had failed to help his son. The mountaintop didn’t last. Jesus did.

Maybe that’s a good word for a Thursday. Most of us aren’t on a mountain today. We’re on the way down, into ordinary rooms and unfinished tasks. But the One who touched the disciples’ shoulders is the same One who goes down the path with you.

So before the day fills up, try this. Close your eyes for a moment and let the other voices step back, the way Moses and Elijah did. Then lift your eyes and look for Jesus only. Not a perfect plan, not a feeling to hold onto. Just Him, near, saying what He said then: Arise, and be not afraid.

“Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith” (Hebrews 12:2, KJV) isn’t a mountaintop discipline. It’s a Thursday-morning one. Get up. He’s already here.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Matthew 17:5–8, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
