Enter into Thy Closet

November 4, 2026 · Midday · 4 min read

Scripture Matthew 6:6–8

  • wednesday
  • prayer
  • quiet
  • father
  • sermon-on-the-mount
  • midday

Midday on a Wednesday is loud. Not always in volume, but in pressure. There’s the inbox, the half-finished task, the conversation you’re rehearsing for later, the phone buzzing face-down on the table. Even when we stop for lunch, our minds rarely stop with us. We’re surrounded, all day long, by people and screens that want something from us.

Into that kind of day, Jesus speaks a surprisingly simple sentence.

“But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly. But when ye pray, use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking. Be not ye therefore like unto them: for your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask him.” (Matthew 6:6–8, KJV)

A door you’re allowed to shut

Jesus said this to people who knew prayer mostly as something public. Prayers were said on street corners and in the synagogue, where everyone could hear how well you did it. Jesus doesn’t throw that out, but He gently moves the center of prayer somewhere else: a small room, a closed door, and you alone with your Father.

The word “closet” here was an inner storeroom, the kind of plain back room an ordinary house had. It wasn’t holy. It was just private. That’s good news for a busy day. You don’t need a chapel or a free hour. You need a door, even a small one. It might be a parked car, a stairwell, a bathroom stall, or two minutes with your eyes closed at your desk. Jesus gives you permission to shut out the noise, even briefly, and be seen by nobody but God.

Seen in secret

Notice how often Jesus uses the word secret in this short passage. Your Father is “in secret.” He “seeth in secret.” So much of our day is performance. We manage how we look in meetings, in messages, even at church. In the closet, there’s no audience to impress. And Jesus says that’s exactly where the Father is waiting.

That means you can come as you actually are at 1:30 on a Wednesday: distracted, a little behind, maybe irritated with someone, maybe quietly afraid about something you haven’t told anyone. The Father who sees in secret has already seen it. You aren’t revealing anything to Him. You’re simply agreeing to be with Him in it.

You don’t have to find the perfect words

Then Jesus says something that lifts a weight many of us didn’t know we were carrying. Don’t pile up words, He says, as though God will hear you better if you say more. “Your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask him.”

So why ask at all, if He already knows? Because prayer was never mainly about informing God. It’s about being with Him. A child who runs to a parent with a scraped knee isn’t delivering a report. They want to be held. When Jesus teaches us to pray, the very next words are “Our Father.” The closet isn’t a place where you work hard to get God’s attention. It’s a place where you remember you already have it.

That frees your midday prayer to be short and honest. Father, I’m tired. Help me with this afternoon. Be near the person I’m worried about. That’s enough. He knew before you asked, and He’s glad you came.

Back out the door

Jesus says the Father “shall reward thee openly.” We don’t always see what that looks like right away. But often the reward is quiet: a steadier heart walking back into the meeting, a softer word for a coworker, a bit of peace that doesn’t match the circumstances. What happened behind the closed door starts to show on the open side of it.

So sometime in the next hour, find a door. Shut it for a minute or two. Say “Father,” and then say whatever is true. You won’t be heard because of your many words. You’ll be heard because He is your Father, and He’s already there.

“Trust in him at all times; ye people, pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us.” (Psalm 62:8, KJV)

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in Matthew 6:6–8, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
