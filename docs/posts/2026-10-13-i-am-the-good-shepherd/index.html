I Am the Good Shepherd

October 13, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture John 10:11

  • shepherd
  • trust
  • belonging
  • comfort

Tuesday evening often finds us a little scattered. The day’s tasks are mostly done, but the mind is still sorting what went well, what didn’t, and who showed up for you when it mattered. Some of us feel looked after. Some of us feel like we spent the whole day fending for ourselves.

Tonight I want to sit with a picture Jesus gave of Himself, because it was meant for hearts that need to know they are not alone in the dark.

Not a hired hand

Jesus said:

“I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep. But he that is an hireling, and not the shepherd, whose own the sheep are not, seeth the wolf coming, and leaveth the sheep, and fleeth: and the wolf catcheth them, and scattereth the sheep.” (John 10:11–12, KJV)

A hired hand clocks out when danger shows up. The sheep are a job to him, not a love. Jesus refuses that category. He is not managing you from a safe distance. He is not waiting to see if you are worth the trouble. He calls Himself the good shepherd — and the mark of that goodness is costly: He gives His life for the sheep.

If today left you feeling disposable, replaceable, or easy to abandon, hear Him. You are not a shift He can skip. You are someone He stays for.

He knows His own

Jesus keeps going:

“I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep, and am known of mine. As the Father knoweth me, even so know I the Father: and I lay down my life for the sheep.” (John 10:14–15, KJV)

I know my sheep. Not as a crowd. Not as a statistic. The way a shepherd who has walked the same hills for years knows which lamb limps, which one strays toward the thorns, which one startles at every sound. He knows you that way — the tired parts, the brave parts, the parts you hide when the day gets loud.

And He says you can know Him too. Relationship with Jesus is not a one-way surveillance. It is mutual nearness. The same intimacy He shares with the Father is the pattern of how He holds you: close, personal, faithful.

When the night feels unsafe

In Jesus’s world, night was when wolves prowled and flocks needed watching. That is still how many of our evenings feel. Old worries wake up. Loneliness gets louder. You replay a hard conversation. You wonder who would actually come if you called.

The Good Shepherd does not pretend the wolf is imaginary. He names the danger, and then He places Himself between it and you. The cross is that placement forever: He laid down His life so that nothing — not shame, not death, not the scattered feeling of a long Tuesday — gets the final claim on you.

You do not have to be the shepherd of your own soul tonight. That job is taken.

For tonight

Before you sleep, try this simple prayer. Say it slowly, even if your faith feels thin:

Lord Jesus, You are the Good Shepherd. You know me. You stay. You laid down Your life for me. Gather what feels scattered in me tonight, and keep me close until morning.

Then rest as a sheep under watch — not because you managed the day perfectly, but because He is good, and He has not left the field.

He knows your name. He is not going anywhere. Sleep in that.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in John 10:11, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
