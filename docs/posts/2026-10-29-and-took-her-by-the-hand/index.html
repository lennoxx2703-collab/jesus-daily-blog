And Took Her by the Hand

October 29, 2026 · Midday · 4 min read

Scripture Mark 1:29–31

  • thursday
  • healing
  • home
  • being-noticed
  • midday

Midday on a Thursday is a strange in-between place. The morning’s energy has worn off, the afternoon still stretches ahead, and if something has been quietly wrong all week (a tiredness, a worry, a low fever of the soul) this is usually when you feel it.

Mark tells us about a midday-ish moment like that. It doesn’t happen on a mountain or in a crowd. It happens in a house.

“And forthwith, when they were come out of the synagogue, they entered into the house of Simon and Andrew, with James and John. But Simon’s wife’s mother lay sick of a fever, and anon they tell him of her. And he came and took her by the hand, and lifted her up; and immediately the fever left her, and she ministered unto them.” (Mark 1:29–31, KJV)

Lying in the back room

It’s the Sabbath. Jesus has just taught in the synagogue at Capernaum, and people are astonished. He has just cast out an unclean spirit in front of everyone. It’s a big public morning.

Then they walk home for the meal, and the house is not ready. Someone is missing from the table. Simon’s mother-in-law is lying down with a fever, probably in a back room, probably embarrassed that guests have arrived and she can’t get up.

I love that Scripture names this. Not every hard thing in life is dramatic. Some of it is just lying down when you’d rather be up. It’s being sick on the day that mattered. It’s feeling like you’re the reason things aren’t going the way they should.

If that’s you at midday today, you’re not too small a story for Jesus. Mark puts this quiet sickbed right after the synagogue miracle, as if to say that the same Lord who commands spirits also walks into kitchens.

“Anon they tell him of her”

Notice how simple the next step is: they tell him of her. Nobody gives a speech. Nobody earns anything. Someone just mentions her to Jesus.

That’s what prayer often is at midday. Not a polished hour. Just a sentence: Lord, she’s sick. Lord, I’m worn out. Lord, my friend is struggling and I don’t know what to do. Telling Jesus is enough to bring Him into the room.

Maybe you can’t fix the person you’re worried about today. You can tell Him about them. That counts.

He came close

Then Mark gives us three tender verbs: He came. He took her by the hand. He lifted her up.

Jesus didn’t heal her from the doorway, though He could have. He came close enough to touch a feverish hand. He took hold of her, not waiting for her to reach for Him. And He lifted, using His own strength to raise her when she had none.

That’s the shape of His care for you too. Isaiah heard God say it centuries before: “For I the LORD thy God will hold thy right hand, saying unto thee, Fear not; I will help thee” (Isaiah 41:13, KJV). Jesus is that promise walking into a house in Capernaum.

You don’t have to climb out of your fever before He’ll come. He comes to where you’re lying down.

Lifted to serve

The last line is easy to skip: “and she ministered unto them.” The fever left immediately, and her first move was to get up and care for the people in her home.

That isn’t Jesus putting her straight back to work. It’s a picture of what restoration looks like: being lifted up so fully that you have something to give again. Healing in the Gospels isn’t only about getting rid of something. It’s about getting your life back, your place at the table, your joy in serving.

So this afternoon, if you’ve felt sidelined, keep watching for His hand. When He lifts you, even a little, the next small kindness you do for someone, whether it’s a cup of coffee, a kind text, or patience with a coworker, can be your way of saying thank you.

A midday prayer

Lord Jesus, You walked out of a crowded synagogue and into a quiet house where someone was lying sick. Come into the back rooms of my life today. I’m telling You about the people I’m carrying and the weariness I’m carrying too. Take my hand. Lift me up. And when You do, let me serve the people around me with the strength You give. Amen.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in Mark 1:29–31, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
