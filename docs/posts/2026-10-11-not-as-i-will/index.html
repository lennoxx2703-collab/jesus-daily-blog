Not As I Will

October 11, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture Matthew 26:39

  • prayer
  • surrender
  • trust
  • anxiety

Sunday evening has its own particular weight. The weekend is closing, and Monday is already leaning in through the window: the meeting you dread, the bill you haven’t opened, the conversation you keep putting off, the diagnosis you’re waiting on. Maybe nothing is wrong at all, and you just feel tired before the week has even started.

Tonight I want to take you to a garden at night, because Jesus spent one of His hardest evenings there.

A garden called Gethsemane

After the last supper, Jesus walked with His friends across the Kidron valley to an olive grove. He knew what the morning would bring. Betrayal, arrest, mockery, a cross. And He didn’t pretend otherwise. Matthew tells us what He said to Peter, James, and John:

“My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death: tarry ye here, and watch with me.” (Matthew 26:38, KJV)

Sit with that for a moment. The Son of God said, out loud, I am overwhelmed. He didn’t put on a brave face for His disciples. He told them the truth, and He asked them to stay close.

If you’ve ever felt ashamed of being anxious or heavy-hearted, let this free you a little. Sorrow isn’t a lack of faith. Jesus felt it fully, and He was without sin.

The honest prayer

Then He went a little further, fell on His face, and prayed:

“O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt.” (Matthew 26:39, KJV)

There are two halves to that prayer, and both matter.

The first half is honest: Father, if there is another way, please. Jesus didn’t hide what He wanted. He asked plainly. You’re allowed to do the same. You can tell God tonight exactly what you hope Monday holds, and what you hope it doesn’t.

The second half is trust: Nevertheless, not as I will. This isn’t giving up. It’s handing the outcome to Someone who loves you more than you love yourself. Jesus wasn’t surrendering to fate. He was surrendering to His Father.

Most of us want to skip one half. Some of us only pray the first part and then panic when the answer isn’t what we asked. Others jump straight to “Thy will be done” without ever admitting what we actually feel. Gethsemane shows us the whole thing: honesty first, then trust.

When your friends fall asleep

When Jesus came back, His closest friends were asleep.

“What, could ye not watch with me one hour? Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation: the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak.” (Matthew 26:40–41, KJV)

There’s no cruelty in His words. He even makes room for them: the spirit indeed is willing. He knew they loved Him. He knew they were tired.

Maybe you’ve had a night like that, where the people who love you couldn’t quite stay awake with your pain. Jesus knows that loneliness from the inside. And here’s the gift of it: because He went through Gethsemane alone, you never have to. He’s the friend who stays awake.

Luke adds a small, beautiful detail:

“And there appeared an angel unto him from heaven, strengthening him.” (Luke 22:43, KJV)

The cup didn’t pass. But strength came. Often that’s how the Father answers us too. Not always by removing the hard thing, but by meeting us in it with enough strength for the next step.

For tonight

Before you sleep, try praying the Gethsemane way. Find a quiet minute and say it in your own words:

Father, here’s what I’m afraid of this week. Here’s what I wish You would change. I’m telling You honestly. And nevertheless, not my will, but Yours. Strengthen me for tomorrow.

Then let it go into His hands. You don’t have to carry Monday tonight. Jesus already walked through the darkest garden so that you could walk into this week with Him beside you.

Rest well. He’s watching with you.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Matthew 26:39, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
