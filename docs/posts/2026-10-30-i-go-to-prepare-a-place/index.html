I Go to Prepare a Place

October 30, 2026 · Evening · 4 min read

Scripture John 14:1–3

  • friday
  • home
  • hope
  • worry
  • heaven
  • evening

Friday evening has its own kind of quiet. The week is finished, whether or not the work is. Some of us walk through the door with relief. Others walk in carrying what didn’t get resolved: a conversation that went badly, a bill, a test result, a worry about someone we love. The weekend opens up in front of us, and sometimes the open space is exactly where the anxious thoughts come in.

On the night before the cross, Jesus sat with friends whose hearts were full of that kind of trouble. He had just told them He was going away. Peter would deny Him. One of them would betray Him. The room felt like the ground was shifting. And into that room, Jesus said this:

“Let not your heart be troubled: ye believe in God, believe also in me. In my Father’s house are many mansions: if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you. And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also.” (John 14:1–3, KJV)

He spoke to the heart first

Notice where Jesus starts. Not with an explanation of everything that is about to happen, and not with a rebuke for being afraid. He goes straight to the heart: let not your heart be troubled.

That isn’t a command to pretend. Jesus Himself was troubled in spirit that same night. It’s an invitation to put the weight somewhere. Ye believe in God, believe also in me. In other words: the trouble is real, but it doesn’t get the last word, because I am here and I am trustworthy. The disciples didn’t need every answer. They needed to know whose hands they were in.

You may not get answers tonight either. But you can bring your troubled heart to the same Jesus and let Him speak to it before you go to bed.

There is room

In my Father’s house are many mansions. The older word means dwelling places, rooms. Jesus pictures a large family home, the kind where no one is turned away at the door, where there is always one more place at the table.

And then He adds a line that is easy to read past: if it were not so, I would have told you. Jesus doesn’t flatter. He told His friends hard things all evening long. So when He says there is room, He means it. There is room for the one who failed this week. Room for the one who feels like a guest at best. Room for you.

He is preparing it

I go to prepare a place for you. Jesus was about to walk out of that room toward Gethsemane and the cross. That was the preparing. The way into the Father’s house would be opened by His own body given and His own blood poured out, and then by an empty tomb.

So the place isn’t a vague hope. It’s been paid for. Whatever you are worried about tonight, it is held inside a bigger story where Jesus has already gone ahead of you and done the hardest part.

The point is Him

Listen to how the promise ends: I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also.

The best thing about the Father’s house is not the rooms. It’s who will be there. Jesus doesn’t only promise a place; He promises Himself. Near the end of Scripture, John hears the same hope from heaven:

“Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God. And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes.” (Revelation 21:3–4, KJV)

That’s where all of this is going. Not to an empty house, but to a home with Him in it.

Coming home tonight

As you settle in this Friday evening, try this. Name the one thing that is troubling your heart most. Say it out loud to Jesus, plainly. Then hear Him say back to you what He said to His friends: Let not your heart be troubled. Believe also in me. There is room. I’m preparing it. I’m coming back for you.

You don’t have to solve the weekend tonight. You only have to rest your heart in the One who has already gone ahead and is getting your place ready.

Lord Jesus, You see what I’m carrying home tonight. Thank You that I can believe in You when I can’t see the way. Thank You that there is room for me in Your Father’s house, and that You are preparing it. Keep my heart quiet in You tonight, and bring me safely home to where You are. Amen.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in John 14:1–3, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
