Jesus Rejoiced in Spirit

October 24, 2026 · Morning · 3 min read

Scripture Luke 10:17–21

  • saturday
  • joy
  • identity
  • prayer
  • morning

Saturday mornings have their own kind of quiet. The week is behind you, with its lists and meetings and small wins and small failures, and for a moment nobody is asking you for a report.

Luke tells us about a day when a group of Jesus’ followers came back to Him with a report anyway, and they could hardly contain it.

“And the seventy returned again with joy, saying, Lord, even the devils are subject unto us through thy name.” (Luke 10:17, KJV)

Jesus had sent them out two by two, into towns He Himself was about to visit. No purse, no extra shoes, just His name and His peace. And it worked. People were healed. Darkness backed away. They came home buzzing, the way you do when something finally goes right.

What He told them to be glad about

Jesus didn’t wave their excitement away. He shared it: “I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven” (Luke 10:18). He was glad with them.

But then He gently moved their joy to firmer ground:

“Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.” (Luke 10:20, KJV)

That’s a remarkable thing to say to people on their best day. Don’t build your gladness on what went well. Build it on something that was true before you left and will still be true after a bad week: you are known, and your name is kept by God.

Results come and go. Some weeks the seventy would come back with nothing but sore feet and closed doors. If their joy rested on outcomes, it would rise and crash with every trip. Jesus wanted them to have a joy that doesn’t need a good report.

Maybe this week gave you something to be proud of. Maybe it gave you mostly things you’d rather forget. Either way, the deepest reason for gladness hasn’t changed since Monday. Your name is still written in heaven, and not in pencil.

The moment Jesus was glad

Then Luke slips in a line that’s easy to miss:

“In that hour Jesus rejoiced in spirit, and said, I thank thee, O Father, Lord of heaven and earth, that thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them unto babes: even so, Father; for so it seemed good in thy sight.” (Luke 10:21, KJV)

We often picture Jesus as serious, sorrowful, the man of sorrows. And He was. But here He is full of joy, and the reason is beautiful: ordinary people were getting it. Fishermen and tax collectors and nobodies were seeing what the experts missed. That made Him glad enough to break into prayer.

It tells us something about His heart. Jesus isn’t impressed by credentials. He delights when a child-like person simply trusts Him. When you come to Him without polish this morning, coffee in hand, still half-asleep, you aren’t disappointing Him. That’s the very kind of coming that made Him rejoice.

For your Saturday

You don’t have to earn a restful day by having had a productive week. Before you plan anything, try this:

  • Let Him hear your report. Tell Jesus how the week really went, the good and the hard. He listens like He listened to the seventy.
  • Move your joy to firmer ground. Say it out loud if it helps: “My name is written in heaven.” Let that be enough for today.
  • Come as a child. Skip the impressive words. Just thank the Father for something simple you can see right now.

Jesus rejoiced in spirit over people like you. Let this Saturday begin there.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Luke 10:17–21, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
