Not My Will, but Thine

November 13, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture Luke 22:41–44

  • friday
  • evening
  • prayer
  • surrender
  • gethsemane
  • strength

Friday evening. The week lets go of you slowly. Maybe you are relieved. Maybe you are carrying something home that the weekend won’t fix: a decision still hanging, a diagnosis, a person you can’t reach, a fear you haven’t said out loud.

Tonight I want to take you into a garden, on a Thursday night long ago, the night before the first Good Friday.

“And he was withdrawn from them about a stone’s cast, and kneeled down, and prayed, saying, Father, if thou be willing, remove this cup from me: nevertheless not my will, but thine, be done. And there appeared an angel unto him from heaven, strengthening him. And being in an agony he prayed more earnestly: and his sweat was as it were great drops of blood falling down to the ground.” — Luke 22:41–44 (KJV)

He said what He wanted

The first thing I notice is how honest Jesus is. He doesn’t open with something polished. He says, Father, if thou be willing, remove this cup from me. In Mark He calls Him “Abba” and says, “all things are possible unto thee; take away this cup from me” (Mark 14:36).

The Son of God asked for a way out.

He knew what was coming and He did not pretend it was easy. To Peter, James and John He said, “My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death: tarry ye here, and watch with me” (Matthew 26:38). That is not weakness. It is love that is fully human, feeling the full weight.

So you are allowed to tell the Father what you actually want. You don’t have to dress it up as spiritual first. Jesus didn’t.

Then He let go of it

But He didn’t stop there. Nevertheless. That one word holds the whole night. “Not my will, but thine, be done.”

This is not resignation, the shrug of someone who has given up. It’s trust. He handed the cup back into the hands of a Father He knew was good, even while every part of Him was trembling.

Surrender in the Bible almost never means I feel fine about this now. It means I trust You with it, even though I don’t.

He was strengthened, not spared

Then this line, which I love: “There appeared an angel unto him from heaven, strengthening him.”

The cup wasn’t taken away. The cross still came. But He was not left alone in it. The Father answered, not by removing the burden, but by giving strength to carry it. And notice what came next: “he prayed more earnestly.” Being strengthened didn’t end the prayer. It kept Him praying.

Hebrews says Jesus “offered up prayers and supplications with strong crying and tears unto him that was able to save him from death, and was heard” (Hebrews 5:7). Heard. Even in the garden, even in the agony, heard.

And He went through with it for you. That cup He drank so that you would never have to stand before God alone.

For tonight

Here is a simple way to end your Friday.

Find your own stone’s cast: a chair, the edge of your bed, a quiet minute in the car. Then pray in the shape Jesus showed you:

Father, here is what I want — and say it plainly. Nevertheless, not my will, but Thine. Strengthen me for whatever comes.

You might not feel anything change. The disciples nearby were “sleeping for sorrow” (Luke 22:45); some nights that’s all we can manage too. But the One who knelt in Gethsemane knows this ground. He has walked every step of it before you, and He is with you on it now.

Lay it down tonight. He is able. And He is good.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Luke 22:41–44, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
