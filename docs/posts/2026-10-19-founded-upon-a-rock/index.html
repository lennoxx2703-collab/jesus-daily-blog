Founded Upon a Rock

October 19, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture Matthew 7:24–25

  • monday
  • foundation
  • obedience
  • storms
  • sermon-on-the-mount

Monday has a way of arriving all at once. The inbox is full before the coffee is ready, the calendar has opinions about your day, and whatever you were carrying on Friday is still sitting there waiting for you. Before any of it gets loud, it’s worth asking a simple question: what am I standing on this week?

Jesus ended His Sermon on the Mount with exactly that question, told as a story about two houses.

“Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock: and the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell not: for it was founded upon a rock.” (Matthew 7:24–25, KJV)

Same storm, different ground

Notice what Jesus doesn’t promise. He doesn’t say the wise builder gets better weather. The rain descends on both houses. The floods come for both. The wind beats on both. Following Jesus is not a way around the storm, and if you’ve walked with Him for any length of time, you already know that.

What He does promise is that the house on the rock fell not. It was shaken, soaked, and battered, and it was still standing in the morning.

The difference was never the house. Both builders might have used the same timber and the same nails. The difference was what lay underneath, the part nobody sees once the walls go up.

Digging deep

Luke’s version adds a detail that makes it feel very real:

“He is like a man which built an house, and digged deep, and laid the foundation on a rock: and when the flood arose, the stream beat vehemently upon that house, and could not shake it: for it was founded upon a rock.” (Luke 6:48, KJV)

Digged deep. That’s slow, unglamorous work. It’s shovels and sore backs and days when it looks like nothing is happening. Sand is quicker. You can have walls up on sand while the other builder is still in a hole.

Most of the deep digging in a life of faith looks just as ordinary. It’s reading a few verses of the Gospels when you’d rather scroll. It’s a short prayer in the car. It’s choosing to forgive again, telling the truth when a small lie would be easier, and doing the kind thing nobody will notice. None of it feels like much on a Monday. All of it is foundation.

Hearing and doing

Jesus is clear that the rock isn’t just knowing His words. Both builders heard. The wise one heard and did.

That’s good news, not a heavier load. It means you don’t need to master the whole Sermon on the Mount by Friday. You need to take one thing He said and actually lean your weight on it today. Maybe it’s “Blessed are the merciful.” Maybe it’s “Take no thought for the morrow.” Maybe it’s simply “Ask, and it shall be given you.” Pick one, and build on it.

And remember who the Rock is. Jesus isn’t only handing out good building advice. He’s the foundation Himself. When the people heard Him finish this sermon, Matthew says, “they were astonished at his doctrine: for he taught them as one having authority” (Matthew 7:28–29, KJV). His words hold because He holds.

For this week

Before the week picks up speed, take a minute and name the storm you can already see coming: a hard conversation, a deadline, a diagnosis, a tired marriage, a lonely evening. Then put one of His words underneath it on purpose.

You might pray: Lord Jesus, I don’t know what weather this week will bring, but I want to be standing on You when it comes. Help me not only to hear You but to do what You say, one small step at a time. Be my rock today.

A Monday blessing

May the rain find you on solid ground this week. May the unseen work of digging deep, the prayers and the small obediences, hold more than you expect. And when the wind has passed, may you look around and find that your house, built on Jesus, is still standing.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Matthew 7:24–25, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
