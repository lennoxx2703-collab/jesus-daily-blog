Give Us This Day

September 25, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture Matthew 6:11

  • prayer
  • invitation
  • mercy
  • rest

Friday mornings can feel like a hallway already crowded. The week’s unfinished tasks lean against the wall. Weekend plans whisper from the other end. Somewhere in the middle stands today — and we often rush past it on our way to everything else.

Jesus places a short prayer in our hands for mornings like this:

“Give us this day our daily bread.” (Matthew 6:11)

It is not a speech. It is not a strategy. It is a child’s request, spoken to a Father who knows what we need before we ask (Matthew 6:8). And it quietly rearranges the day: we are invited to receive this day’s portion from God, rather than dragging yesterday’s weight or borrowing tomorrow’s fear.

Bread for today, not a warehouse for later

Daily bread is honest about human limits. We are creatures who need again. We cannot stockpile grace and expect it to stay fresh forever. God fed Israel with manna that arrived morning by morning — enough for the day, spoiled if clutched beyond trust (Exodus 16:4, 19–21). The lesson was never scarcity. It was relationship. He wanted a people who would meet Him again at dawn.

So when Jesus teaches us to ask for daily bread, He is not only talking about toast and coffee — though food itself is a mercy worth naming. He is teaching a posture. I will need You today. I do not already possess everything my soul requires. Please give what fits this day’s hunger.

That is relief for perfectionists and planners. You do not have to solve September before breakfast. You are allowed to ask for bread sized to Friday — strength for what is actually in front of you.

What we often carry instead

Many of us wake already holding two other loaves that cannot nourish us.

One is yesterday: the sharp word we wish we had not said, the missed opportunity, the quiet shame that did not dissolve overnight. Another is tomorrow: the meeting not yet held, the bill not yet due, the outcome we cannot control. Both can feel more urgent than the present. Both can crowd out the simple gift of now.

Jesus knows this pattern. A few verses later He says, “Therefore do not be anxious about tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble” (Matthew 6:34). He does not deny that days have trouble. He refuses to let us carry three days at once. Mercy meets us in the portion we actually inhabit.

If you woke with regret, bring it to Him without dressing it up. Confession is not a performance; it is opening empty hands so they can receive. If you woke with dread, name it. Then ask for bread — the nearness, wisdom, and steadiness that belong to this morning — and let tomorrow stay tomorrow until God walks you into it.

Receiving is also trusting

“Give us” assumes a Giver. Prayer is not self-talk with better vocabulary. It is dependence voiced toward Someone who delights to provide. James reminds us that “every good gift and every perfect gift is from above” (James 1:17). The breakfast on the table, the breath in the lungs, the verse that finds you, the courage for a hard call — all breads from the Father’s hand.

Jesus Himself is the deepest answer to the prayer. He said, “I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger” (John 6:35). Daily bread, at its center, is communion with Him. Before the inbox, before the noise, before we try to be enough for everyone, we come. We ask. We receive.

This does not mean every morning feels full. Some Fridays open thin and tired. The prayer still fits. Especially then. Empty people are not disqualified from asking; they are the ones the request was written for. You can pray it with a dry mouth and a distracted mind. God is not grading the poetry of your need. He is listening to His child.

A quiet way to begin

You might start simply. Before the phone claims the first ten minutes, pause long enough to say the line Jesus gave you: Give us this day our daily bread. Add what is honest: Give me patience for this conversation. Give me calm for this body. Give me love where I feel thin. Give me Yourself.

Then watch for small provisions as the day unfolds — not as proof that you prayed correctly, but as evidence that the Father remains kind. A needed word. Enough clarity for the next decision. Enough mercy to begin again after a stumble at noon.

Discipleship is often this ordinary. A morning return to the One who feeds His people. The invitation is warm and close: you do not have to invent the day’s strength. You may ask for it. You may receive it. And when evening comes, you can thank Him for bread that was enough — and rest knowing that tomorrow’s mercies will be new when they arrive.

Lord Jesus, Bread of life, meet us in this Friday. Loosen our grip on yesterday and tomorrow. Teach our hearts to ask, and our hands to receive, the portion You give for today. Amen.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Matthew 6:11, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
