Moved with Compassion

November 15, 2026 · Morning · 3 min read

Scripture Mark 6:34

  • sunday
  • morning
  • compassion
  • shepherd
  • rest
  • belonging

Sunday morning has a way of showing us what the week did to us. Maybe you’re waking up rested. Or maybe you’re waking up with the feeling that you’ve been pulled in ten directions and aren’t sure which way is home anymore.

If that’s you this morning, look at what Jesus did on a day that didn’t go to plan.

“And Jesus, when he came out, saw much people, and was moved with compassion toward them, because they were as sheep not having a shepherd: and he began to teach them many things.” — Mark 6:34 (KJV)

A day off that wasn’t

The backstory matters. Jesus had just told His tired disciples to come away to a quiet place and rest. They got into a boat to find some space. But the crowds saw where they were going and ran ahead on foot, so when the boat landed, the “desert place” was full of people.

Most of us know exactly how that feels. You finally carve out a little room to breathe, and the needs show up before you’ve even sat down. Irritation would be the normal reaction.

But Mark tells us what happened in Jesus’ heart first: He saw them, and He was moved with compassion. The Greek word behind that phrase is a gut word. It describes something felt deep inside, not a polite sympathy. Before He did anything, He felt for them.

What He saw

Notice what Jesus noticed. He didn’t see a crowd that was in His way or a problem to manage. He saw sheep with no shepherd: people wandering without direction, without protection, without someone to lead them to good pasture. Matthew describes the same look on His face in another place: “he was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd” (Matthew 9:36, KJV).

Fainted, and scattered abroad. Tired and pulled apart. That is not a description of bad people. It’s a description of lost ones. And that is how Jesus looks at us when we feel worn out and directionless. He doesn’t sigh at our scatteredness. His heart goes out to it.

What He did

Then Mark says simply, “he began to teach them many things.” His first gift to lost sheep was His voice. Later that same day He fed them, five thousand men with five loaves and two fishes. But first He gave them what they needed most: someone to listen to who knew the way.

That’s still how He shepherds. He said of Himself, “I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep” (John 10:11, KJV). A good shepherd doesn’t wait for the sheep to find their own way back. He comes out to where they are, gathers them, and speaks so they can recognize His voice.

For this Sunday morning

So here is a gentle invitation for today, whether you’re heading to church or sitting quietly at home:

  • Let Him see you as you are. You don’t need to pull yourself together first. He already knows how scattered the week left you, and what He feels toward you is compassion.
  • Listen before you do. Before the to-do list, give Him a few minutes. Read one Gospel passage slowly and let Him “teach you many things,” even if it’s just one thing.
  • Look at others the way He does. The person who interrupts your quiet today may be a sheep without a shepherd too. Ask Him for a little of His compassion before your irritation gets the first word.

A prayer for the morning

Lord Jesus, You saw the crowd and were moved with compassion. See me this morning, tired and scattered as I am. Be my Shepherd. Speak, and help me hear Your voice above all the others. Gather what the week pulled apart, and lead me today. And give me Your eyes for the people I meet, so that I see them the way You see me. Amen.

The Lord is your shepherd, today too. You are not wandering alone.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Mark 6:34, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
