Become as Little Children

November 13, 2026 · Midday · 4 min read

Scripture Matthew 18:1–4

  • friday
  • midday
  • humility
  • childlike-faith
  • rest
  • identity

By Friday at noon, most of us have been measured a few times this week. A performance number. A reply that came back slower than we hoped. A quiet comparison scrolling past on a screen — someone further along, someone doing better, someone who seems to have it figured out. Without ever saying it out loud, we’ve been keeping score.

The disciples kept score too.

Mark tells us they were walking to Capernaum, and somewhere along the road the conversation drifted. When they got to the house, Jesus asked them a simple question:

“What was it that ye disputed among yourselves by the way? But they held their peace: for by the way they had disputed among themselves, who should be the greatest.” (Mark 9:33–34, KJV)

They held their peace. That silence says everything. They knew. You don’t go quiet about something you’re proud of.

A child in the middle of the room

Matthew picks up the moment from there. The disciples finally put the question to Him directly, maybe hoping He’d settle it:

“Who is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven? And Jesus called a little child unto him, and set him in the midst of them, and said, Verily I say unto you, Except ye be converted, and become as little children, ye shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven. Whosoever therefore shall humble himself as this little child, the same is greatest in the kingdom of heaven.” (Matthew 18:1–4, KJV)

Jesus doesn’t give them a ranking. He gives them a child.

Picture it — grown fishermen and a former tax collector standing around, and in the center of them, a small boy or girl who probably just wandered in from the courtyard. Mark adds that Jesus “had taken him in his arms” (Mark 9:36). The answer to “who is greatest?” was being held.

A child in that world had no status at all. No résumé, no influence, nothing to bargain with. A child couldn’t earn a place at the table; a child was simply given one. And that, Jesus says, is the way in. Not climbing higher, but turning around — “except ye be converted” — and becoming small enough to be carried.

What a child knows

Little children are not perfect. They’re loud and they’re needy. But a few things come naturally to them that we spend most of our adult lives unlearning.

They ask for help without embarrassment. They take a gift without trying to pay it back. They fall asleep in the car trusting someone else to get them home. They don’t keep a ledger of their own worth; they just know whose they are.

That’s the humility Jesus is talking about. It isn’t thinking badly of yourself. It’s not having to think about yourself so much at all — because Someone bigger is holding you, and the question of your greatness is no longer your job to answer.

Jesus once thanked His Father for exactly this:

“I thank thee, O Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them unto babes.” (Matthew 11:25, KJV)

The kingdom isn’t hidden from clever people because God is unkind. It’s hidden from anyone who insists on getting in by being impressive. It opens to whoever comes with empty hands.

Setting down the scoreboard

So here, in the middle of a Friday, maybe the invitation is simple: put the scoreboard down for a minute.

Whatever this week said about you — the wins, the misses, the comparisons — none of it is how Jesus measures you. He isn’t standing at the end of the road waiting to see if you ranked high enough. He’s already reached for you and lifted you into His arms, the way He did with that child in Capernaum.

“Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time.” (1 Peter 5:6, KJV)

You don’t have to lift yourself. That’s His part.

Take a breath before the afternoon starts again. Tell Him plainly what you need, like a child would. Receive whatever grace He gives without trying to earn it. And walk back into the rest of the day as someone who is already held.

Lord Jesus, I’ve been keeping score again. Forgive me for wanting to be great more than I want to be Yours. Turn me around. Make me small enough to be carried, honest enough to ask, and glad enough to receive. Hold me through this afternoon. Amen.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in Matthew 18:1–4, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
