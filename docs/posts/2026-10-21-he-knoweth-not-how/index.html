He Knoweth Not How

October 21, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture Mark 4:26–29

  • evening
  • rest
  • trust
  • growth
  • parables
  • wednesday

The day is nearly done, and most of us end it the same way: with a list of things that didn’t get finished. The email still waiting. The prayer that still seems unanswered. The child, the friend, the habit, the part of your own heart that doesn’t look any different tonight than it did this morning.

Jesus told a parable for exactly this hour. It’s one of His shortest, and only Mark records it.

“So is the kingdom of God, as if a man should cast seed into the ground; and should sleep, and rise night and day, and the seed should spring and grow up, he knoweth not how. For the earth bringeth forth fruit of herself; first the blade, then the ear, after that the full corn in the ear.” (Mark 4:26–28, KJV)

The farmer goes to bed

Notice what the man in the story actually does. He scatters seed. Then he sleeps. Then he gets up. Then he sleeps again. Night and day, day and night.

He doesn’t sit up in the field with a lamp, watching the soil. He doesn’t dig the seed up every morning to check on it. He does his small part, and then he lives his ordinary life, and the growing happens without him.

That’s not laziness. It’s trust. The farmer knows something we forget on a Wednesday evening: the most important work in the field isn’t the work he’s doing.

“He knoweth not how”

I love how honest Jesus is here. The seed springs and grows up, he knoweth not how. The farmer can’t explain it and can’t control it. He just sees it happen.

A lot of what God does in us and in the people we love works the same way. You’ve prayed, you’ve spoken a kind word, you’ve opened Scripture with a tired heart, you’ve forgiven someone for the third time. And nothing seems to move. But under the surface, where you can’t see, the Lord is at work. Paul put it simply: “I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase” (1 Corinthians 3:6).

You don’t have to understand how. You don’t have to make it happen tonight.

First the blade

Jesus also tells us the growth comes in stages: “first the blade, then the ear, after that the full corn in the ear.”

A blade of green is not a harvest. But it’s not nothing, either. It’s the first proof that something living is underneath.

Maybe that’s where you are. Maybe your faith tonight is just a thin green blade: a little more patience than last year, a little less fear, one prayer that came more easily. Don’t despise it because it isn’t full grain yet. Jesus doesn’t. He’s the One who “shall not break” a bruised reed (Matthew 12:20), and He’s patient with things that are still growing.

The harvest will come

The parable ends with confidence: “But when the fruit is brought forth, immediately he putteth in the sickle, because the harvest is come” (Mark 4:29).

The seed doesn’t stay hidden forever. What God plants, He finishes. “He which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ” (Philippians 1:6). The harvest isn’t up to your effort tonight. It’s up to His faithfulness, and He has never once failed at that.

For tonight

So here’s your permission: go to bed. Leave the field for the night. The seed you sowed today, the small obedience, the quiet prayer, the word you weren’t sure anyone heard, is in good soil and in good hands.

Here’s a prayer to close your Wednesday:

Lord Jesus, I’ve scattered what little I had today, and I can’t see much growing. Thank You that You work while I sleep, in ways I don’t understand. I give You the unfinished things, the people I worry about, and my own slow heart. Grow what You’ve planted, in Your time. I’m going to rest now, trusting You. Amen.

Sleep well. The growing doesn’t stop when you close your eyes.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Mark 4:26–29, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
