Supposing Him to Have Been in the Company

November 2, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture Luke 2:43–52

  • monday
  • new-week
  • seeking-jesus
  • ordinary-life
  • boy-jesus
  • morning

Monday mornings run on autopilot. The alarm, the coffee, the bag by the door, the inbox, the route you could drive with your eyes closed. A new week and a new month have started, and most of us are already a few steps down the road before we’ve really thought about anything.

Luke tells about a family who did exactly that.

And when they had fulfilled the days, as they returned, the child Jesus tarried behind in Jerusalem; and Joseph and his mother knew not of it. But they, supposing him to have been in the company, went a day’s journey; and they sought him among their kinsfolk and acquaintance. And when they found him not, they turned back again to Jerusalem, seeking him. (Luke 2:43–45, KJV)

A whole day of supposing

There’s no scandal here. Mary and Joseph weren’t careless people. They had just kept the feast. They were travelling in a big, friendly crowd of relatives and neighbours, the kind of group where a twelve-year-old could easily be walking with cousins up ahead. It was a reasonable assumption.

But it was an assumption. “Supposing him to have been in the company.” They went a whole day’s journey on that.

That’s an honest picture of how a week can go. We had a good Sunday. We prayed, maybe we sang, maybe we read something that moved us. And then Monday starts, and we just assume Jesus is somewhere in the crowd of our plans. We don’t turn and look. We don’t ask. We keep walking. Sometimes it’s Wednesday or Friday before we notice how quiet it has been.

If that’s you, the story isn’t scolding you. It’s showing you what to do next.

They turned back

And it came to pass, that after three days they found him in the temple, sitting in the midst of the doctors, both hearing them, and asking them questions. (Luke 2:46, KJV)

When they noticed, they turned back and kept seeking until they found Him. And He wasn’t lost the way they feared. He was exactly where He said He would be:

How is it that ye sought me? wist ye not that I must be about my Father’s business? (Luke 2:49, KJV)

There’s something gentle in that answer. Jesus wasn’t hiding. He wasn’t wandering. He was in His Father’s house, doing His Father’s work. The searching felt long and anxious to Mary, “thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing,” but He was never actually far away.

So this morning, before the day picks up speed, it’s worth turning back for a minute. Not in a panic, but on purpose. You don’t have to guess whether He’s in the company. You can stop and ask Him: Lord, are You with me in this week? Walk with me today. He has promised that those who seek Him will find Him (Matthew 7:7).

And He went down with them

Here’s my favourite part, and it’s easy to miss:

And he went down with them, and came to Nazareth, and was subject unto them… And Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man. (Luke 2:51–52, KJV)

After the temple, after the big moment, Jesus went home. Back to Nazareth. Back to an ordinary house, ordinary chores, a carpenter’s workshop, years that the Gospels hardly describe at all. The Son of God spent most of His life in the kind of days you’re about to have: work, family, meals, small faithfulness that no one writes down.

That means your Monday isn’t too ordinary for Him. The meeting, the school run, the shift, the dishes, the commute. He knows that road. He walked it for years before anyone called Him Teacher. Being about the Father’s business and going down to Nazareth turned out to be the same life.

So don’t just suppose He’s somewhere in the crowd this week. Turn and find Him this morning, and then let Him walk down into the ordinary hours with you. He’s glad to come.

A prayer for this morning: Lord Jesus, I don’t want to spend this week just assuming You’re near. I turn to You now. Go with me into the ordinary hours of today, and let me grow, a little at a time, in favour with God and with the people around me. Amen.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Luke 2:43–52, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
