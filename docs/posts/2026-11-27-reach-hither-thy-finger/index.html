Reach Hither Thy Finger

November 27, 2026 · Evening · 4 min read

Scripture John 20:24–29

  • friday
  • evening
  • doubt
  • resurrection
  • honesty
  • grace

Friday evening is when the week finally stops talking. The emails go quiet, the to-do list gives up for the night, and whatever you’ve been too busy to feel all week walks into the room and sits down. Sometimes that’s gratitude. Sometimes it’s tiredness. And sometimes, if you’re honest, it’s a question you don’t quite want to say out loud: Is any of this real? Is He really there?

If that’s where you are tonight, you’re in good company. One of the twelve sat exactly there.

“But Thomas, one of the twelve, called Didymus, was not with them when Jesus came. The other disciples therefore said unto him, We have seen the Lord. But he said unto them, Except I shall see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my finger into the print of the nails, and thrust my hand into his side, I will not believe.” — John 20:24–25 (KJV)

The one who missed it

We don’t know where Thomas was that first Easter evening. Maybe he couldn’t bear to be in a room full of grief. Maybe he was walking the streets trying to make sense of the cross. Wherever he was, he missed it. Everyone else saw Jesus, and he didn’t.

There’s a particular loneliness in that. Everyone around you seems sure. They sing with their eyes closed, they talk about what God is doing, and you’re standing there thinking, I wasn’t in the room for that.

We call him “Doubting Thomas,” but that isn’t fair to him. This is the same man who once said, when Jesus headed back toward danger, “Let us also go, that we may die with him” (John 11:16). He’s the one who asked honestly at the Last Supper, “Lord, we know not whither thou goest; and how can we know the way?” (John 14:5). Thomas wasn’t cynical. He was brave and he was honest, and his heart had been broken on a Friday. He didn’t want to hope again and be wrong.

Eight days

“And after eight days again his disciples were within, and Thomas with them: then came Jesus, the doors being shut, and stood in the midst, and said, Peace be unto you.” — John 20:26 (KJV)

Eight days. That’s a whole week of Thomas carrying his doubt around. A week of the others’ joy pressing on his question. Notice two quiet mercies here.

First, Thomas was with them. He hadn’t walked away. Doubting and still showing up is not failure. It’s a kind of faith that keeps its feet in the room even when its heart isn’t sure.

Second, Jesus came back. He didn’t send a message through Peter. He didn’t say, “They told you; that should be enough.” He came again, through shut doors, and His first word was the same as before: Peace.

Here are My hands

“Then saith he to Thomas, Reach hither thy finger, and behold my hands; and reach hither thy hand, and thrust it into my side: and be not faithless, but believing.” — John 20:27 (KJV)

Do you see what Jesus does? He repeats Thomas’s own words back to him, almost exactly. Finger. Print of the nails. Hand. Side. He had heard every word of that bitter, grieving sentence a week ago, even though He wasn’t visibly there. And He didn’t come to scold it. He came to answer it.

Jesus doesn’t shrink from your hardest question. He holds out His wounded hands, the very proof of how far His love went, and says, Come and see. Come closer.

“And Thomas answered and said unto him, My Lord and my God.” — John 20:28 (KJV)

The man who doubted the most made one of the clearest confessions in all the Gospels. Doubt honestly brought to Jesus doesn’t have to end in distance. It can end in worship.

For those of us who weren’t in the room

“Jesus saith unto him, Thomas, because thou hast seen me, thou hast believed: blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed.” — John 20:29 (KJV)

That last sentence is for you and me. We weren’t in the room either. Jesus knew we wouldn’t be, and He spoke a blessing over us before we were born. Your faith tonight may feel small and shaky. It is still blessed, because of who it’s resting on.

Tonight

Before you sleep, try being as honest as Thomas. Tell Jesus the question you’ve been carrying this week, the one you haven’t said out loud. You don’t have to dress it up. He has already heard it.

Then picture those hands held out toward you. Not clenched, not pointing. Open and scarred, and still reaching.

Lord Jesus, I don’t always see You, and some nights I’m not sure. Thank You for coming back for Thomas, and for coming back for me. Here are my questions. Here is my small faith. You are my Lord and my God. Amen.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in John 20:24–29, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
