Let Your Light So Shine

November 11, 2026 · Morning · 3 min read

Scripture Matthew 5:14–16

  • wednesday
  • morning
  • light
  • witness
  • good-works
  • sermon-on-the-mount

November mornings come in slowly. The dark hangs on past breakfast, and a lot of us leave the house before the sky has made up its mind. Somewhere on your street a kitchen window is already lit, and you notice it without trying. That’s the thing about light: it doesn’t argue for itself. It just shines, and people see.

Jesus was sitting on a hillside in Galilee, talking to fishermen, farmers, tired mothers, and people nobody had ever called important. And He said this over them:

“Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid. Neither do men light a candle, and put it under a bushel, but on a candlestick; and it giveth light unto all that are in the house. Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.” (Matthew 5:14–16, KJV)

Notice what He didn’t say. He didn’t say, Try to become the light someday, once you’ve got your life together. He said ye are. Present tense. Spoken to ordinary people on an ordinary day.

The light isn’t yours to make

That could sound like pressure, as if Jesus were handing you a job you’re not up to. But He said something else later that changes how this lands: “I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life” (John 8:12, KJV).

He is the light. We are lit. A candle doesn’t produce fire out of its own wax; it receives a flame and carries it. Paul put it this way: “God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts” (2 Corinthians 4:6, KJV). Whatever light you carry this morning, you got it from Him. Your job isn’t to manufacture it. Your job is not to hide it.

The bushel we reach for

Jesus’ picture is almost funny. Nobody lights a lamp and then puts a basket over it. And yet we do it all the time.

We cover it with busyness, rushing past the coworker who looks like they didn’t sleep. We cover it with embarrassment, keeping faith in a private drawer because it might be awkward. We cover it with the quiet belief that our light is too small to matter, so why bother.

But look where the lamp goes in His story: on the stand, where “it giveth light unto all that are in the house.” Not the whole world at once. The house. The people right around you. Your kitchen, your office, the bus, the group chat.

Good works, His glory

The shining Jesus describes is mostly practical. “That they may see your good works.” A meal dropped off. Patience with a slow cashier. Telling the truth when a small lie would be easier. Forgiving before the other person asks. Showing up again.

And the point of it is not that people admire you. It’s that they “glorify your Father which is in heaven.” A good lamp doesn’t make you stare at the lamp. It lets you see the room. When your life is lit by Jesus, people should end up seeing Him more clearly, not you.

For today

Before you head out, try a simple prayer:

Lord Jesus, You are the light. Shine in me today. Show me the basket I’ve been hiding under, and help me set it aside. Let someone in my house, my street, or my work see a little of Your kindness through me, and let them thank the Father for it, not me.

Then go. The sky may still be dark when you leave. That’s fine. That’s exactly when a lit window matters most.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Matthew 5:14–16, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
