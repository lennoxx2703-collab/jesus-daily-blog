With Desire I Have Desired

October 28, 2026 · Evening · 3 min read

Scripture Luke 22:14–15, 19

  • evening
  • table
  • last-supper
  • belonging
  • reflection
  • wednesday

It’s evening in Jerusalem. The streets are crowded with Passover pilgrims, lamps are being lit in upper rooms all over the city, and in one of them a borrowed table has been set for thirteen. Jesus knows exactly what tomorrow holds. And this is the first thing He says when they sit down:

“And when the hour was come, he sat down, and the twelve apostles with him. And he said unto them, With desire I have desired to eat this passover with you before I suffer.” (Luke 22:14–15, KJV)

With desire I have desired. It’s an old way of saying it, doubled for emphasis: I have really, deeply wanted this. Not “I suppose we should eat.” Not “Let’s get this over with.” He has been looking forward to this meal. With them.

Look at who was at the table

That’s the part that catches me. Around that table sat a man who would deny Him before sunrise, a group who would argue that very night about which of them was the greatest, friends who would fall asleep when He asked them to stay awake, and one who had already taken the silver. Jesus knew all of it. He’d just told Peter, more or less, what was coming.

And still: I have wanted to eat with you.

We tend to assume that if Jesus really saw us clearly, He would tolerate us at best. Let us in the back door. Put up with us at the far end of the table. But the evening before the cross shows something different. He sees them completely and He wants them close. Being known and being wanted turn out to be the same room.

Bread, broken and given

Then He does something very ordinary with something very ordinary:

“And he took bread, and gave thanks, and brake it, and gave unto them, saying, This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me.” (Luke 22:19, KJV)

Notice the little words: given for you. Not given for the impressive ones. Not given for whoever finally gets their act together. For the ones at that table, with all their muddle, and for everyone who would later sit down at His table the same way.

He didn’t wait until they understood. They didn’t, not yet. He gave first.

Your table tonight

Maybe tonight’s table is a kitchen counter and a plate you ate standing up. Maybe it’s a family dinner that got tense, or a quiet meal alone with the TV on. Wherever you ate, it’s worth knowing that Jesus still talks about meals this way. Long after that night, He said:

“Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me.” (Revelation 3:20, KJV)

Sup with him. Eat supper together. That’s the picture He chooses for being close to us: not an inspection, a meal.

So before the day closes, you might try this:

  • Remember one moment today you’d rather He hadn’t seen. Then remember that He did, and still wants to sit with you.
  • Give thanks for one ordinary thing, the way He gave thanks for plain bread. A meal, a roof, a person.
  • Open the door with a simple word. Something like, “Lord, come in. Stay with me tonight.”

You don’t have to clean the whole house first. He’s said it plainly, on the hardest night of His life: I have wanted to be with you. That was true at that table, and it’s still true at yours.

Good night, friend. You’re wanted at His table.

A prayer

Stay with us, Lord, for it is toward evening. Meet me in Luke 22:14–15, 19, and let Your word rest in my heart. Remain with us through the night, and keep our hearts burning.
