Hid in Three Measures of Meal

November 12, 2026 · Midday · 4 min read

Scripture Matthew 13:31–33

  • thursday
  • midday
  • kingdom
  • parables
  • patience
  • growth

Midday has a way of making everything feel small. The big hopes you carried into the morning have been chopped into emails, errands, dishes, and conversations that didn’t go anywhere. You look at the half-finished day and wonder whether anything that matters is actually happening.

Jesus has an answer for that, and He tells it with things you could find in any kitchen or garden.

“Another parable put he forth unto them, saying, The kingdom of heaven is like to a grain of mustard seed, which a man took, and sowed in his field: Which indeed is the least of all seeds: but when it is grown, it is the greatest among herbs, and becometh a tree, so that the birds of the air come and lodge in the branches thereof. Another parable spake he unto them; The kingdom of heaven is like unto leaven, which a woman took, and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened.” (Matthew 13:31–33, KJV)

He chose small pictures on purpose

If you wanted to describe the reign of God, you might reach for thunder, armies, or a throne. Jesus reached for a seed you could lose between your fingers and a lump of leaven a woman pressed into flour before breakfast.

That is not an accident. Jesus is telling us how God usually works: not by announcing Himself loudly, but by planting something tiny and letting it grow. The kingdom came into the world as a baby in a borrowed manger, grew up in a carpenter’s shop in a town people joked about, and gathered twelve ordinary men. From the outside it looked like almost nothing. It was the beginning of everything.

Hidden is not the same as idle

Notice the word Jesus uses for the leaven: the woman hid it. Once it’s mixed into three measures of meal — a huge amount, enough bread for a crowd — you can’t see it anymore. Nothing seems to be happening. And yet the whole lump is quietly being changed from the inside.

That is often what God’s work in us feels like. You prayed this morning and nothing visibly shifted. You chose patience with someone at lunch and nobody noticed. You opened the Gospel for five minutes and forgot most of it by noon. It can feel like flour that’s just sitting there.

But Jesus says the leaven keeps working “till the whole was leavened.” Elsewhere He describes the same mystery: a man casts seed into the ground, “and should sleep, and rise night and day, and the seed should spring and grow up, he knoweth not how” (Mark 4:27, KJV). You don’t have to understand the process or watch it happen. You only have to let Him keep working.

Don’t despise the small things

There’s an old line from the prophet Zechariah: “For who hath despised the day of small things?” (Zechariah 4:10, KJV). Most of our days are days of small things. A kind word. A quiet confession. A choice not to say the sharp sentence. A prayer whispered between meetings.

We tend to think those don’t count because they’re small. Jesus says that is exactly the size the kingdom likes to start at. The mustard seed doesn’t stay a seed. It becomes a place where birds come to rest. Your small faithfulness today may one day be shade for someone you haven’t met yet.

A midday pause

So here, in the middle of the day, take a breath and offer Jesus the small things:

  • The seed you’re carrying. Maybe it’s a hope, a prayer for someone, a change you want in yourself. Tell Him it feels tiny. He already knows, and He isn’t worried about the size.
  • The hidden work. Thank Him for whatever He’s doing in you that you can’t see yet. Faith often sounds like, “Lord, I trust You’re working even here.”
  • The rest of the afternoon. Ask Him to help you do the next small thing well, without needing it to look impressive.

Paul wrote with quiet confidence that “he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ” (Philippians 1:6, KJV). The One who hid the leaven doesn’t forget the bread.

A short prayer

Lord Jesus, my day feels small and scattered. Thank You that Your kingdom starts small too. Hide Your life in the ordinary parts of me — my words, my work, my waiting — and keep working until all of me is changed. Help me trust what I can’t see yet, and be faithful in the little things this afternoon. Amen.

Go back into your afternoon gently. The seed is in the ground. The leaven is in the meal. And He is not finished.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in Matthew 13:31–33, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
