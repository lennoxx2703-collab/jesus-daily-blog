Someone Did Touch Me

October 10, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture Luke 8:48

  • healing
  • faith
  • shame
  • gospel

There’s a kind of tired that doesn’t go away with sleep. It’s the tiredness of carrying something for years: a sickness, a sorrow, a shame you’ve learned to hide so well that nobody asks about it anymore.

Luke tells us about a woman who knew that kind of tired.

“A woman who had a flow of blood for twelve years, who had spent all her living on physicians and could not be healed by any, came behind him, and touched the fringe of his cloak. Immediately the flow of her blood stopped.” (Luke 8:43–44)

Twelve years

Twelve years is a long time to hope and be disappointed. She had tried everything. She had spent everything. And under the law of her day, her condition made her unclean, so she had likely spent those years on the edges of things: kept at a distance from worship, from friends, from touch itself.

So she didn’t come to Jesus the way Jairus did, falling at His feet in front of everyone. She came from behind. She reached for the very edge of His cloak. Her plan was simple: get healed, slip away, never be seen.

Maybe you know that plan. Lord, just fix this quietly. Don’t make me talk about it. Don’t make me be seen.

He stopped

And it worked. She was healed in an instant. But Jesus did something she hadn’t planned for.

“Jesus said, ‘Who touched me?’ When all denied it, Peter and those with him said, ‘Master, the multitudes press and jostle you, and you say, “Who touched me?”’ But Jesus said, ‘Someone did touch me, for I perceived that power has gone out of me.’” (Luke 8:45–46)

Peter’s point was reasonable. Everyone was touching Him. The crowd was pressing on every side. But Jesus knew the difference between being bumped by a crowd and being reached for in faith. One desperate hand on the hem of His cloak, and He stopped walking.

He was on His way to a dying girl. It was urgent. And still He stopped, because this woman was not an interruption to Him. She was the reason He was there too.

Seen, not exposed

“When the woman saw that she was not hidden, she came trembling; and falling down before him declared to him in the presence of all the people the reason why she had touched him, and how she was healed immediately.” (Luke 8:47)

She came trembling. She probably expected a rebuke. An unclean woman had touched a teacher in public. But listen to what He says:

“Daughter, cheer up. Your faith has made you well. Go in peace.” (Luke 8:48)

Daughter. It’s the only time in the Gospels Jesus calls someone by that word. A woman nobody had wanted near them for twelve years is now named, in front of the whole town, as family.

Jesus didn’t call her out to shame her. He called her out so she wouldn’t leave with only half a healing. Her body was made well when she touched Him. But her heart was made well when He looked at her. She came hoping to go unnoticed. She left known, blessed, and at peace.

For your Saturday morning

You may be coming to Jesus from behind this morning. Quietly. Hoping He’ll help without asking too many questions. That’s all right. He is not offended by a trembling reach. A small, desperate faith is still faith, and He feels it.

But don’t be surprised if He stops and turns toward you. He’s not looking to expose you. He wants to give you more than you came for. You came for relief, and He wants to give you a name. You came to be fixed, and He wants you to know you belong.

Whatever you’ve carried for twelve years, or twelve months, or twelve days, you can reach for Him today. And when He turns to look at you, you’ll find no anger in His face. Only welcome.

A prayer for this morning: Lord Jesus, I reach for You, even if it’s only the edge of Your cloak. You know what I’ve been carrying and how long. Heal what needs healing, and let me hear You call me Yours. Help me go into this day in Your peace. Amen.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Luke 8:48, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
