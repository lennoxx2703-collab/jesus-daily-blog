Consider the Lilies

October 8, 2026 · Midday · 4 min read

Scripture Matthew 6:28–30

  • trust
  • comfort
  • rest
  • prayer

It’s the middle of the day, and if you’re like most of us, part of your mind has already left the room. It’s sitting with the bill that’s due, the conversation you’re dreading, the email you haven’t answered, the thing your body did this morning that you’re trying not to think about. Your hands are here. Your worry is somewhere around next Tuesday.

Jesus once spoke to a crowd of people exactly like that. Most of them lived close to the edge. A bad harvest or a sick week could mean real hunger. And into that crowd He said something that sounds almost too simple:

“Therefore I tell you, don’t be anxious for your life: what you will eat, or what you will drink; nor yet for your body, what you will wear. Isn’t life more than food, and the body more than clothing?” (Matthew 6:25)

He didn’t say it from a palace. He said it on a hillside, to people who had every practical reason to worry. Which tells me He wasn’t being naïve. He was showing them something they couldn’t see from inside their fear.

Look up, then look down

First He points up.

“See the birds of the sky, that they don’t sow, neither do they reap, nor gather into barns. Your heavenly Father feeds them. Aren’t you of much more value than they?” (Matthew 6:26)

Then He points down, at whatever was growing wild around their feet.

“Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow. They don’t toil, neither do they spin, yet I tell you that even Solomon in all his glory was not dressed like one of these. But if God so clothes the grass of the field, which today exists, and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, won’t he much more clothe you, you of little faith?” (Matthew 6:28–30)

Consider is a slow word. It means stop and actually look. Jesus isn’t asking us to admire a pretty picture. He’s asking us to notice evidence. Every flower in that field was being cared for by Someone, and not one of them had earned it. If the Father pays that much attention to something that will be gone by next week, how much more does He pay attention to you?

Notice what He calls God here. Not “the Creator” or “the Almighty,” though He is both. Your heavenly Father. The whole argument rests on that one relationship. You’re not a lily. You’re a child.

Worry can’t add a minute

In the middle of all this Jesus asks a question that’s almost funny if you let it land:

“Which of you, by being anxious, can add one moment to his lifespan?” (Matthew 6:27)

Worry feels like work. It feels responsible, like if we stop turning the problem over, something bad will slip through. But Jesus gently names what we already know: worry doesn’t actually do anything. It doesn’t pay the bill or fix the diagnosis or soften the hard conversation. It just takes tomorrow’s weight and makes you carry it today, on top of today’s.

That isn’t a scolding. “You of little faith” in Jesus’ mouth sounds less like a teacher marking a paper and more like a father kneeling down to eye level: You really think I’d forget you?

Just today

He ends the thought like this:

“Therefore don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient.” (Matthew 6:34)

He doesn’t pretend today will be easy. He admits each day has its own trouble. He just won’t let you borrow trouble from days that haven’t come yet. Today has enough, and today is where your Father’s care actually meets you.

So here’s a small practice for the rest of your afternoon. Next time you feel your mind sprint ahead, find one living thing nearby. A tree outside the window, a plant on someone’s desk, a bird on a wire. Consider it. Let it remind you that you’re being held by the same hands, and far more tenderly.

A prayer for the middle of your day:

Father, my mind keeps running ahead of me into tomorrow. Thank You that You feed the birds and dress the flowers, and that You know exactly what I need before I ask. I give You the things I’ve been carrying that belong to days that haven’t come yet. Help me do today’s work with today’s strength, and trust You with the rest. Teach me to look at the lilies and remember whose child I am. In Jesus’ name.

You don’t have to hold the whole week together. You only have to walk through this afternoon with Him.

A prayer

Jesus, I bring You the weight of this hour. Meet me in Matthew 6:28–30, and let Your word rest in my heart. Give rest to my soul and strength for what remains.
