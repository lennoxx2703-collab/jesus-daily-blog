Thou Shalt Call His Name Jesus

November 28, 2026 · Morning · 4 min read

Scripture Matthew 1:20–23

  • saturday
  • morning
  • advent
  • the-name-of-jesus
  • emmanuel
  • salvation

Saturday mornings move a little slower. No alarm, maybe. Coffee that actually gets finished while it’s still warm. And this particular Saturday sits right on the doorstep of Advent — tomorrow the season of waiting begins, when Christians all over the world start leaning toward Christmas.

So before the lights and the lists and the noise, let’s start where Matthew starts: with a worried carpenter, a sleepless night, and a name.

“But while he thought on these things, behold, the angel of the Lord appeared unto him in a dream, saying, Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost. And she shall bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins. Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us.” — Matthew 1:20–23 (KJV)

While he thought on these things

Joseph’s world had just cracked. The woman he was promised to was expecting a child that wasn’t his. He was a just man, Matthew says, and he had already worked out a quiet, decent plan to step away without shaming her. He was lying awake with it, turning it over — thinking on these things.

That’s where God met him. Not after Joseph had sorted it out, not once he’d calmed down, but right in the middle of the turning-over.

Maybe that’s where you are this morning. Something you didn’t plan for has landed in your life, and you’ve been quietly rehearsing your options. Hear the first words the angel spoke: fear not. God isn’t waiting for you to finish thinking before He speaks. He steps into the worry itself.

A name that is a promise

Then comes the name. In Joseph’s language it was Yeshua — “the Lord saves.” It was a common enough name in those days; plenty of boys in Galilee answered to it. But the angel attaches a reason that no other boy could carry: for he shall save his people from their sins.

Notice what the name doesn’t say. It doesn’t say He’ll make His people impressive, or comfortable, or always right. It says He will save them — and from the thing that’s hardest to fix on our own: our sins. The guilt we keep reopening. The patterns we swore we’d break. The words we can’t take back.

Before Jesus had ever drawn a breath, His name was already a rescue mission. Every time you say it — even in a tired, half-formed prayer — you’re saying the promise out loud: the Lord saves.

God with us

And then Matthew reaches back to Isaiah for a second name: Emmanuel, God with us. Not God watching us. Not God sending advice from far away. God with us — in a womb, in a manger, in a carpenter’s workshop, on dusty roads, at tables with sinners, and finally on a cross.

Matthew opens his Gospel with that promise and closes it the same way. The very last words of Jesus in Matthew are: “and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world” (Matthew 28:20). The whole book is wrapped in with.

That means this slow Saturday, the busy week ahead, and the whole waiting season of Advent are not empty spaces. He is in them.

Joseph got up

The next verse is beautifully plain: “Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him” (Matthew 1:24). No speech. No negotiation. He woke up and trusted the name.

You can do the same this morning. Before the day fills up, say His name slowly. Let it mean what the angel said it means: He saves, and He is with you.

A prayer for this morning

Jesus, as this season of waiting begins, I want to start with You. Thank You that Your name is a promise — that You came to save me from my sins, not just to cheer me up. Meet me in the things I’ve been turning over in the night. Help me hear “fear not,” and then get up and trust You like Joseph did. Emmanuel, be with me today. Amen.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day begins, draw me near. Meet me in Matthew 1:20–23, and let Your word rest in my heart. Teach me to walk with You in trust and joy.
